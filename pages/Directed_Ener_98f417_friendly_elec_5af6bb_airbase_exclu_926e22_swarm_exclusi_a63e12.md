## Introduction

High-power microwave (HPM) weapons are attractive for airbase defence because they can engage many drones at once rather than defeating them individually. That capability is especially valuable against drone [swarms]({{ 'swarms/' | relative_url }}), which are designed to overwhelm traditional air defences through numbers, speed and coordinated movement. However, the same characteristic that makes microwave systems effective against swarms also makes safety management more difficult. Instead of focusing on a single target, an HPM engagement may expose a broader volume of airspace and nearby electronics to intense electromagnetic energy. As the number of hostile drones increases, the challenge of ensuring that no friendly aircraft, vehicles, sensors or communications systems are inside the affected area becomes significantly more complex.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afrl.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/3396995/afrl-conducts-swarm-technology-demonstration/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afrl.af.mil">[AFRL+2Air Force Research Laboratory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afrl.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">RLAFRL conducts swarm technology demonstration</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RL conducts swarm technology demonstrationMay 16, 2023 — 16 May 2023 — “THOR was exceptionally effective at disabling the swarm wit...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 16, 2023</span></span></span>

## How Wide-Area Effects Differ from Point Engagements

A laser or kinetic interceptor generally engages one target at a time. The operator can often identify a specific drone, track it and fire along a relatively narrow engagement path. Microwave counter-swarm systems are different. They are designed to create effects across an area or volume of space so that multiple drones can be disrupted simultaneously. Systems such as [THOR]({{ 'thor/' | relative_url }}) and Leonidas have been publicly demonstrated against groups of drones specifically because their value lies in defeating many targets within a single engagement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afrl.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/3396995/afrl-conducts-swarm-technology-demonstration/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afrl.af.mil">[AFRL+2Air Force Research Laboratory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afrl.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">RLAFRL conducts swarm technology demonstration</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RL conducts swarm technology demonstrationMay 16, 2023 — 16 May 2023 — “THOR was exceptionally effective at disabling the swarm wit...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 16, 2023</span></span></span>

This creates a different safety problem. When commanders approve a microwave shot, they are not only considering the location of the hostile drones. They must also consider every friendly electronic system that could potentially fall within the engagement geometry. The question becomes less “Can we hit that drone?” and more “What else is inside the effect area at the same time?”

The challenge grows as swarms become larger. A single drone may present a compact target area. A swarm may occupy a much wider section of airspace, approach from multiple directions or spread across different altitudes. Because the engagement area must encompass the swarm, the zone that commanders must evaluate for friendly-system safety often expands as well.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.droneshield.com/blog/a-counter-to-drone-swarms-high-power-microwave-weapons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: droneshield.com">[DroneShield]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">droneshield.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Counter to Drone Swarms: High-Power Microwave...Through programming or increasing autonomy, swarms can coordinate attacks f...</span></span></span>

## What Swarm Defence Changes on an Airbase

Airbases are dynamic environments. Aircraft taxi, [maintenance]({{ 'maintenance/' | relative_url }}) teams move equipment, ground vehicles cross runways and communications systems operate continuously. A microwave exclusion zone therefore cannot be treated as a static circle drawn on a map.

Drone swarms complicate matters because they introduce uncertainty in several ways:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* **Larger target formations:** Multiple drones may occupy a broader airspace volume than a single intruder.
* **Distributed attack axes:** Swarms can approach from several directions simultaneously, forcing defenders to consider multiple engagement sectors.
* **Constant movement:** Individual drones within a swarm may separate, regroup or manoeuvre unpredictably.
* **Time pressure:** Swarms are intended to compress a defender's decision cycle, reducing the time available for safety checks.

</div>

The practical consequence is that exclusion-zone management becomes a moving problem rather than a fixed one. An area considered safe for an HPM shot a few seconds earlier may no longer be acceptable if a friendly helicopter enters the sector, a maintenance vehicle moves into position or aircraft begin taxi operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.droneshield.com/blog/a-counter-to-drone-swarms-high-power-microwave-weapons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: droneshield.com">[DroneShield]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">droneshield.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Counter to Drone Swarms: High-Power Microwave...Through programming or increasing autonomy, swarms can coordinate attacks f...</span></span></span>

## Why More Targets Mean More Friendly Assets to Track

Counter-[swarm microwave]({{ 'swarm-microwave/' | relative_url }}) weapons are often described as “one-to-many” systems because a single pulse can affect multiple drones. Recent demonstrations have highlighted this capability, including tests in which dozens of drones were disrupted in a single engagement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.epirusinc.com/press-releases/epirus-leonidas-high-power-microwave-defeats-49-drone-swarm-100-of-drones-flown-at-live-fire-demonstration" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: epirusinc.com">[Epirus+2Tom&#x27;s Hardware]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">epirusinc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">epirus leonidas high power microwave defeats 49 drone swarm 100 of drones flown at live fire demonstration</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Leonidas&#x27; suite of capability ...Read more</span></span></span>

From a safety perspective, however, one-to-many effects also create one-to-many accountability. Before firing, operators must understand the status of many categories of friendly systems simultaneously:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Aircraft in the air and on the ground.
* Airfield communications and navigation infrastructure.
* Command-and-control networks.
* Ground support equipment.
* Vehicles carrying digital electronics.
* Personnel using radios or specialised electronic devices.

</div>

As the defended area becomes more crowded, the burden of maintaining an accurate real-time picture increases. The swarm threat therefore magnifies a pre-existing airbase challenge: maintaining awareness of every friendly electronic system that could be exposed during an engagement.

This is one reason modern HPM developers increasingly emphasise control, beam management and engagement planning rather than presenting microwave effects as indiscriminate. The military value of the weapon depends not only on defeating the swarm but also on avoiding disruption to friendly operations taking place nearby.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.epirusinc.com/electronic-warfare" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: epirusinc.com">[Epirus]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">epirusinc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EpirusEpirus Leonidas High-Power Microwave: Directed Energy...Leonidas is a solid-state, high-power HPM system, utilizing Gallium Nitrid...</span></span></span>

## Balancing Rapid Shots Against Friendly-System Risk

The greatest operational advantage of a microwave counter-swarm weapon is speed. Public descriptions of systems such as THOR emphasise rapid engagement, low cost per shot and the ability to affect multiple drones nearly instantaneously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.popsci.com/technology/thor-weapon-drone-swarm-test/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: popsci.com">[Popular Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">popsci.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">thor weapon drone swarm test</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Popular ScienceAir Force uses THOR&#x27;s energy to zap drones23 May 2023 — “The system uses high power microwaves to cause a counter electron...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span>

Yet rapid engagement can conflict with exclusion-zone management. The faster a commander wants to fire, the less time exists to verify that friendly assets remain outside the relevant safety boundaries. This creates a tension at the heart of airbase microwave defence:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* Delay the shot too long and the swarm may reach valuable targets.
* Fire too quickly and the risk of exposing friendly systems increases.

</div>

The problem is especially acute during large-scale attacks. Swarms are often intended to saturate defensive decision-making. Every additional drone can increase the number of tracks, alerts and movement patterns that operators must assess. Even if the microwave weapon itself can engage many targets at once, commanders still need confidence that the surrounding electromagnetic environment is sufficiently clear to permit safe use.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.droneshield.com/blog/a-counter-to-drone-swarms-high-power-microwave-weapons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: droneshield.com">[DroneShield]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">droneshield.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Counter to Drone Swarms: High-Power Microwave...Through programming or increasing autonomy, swarms can coordinate attacks f...</span></span></span>

## The Core Mechanism Behind the Safety Challenge

The reason drone swarms make microwave exclusion zones harder is ultimately straightforward: the weapon's area-effect advantage scales the safety problem along with the defensive benefit.

A point target allows commanders to focus on a narrow engagement path. A swarm encourages use of a broader electromagnetic effect capable of reaching multiple drones simultaneously. As the defended volume expands, the number of friendly systems that must be tracked, protected and excluded from potential exposure also increases.
