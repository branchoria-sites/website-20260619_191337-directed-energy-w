## Introduction

The Marine Corps did not need a smaller Leonidas simply because it preferred lighter equipment. It needed a different balance of capabilities. While the original Leonidas high-power microwave (HPM) system demonstrated the ability to disable multiple drones simultaneously, Marine expeditionary operations demand equipment that can move rapidly between temporary positions, operate with limited logistics support and deploy from light tactical vehicles rather than large fixed sites. The resulting Leonidas Expeditionary, developed through the Expeditionary Directed Energy Counter-Swarm (ExDECS) programme, represents an effort to preserve the core counter-swarm effect while dramatically reducing the size, weight and [deployment]({{ 'deployment/' | relative_url }}) burden of the system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.epirusinc.com/press-releases/epirus-to-deliver-leonidas-expeditionary-in-partnership-with-onr-jco-usmc-expanding-high-power-microwave-product-suite" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: epirusinc.com">[Epirus]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">epirusinc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EpirusEpirus to Deliver Leonidas Expeditionary in Partnership...23 Sept 2024 — As part of the contract, Epirus will deliver a completed...</span></span></span>

For Marines operating dispersed expeditionary forces, a powerful system that cannot move easily may be less valuable than a somewhat smaller system that can accompany the force wherever it goes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://defensescoop.com/2024/09/23/marines-microwave-weapon-hpm-epirus-leonidas-expeditionary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defensescoop.com">[DefenseScoop]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defensescoop.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">marines microwave weapon hpm epirus leonidas expeditionary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Marines to get new drone-killing microwave weapon...23 Sept 2024 — The high-power microwave weapon prototype, known as Leoni...</span></span></span>

## What Expeditionary Counter-Swarm Missions Require

Marine Corps operational concepts such as Expeditionary Advanced Base Operations (EABO) and stand-in forces emphasise small, distributed units operating from temporary locations that may be established and abandoned quickly. These forces are expected to remain mobile, avoid presenting large signatures and operate with limited infrastructure. At the same time, they face increasing threats from reconnaissance drones, loitering munitions and coordinated drone [swarms]({{ 'swarms/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.epirusinc.com/press-releases/epirus-to-deliver-leonidas-expeditionary-in-partnership-with-onr-jco-usmc-expanding-high-power-microwave-product-suite" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: epirusinc.com">[Epirus]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">epirusinc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EpirusEpirus to Deliver Leonidas Expeditionary in Partnership...23 Sept 2024 — As part of the contract, Epirus will deliver a completed...</span></span></span>

That creates a difficult requirement for [counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) systems. Traditional air-defence equipment often assumes access to established bases, larger vehicles or substantial support networks. Marine expeditionary units cannot always rely on those conditions. They require systems that can:

## How ExDECS Changed the Packaging Problem

The ExDECS programme effectively turned a technology problem into a packaging problem. The central question was not whether Leonidas could defeat drones; earlier [variants]({{ 'variants/' | relative_url }}) had already demonstrated the viability of the microwave approach. The challenge was whether the capability could be condensed into a form suitable for Marine expeditionary operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://defensescoop.com/2023/01/23/microwave-weapon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defensescoop.com">[DefenseScoop]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defensescoop.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Army awards $66M contract to Epirus for microwave...January 23, 2023 — 23 Jan 2023 — Army awards $66M contract to Epirus for...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 23, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why Transportability Matters as Much as Power

A recurring misunderstanding about directed-energy weapons is that maximum power output is always the dominant performance metric. For expeditionary forces, transportability can be equally important.

A larger microwave system may provide greater engagement capacity, but it also creates challenges. Bigger systems require larger vehicles, more fuel, additional support equipment and more predictable operating locations. Reducing size and weight is not automatically beneficial.
