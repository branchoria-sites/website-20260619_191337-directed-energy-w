## Introduction

A high-energy laser can be an exceptionally cheap way to destroy a drone or other airborne threat, but only when the [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}) allows enough energy to reach the target. In poor weather, the key question is not whether a laser works in principle, but whether commanders can trust it to work quickly and reliably enough when failure has consequences. Rain, fog, dust, smoke, sea spray and atmospheric turbulence can weaken, scatter or distort a laser beam, reducing the amount of energy delivered to the target. Under those conditions, a missile often becomes the safer choice because it carries its destructive effect to the target rather than depending on a clear path through the air.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2Air and Space Power Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 106717</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — Directed energy weapons—such as lasers—use energy fired at the speed of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span>

This weather-driven trade-off is one of the most important reasons that modern air-defence planners generally view [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}) as a complement to missiles rather than a replacement for them. ## How Rain, Fog and Dust Weaken the Beam

Laser weapons must deliver a concentrated amount of energy onto a small area of a target for a sufficient period of time. Weather interferes with that process in several ways.

**Rain and fog scatter light.** Water droplets absorb and scatter portions of the beam before it reaches the target. Thick fog is especially problematic because it contains vast numbers of suspended droplets that can significantly reduce beam transmission. Research on atmospheric laser propagation consistently identifies fog, rain and cloud as major sources of attenuation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eureka.patsnap.com/report-atmospheric-effects-on-long-range-laser-power-transmission" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eureka.patsnap.com">[Eureka+2Air and Space Power Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eureka.patsnap.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report atmospheric effects on long range laser power transmission</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atmospheric Effects On Long-Range Laser Power...28 Aug 2025 — Weather conditions dramatically affect transmission efficiency, with...</span></span></span>

**Dust, smoke and aerosols spread the energy.** Airborne particles can divert energy away from the target and reduce the beam's effectiveness. Desert environments, battlefields filled with smoke, and maritime environments with sea spray can all create conditions that degrade performance. **Turbulence distorts the beam.** Uneven heating and movement within the atmosphere alter the path of light as it travels. Instead of arriving as a tightly focused point, the beam can become distorted, reducing the intensity delivered to the target. Atmospheric turbulence is a long-recognised challenge in high-energy laser propagation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitalcommons.ndu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1016&amp;context=ndupress-books" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalcommons.ndu.edu">[Digital Commons+2Baghdad Science Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalcommons.ndu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Digital Commons Effects of Directed Energy Weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The techniques for doing... **Thermal [blooming]({{ 'blooming/' | relative_url }}) adds another complication.** As powerful laser energy heats the air through which it passes, the atmosphere itself can act like a distorting lens. This phenomenon, known as thermal blooming, can further reduce beam concentration, particularly over longer distances or in challenging atmospheric conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aspistrategist.org.au/war-at-the-speed-of-light-the-emerging-role-of-directed-energy-weapons/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aspistrategist.org.au">[The Strategist+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aspistrategist.org.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">war at the speed of light the emerging role of directed energy weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Strategistthe emerging role of directed-energy weapons15 Apr 2026 — atmospheric effects can reduce the effectiveness of high energy l...</span></span></span>

The practical consequence is that a target which might be disabled in a few seconds on a clear day may require longer exposure in degraded conditions, or may not receive enough energy for a reliable kill at all. ## Why Missiles Can Be More Predictable in Poor Conditions

Missiles are not immune to weather. Severe conditions can affect sensors, guidance systems and aerodynamic performance. However, their basic engagement mechanism is fundamentally different from that of a laser.

A missile carries explosive energy directly to the target. Once launched, it does not need to continuously transmit destructive power through kilometres of atmosphere. By contrast, every metre of air between a laser and its target becomes part of the engagement problem.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drillanddefense.com/laser-weapons-the-new-layer-in-modern-air-defense/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drillanddefense.com">[Drill &amp; Defense]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drillanddefense.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Weather and atmosphere can reduce effectiveness. This difference creates several operational advantages for missiles during adverse weather:

* **Less dependence on atmospheric transparency.** A missile can continue toward the target even when rain, fog or dust would significantly degrade [beam quality]({{ 'beam-quality/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://airpower.airforce.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-03/BPAF03_Directed-Energy-Weapons.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: airpower.airforce.gov.au">[Air and Space Power Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">airpower.airforce.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Air and Space Power Centre Directed Energy Weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air and Space Power CentreDirected Energy Weapons - Air and Space Power CentreAtmospheric weather conditions (eg cloud, rain, fog, changi...</span></span></span>
* **More consistent engagement ranges.** Laser range effectively shrinks as atmospheric conditions worsen, whereas missile range is generally less sensitive to visibility conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defencefinancemonitor.com/p/directed-energy-weapons-in-20252035" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defencefinancemonitor.com">[Defence Finance Monitor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defencefinancemonitor.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">directed energy weapons in 20252035</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defence Finance MonitorDirected Energy Weapons in 2025–2035: Development...23 May 2025 — The downside is weather and atmosphere: laser b...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2025</span></span></span>
* **Greater confidence against high-value threats.** When defending a ship, air base or critical infrastructure, commanders may prefer the weapon with the highest predicted probability of kill rather than the lowest cost per shot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 106717</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — Directed energy weapons—such as lasers—use energy fired at the speed of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span>
* **Reduced sensitivity to brief weather changes.** A sudden patch of fog, smoke plume or dust cloud can alter laser performance during an engagement window. Missile intercepts are often less vulnerable to such short-term atmospheric variations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drillanddefense.com/laser-weapons-the-new-layer-in-modern-air-defense/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drillanddefense.com">[Drill &amp; Defense]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drillanddefense.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Weather and atmosphere can reduce effectiveness. Fog, rain, smoke, dust, sea spray, and...</span></span></span>

The result is that missiles frequently provide a more predictable outcome when atmospheric uncertainty becomes a major factor.

## When Weather Turns a Cheap Shot into a Risky Bet

The appeal of laser air defence comes largely from economics. Systems can potentially engage large numbers of inexpensive drones at a tiny fraction of the cost of missile interceptors. The central lesson is not that [bad weather]({{ 'bad-weather/' | relative_url }}) makes lasers useless. Modern high-energy lasers can operate through many atmospheric conditions, and developers continue to improve their resilience.
