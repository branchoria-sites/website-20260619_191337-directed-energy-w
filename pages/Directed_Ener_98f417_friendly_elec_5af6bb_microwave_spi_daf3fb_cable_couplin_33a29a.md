## Introduction

In discussions of microwave spillover from directed energy weapons, attention often focuses on antennas and exposed electronics. In practice, however, cables are often the more important vulnerability. A device may sit inside a metal enclosure and appear well protected, yet still be connected to power leads, network cables, sensor lines or wiring harnesses that extend into the surrounding environment. Those conductors can collect microwave energy and carry it directly into sensitive circuits. As a result, equipment located outside the strongest part of a microwave field may still experience disruption if connected wiring provides an efficient coupling path. Studies of high-power microwave (HPM), intentional electromagnetic interference (IEMI) and electromagnetic pulse (EMP) effects consistently identify cables and wiring harnesses as major routes by which external electromagnetic energy enters electronic systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hplpb.com.cn/en/article/doi/10.11884/HPLPB202537.240225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hplpb.com.cn">[hplpb.com.cn+2EMPCommission.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hplpb.com.cn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Simulation and experimental study on high power...by X Tian · 2025 · Cited by 6 — High power microwave is easy to enter the system throu...</span></span></span>

## When Cables Become Unintended Antennas

A cable is fundamentally a conductor placed in an electromagnetic environment. When a microwave field passes across it, electric and magnetic fields induce currents and voltages along the conductor. In effect, the cable begins behaving like an unintended receiving antenna. The collected energy then travels along the wire toward connected equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3056672_Susceptibility_of_Electronic_Systems_to_High-Power_Microwaves_Summary_of_Test_Experience" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate+2hplpb.com.cn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate Susceptibility of Electronic Systems to High-Power ...wire harnesses acting as unintentional receiving antennas susceptibil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGateSusceptibility of Electronic Systems to High-Power ...wire harnesses acting as unintentional receiving antennas susceptibilit...</span></span></span>

This mechanism matters because modern electronic systems are rarely isolated. A protected radio may be connected to an external antenna feed. A vehicle computer may connect to dozens of sensors through harnesses running throughout the platform. A command post computer may be linked to external power and communications networks. Each cable creates a potential coupling path through which microwave energy can bypass part of the protection provided by the enclosure itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.empcommission.org/docs/A2473-EMP_Commission-7MB.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: empcommission.org">[EMPCommission.org+2ETS-Lindgren]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">empcommission.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">m the many antennae (cable connections) that may compromise...</span></span></span>

Research on high-power electromagnetic environments repeatedly identifies field-to-cable coupling as a key source of induced voltages. Even when the primary equipment remains shielded, energy collected by external wiring can be conducted into internal electronics where voltages become concentrated at circuit inputs, interfaces and semiconductor components.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hplpb.com.cn/en/article/doi/10.11884/HPLPB202537.240225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hplpb.com.cn">[hplpb.com.cn+2Re.Public Polimi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hplpb.com.cn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Simulation and experimental study on high power...by X Tian · 2025 · Cited by 6 — High power microwave is easy to enter the system throu...</span></span></span>

## How Wiring Collects Electromagnetic Energy

The amount of energy coupled into a cable depends on several physical factors.

**Orientation to the incoming field.** A cable aligned favourably with the incident electric field generally intercepts more energy than one oriented differently. This is one reason susceptibility varies with geometry and installation details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://re.public.polimi.it/retrieve/e0c31c0e-a387-4599-e053-1705fe0aef77/FINAL_VERSION.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: re.public.polimi.it">[Re.Public Polimi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">re.public.polimi.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Worst-Case Wideband Radiated IEMI for Unshielded and...by T Liang · Cited by 19 — This work deals with Intentional Electromagnetic...</span></span></span>

**Exposure length.** Longer exposed conductors provide a larger structure on which electromagnetic fields can induce currents. Cable runs extending across vehicles, buildings or equipment bays therefore present larger collection areas than short internal jumpers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/effects-on-structures-electromagnetic-shielding-effectiveness-con" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: impact.ornl.gov">[Oak Ridge National Laboratory+2LinkedIn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">impact.ornl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">effects on structures electromagnetic shielding effectiveness con</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oak Ridge National LaboratoryEffects on Structure&#x27;s Electromagnetic Shielding...by DP Mignardot · 2024 · Cited by 2 — This work studies...</span></span></span>

**Terminal conditions.** The way a cable is connected at each end influences how induced energy behaves. [Reflections]({{ 'reflections/' | relative_url }}) and standing-wave effects can increase voltages at specific points, sometimes producing higher stress at equipment interfaces than a simple field-strength estimate would suggest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://re.public.polimi.it/retrieve/e0c31c0e-a387-4599-e053-1705fe0aef77/FINAL_VERSION.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: re.public.polimi.it">[Re.Public Polimi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">re.public.polimi.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Worst-Case Wideband Radiated IEMI for Unshielded and...by T Liang · Cited by 19 — This work deals with Intentional Electromagnetic...</span></span></span>

**Common-mode coupling.** Energy does not always travel on the intended signal path. External fields often drive currents on the outside of cable shields or across multiple conductors simultaneously. These common-mode currents can later convert into damaging voltages inside equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://re.public.polimi.it/retrieve/e0c31c0e-a387-4599-e053-1705fe0aef77/FINAL_VERSION.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: re.public.polimi.it">[Re.Public Polimi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">re.public.polimi.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Worst-Case Wideband Radiated IEMI for Unshielded and...by T Liang · Cited by 19 — This work deals with Intentional Electromagnetic...</span></span></span>

## Why Cable Length and Routing Change Risk

Not all cables are equally vulnerable. Their physical dimensions and routing strongly influence how much microwave energy they collect.

Cable length is particularly important because conductors exhibit resonant behaviour. When a cable's electrical length becomes comparable to a significant fraction of the incident wavelength, coupling efficiency can increase substantially. ## Why Shielded Equipment Can Still Be Vulnerable

A common misconception is that placing electronics inside a metal enclosure eliminates [microwave risk]({{ 'microwave-risk/' | relative_url }}). Shielding is valuable, but cables often create openings in the protective barrier.

Every power feed, data line, sensor connection or communications cable crossing the enclosure boundary creates a penetration point. If electromagnetic energy is collected outside the enclosure and conducted inward through those connections, the shield no longer acts as a complete barrier. EMP and infrastructure-hardening studies frequently describe cable connections as pathways through which external electromagnetic energy can infiltrate otherwise protected systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.empcommission.org/docs/A2473-EMP_Commission-7MB.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: empcommission.org">[EMPCommission.org+2ETS-Lindgren]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">empcommission.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">m the many antennae (cable connections) that may compromise...</span></span></span>

## The Key Implication for Microwave Spillover

For friendly electronics operating near high-power microwave systems, vulnerability is not determined solely by whether the equipment sits inside the beam. The wiring connected to that equipment may extend far beyond the protected enclosure and into regions where electromagnetic fields are stronger. Those cables can collect microwave energy, conduct it inward and concentrate it at sensitive electronic interfaces. Consequently, assessing [spillover risk]({{ 'spillover-risk/' | relative_url }}) requires examining the entire connected system—including power lines, data cables and wiring harnesses—not just the protected device itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hplpb.com.cn/en/article/doi/10.11884/HPLPB202537.240225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hplpb.com.cn">[hplpb.com.cn+2EMPCommission.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hplpb.com.cn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Simulation and experimental study on high power...by X Tian · 2025 · Cited by 6 — High power microwave is easy to enter the system throu...</span></span></span>
