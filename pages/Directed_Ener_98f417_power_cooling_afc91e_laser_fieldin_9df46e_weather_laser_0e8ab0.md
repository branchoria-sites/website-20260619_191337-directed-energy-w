## Introduction

A laser weapon does not strike a target through empty space. Between the laser and its target lies a constantly changing [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}) filled with water droplets, dust, smoke, aerosols, temperature gradients, and turbulence. That reality explains why a laser that performs impressively on a clear [test range]({{ 'test-range/' | relative_url }}) may struggle under operational conditions. Weather does not merely reduce visibility; it can absorb, scatter, defocus, or redirect the beam itself, reducing the energy that reaches the target. As a result, one of the most important questions in evaluating a directed-energy weapon is not whether it can destroy a target in ideal conditions, but whether it can still deliver sufficient energy when the atmosphere is working against it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2Every CRS Report]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 106717</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — For example, fog and storms can reduce laser beam range and quality. ## What the Atmosphere Does to a Laser Beam

The effectiveness of a high-energy laser depends on concentrating energy onto a very small spot for long enough to damage or disable a target. Weather interferes with that process in several different ways.

**Scattering** occurs when particles such as fog droplets, smoke, dust, sea spray, or pollution divert light away from the beam's intended path. Energy that is scattered cannot contribute to damaging the target. Dense fog is particularly challenging because water droplets are highly effective at disrupting laser transmission. Studies of atmospheric propagation consistently identify fog, aerosols, smoke, and dust as major causes of beam degradation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://opg.optica.org/abstract.cfm?uri=ao-54-31-f201" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: opg.optica.org">[Optica+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">opg.optica.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">High-power lasers for directed-energy applicationsby P Sprangle · 2015 · Cited by 287 — Specifically, we discuss the effects of abs...</span></span></span>

**Absorption** removes energy from the beam as atmospheric gases and water vapour absorb portions of the laser's energy. Even clear air causes some attenuation, but humid conditions can significantly increase losses. The beam arriving at the target may therefore contain substantially less energy than the beam leaving the weapon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.files.ethz.ch/isn/31415/forden.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ethz.ch">[ETH Zurich Files+2Optica]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ethz.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ETH Zurich FilesThe Airborne Laser12 Nov 1996 — Even clear air attenuates, or reduces, the power of the laser beams. **Turbulence** introduces another problem. The atmosphere is not uniform; pockets of air with different temperatures and densities bend light differently. As a laser propagates through turbulent air, its beam can become distorted, wander off aim, or spread over a larger area. Instead of concentrating energy on a small point, the laser delivers a weaker dose across a wider spot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235041191_Atmospheric_Propagation_of_High_Energy_Lasers_Modeling_Simulation_Tracking_and_Control" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate+2DEPS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">laser beams propagate several kms in turbulent atmospheres, where they suffer destructive effects such...</span></span></span>

A further complication is **thermal blooming**. High-power laser beams heat the air through which they travel. That heating changes the air's refractive properties, effectively creating a lens that distorts the beam. In extreme cases, the laser begins degrading its own path, causing defocusing and reduced intensity on target. Thermal blooming has been recognised for decades as one of the major propagation challenges for high-energy laser systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ndupress.ndu.edu/portals/68/Documents/Books/CTBSP-Exports/Effects-of-Directed-Energy-Weapons.pdf?ver=2017-06-16-125220-170" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndupress.ndu.edu">[The Strategist+3NDU Press+3RP Photonics]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndupress.ndu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NDU PressEffects of Directed Energy Weapons - NDU PressSeptember 18, 2014 — 16 Jun 2017 — Thermal blooming results when the absorption of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 18, 2014</span></span></span>

## Why Clear-Range Demonstrations Can Mislead

Public demonstrations often occur under conditions chosen to maximise the chance of a successful engagement. This is not necessarily deceptive; early tests are frequently designed to validate beam control, power generation, target tracking, or another specific subsystem. The problem arises when observers assume that performance in ideal weather translates directly into battlefield effectiveness.

A clear, dry day with limited turbulence allows much more energy to reach the target than a humid day with haze, smoke, precipitation, or strong atmospheric instability. A laser that achieves a rapid kill in one environment may require much longer dwell times—or fail altogether—in another. Government assessments of directed-energy weapons routinely identify atmospheric conditions as a key limitation on range and beam quality. The U.S. Government Accountability Office, for example, notes that fog and storms can reduce laser range and effectiveness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 106717</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — For example, fog and storms can reduce laser beam range and quality. Ba...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span>

The distinction matters because laser weapons often depend on maintaining a precise beam on a target for several seconds. Unlike a missile, which carries its energy with it after launch, a laser must continuously deliver energy through the atmosphere. Any disruption during that transmission path directly affects the engagement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.onr.navy.mil/organization/departments/code-35/division-353/directed-energy-weapons-cdew-and-high-energy-lasers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onr.navy.mil">[Office of Naval Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onr.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Naval ResearchCounter Directed Energy Weapons and High Energy LasersNavy DEWs include systems that use high-energy lasers (HEL)...</span></span></span>

Real-world reporting on operational laser programmes has highlighted this issue. Analyses of deployed and trialled systems frequently cite weather as one of the most persistent operational constraints. Rain, clouds, dust, wind-driven turbulence, and airborne particles can all reduce performance, sometimes enough to change the outcome of an engagement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wsj.com/business/why-lasers-could-be-kryptonite-for-drones-88e4cd4e" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wsj.com">[The Wall Street Journal+2WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wsj.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Wall Street Journal Why Lasers Could Be Kryptonite for Drones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Army successfully used laser weapons to destroy hostile drones in the Middle East, marking a significant milestone in military technology...</span></span></span>

## The Difference Between Bad Visibility and Bad Laser Conditions

One common misunderstanding is that weather affects [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}) only because operators cannot see the target. In reality, a laser can be affected even when the target remains visible.

A light haze may have little effect on a human observer yet still scatter enough energy to reduce laser effectiveness. Similarly, turbulence created by hot ground surfaces, ship exhausts, engine plumes, or solar heating can distort a beam even under apparently clear skies. Naval applications face particular challenges because moisture, salt particles, and maritime atmospheric layers create difficult propagation environments despite unobstructed lines of sight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nps.edu/web/research/-/propagation-of-high-energy-lasers-for-various-wavelengths-power-levels-and-atmospheric-conditions-2018" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.edu">[Naval Postgraduate School+2laserwars.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An important atmospheric effect is ship-induced optical turbulence.Read more...</span></span></span>

This means that assessing weather effects requires more than ordinary visibility measurements. Operators need to understand atmospheric transmission, aerosol concentrations, humidity, turbulence strength, and local thermal conditions. Military research programmes have devoted substantial effort to modelling and characterising these effects because they can determine whether a laser engagement succeeds or fails.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deps.org/DEPSpages/USUKsymp22ShortCourse.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deps.org">[DEPS+2afit.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">USUKsymp22Short Course</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK/US DE Workshop Short Courses18 Jul 2022 —... effects of the atmosphere on directed energy weapons propagation. ## Can Technology Overcome Weather?

Modern laser weapon programmes are not ignoring these limitations. Several approaches aim to reduce atmospheric penalties.

**Adaptive optics** systems measure atmospheric distortion and rapidly adjust mirrors to compensate for it. Rather than sending a perfectly shaped beam into imperfect air, the system deliberately alters the beam before transmission so that it arrives at the target in a more focused state.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ndupress.ndu.edu/Media/News/News-Article-View/Article/2053280/directed-energy-weapons-are-real-and-disruptive/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndupress.ndu.edu">[NDU Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndupress.ndu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Press Directed Energy Weapons Are Real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">And DisruptiveJan 9, 2020 — In this case, the laser weapon system would sense the atmospheric conditions... beam quality can also help t...</span></span></span>

**Higher power levels** can also help. If weather reduces transmission efficiency, a more powerful laser may still deliver enough energy to achieve the desired effect. However, this solution has practical limits because greater power increases demands on electrical generation, cooling systems, and beam control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ndupress.ndu.edu/Media/News/News-Article-View/Article/2053280/directed-energy-weapons-are-real-and-disruptive/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndupress.ndu.edu">[NDU Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndupress.ndu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Press Directed Energy Weapons Are Real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">And DisruptiveJan 9, 2020 — In this case, the laser weapon system would sense the atmospheric conditions... beam quality can also help t...</span></span></span>

Some military research focuses on real-time characterisation of atmospheric conditions so that laser performance can be predicted before firing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nps.edu/web/research/-/propagation-of-high-energy-lasers-for-various-wavelengths-power-levels-and-atmospheric-conditions-2018" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.edu">[Naval Postgraduate School]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An important atmospheric effect is ship-induced optical turbulence.Read more...</span></span></span>

Heavy fog, dense smoke, intense dust storms, and severe precipitation remain fundamentally difficult environments for laser propagation. ## What Adverse-Weather Trials Need to Prove

Because clear-weather demonstrations can exaggerate practical performance, meaningful testing must expose laser systems to the conditions they are likely to encounter in service.

An adverse-weather trial should demonstrate:

* Consistent target tracking through turbulence and degraded visibility.
* Effective energy delivery despite atmospheric attenuation.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://eureka.patsnap.com/report-atmospheric-effects-on-long-range-laser-power-transmission" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eureka.patsnap.com">[eureka.patsnap.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eureka.patsnap.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report atmospheric effects on long range laser power transmission</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effects On Long-Range Laser Power...28 Aug 2025 — Discover how atmospheric conditions impact laser power transmission and explore soluti...</span></span></span>
* Reliable operation in rain, haze, dust, smoke, maritime humidity, or similar operational environments.
* Accurate prediction of engagement effectiveness based on measured atmospheric conditions.
* Acceptable performance degradation rather than catastrophic failure when conditions worsen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2Naval Postgraduate School]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 106717</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — For example, fog and storms can reduce laser beam range and quality. The most informative result is often not whether the laser scores a hit, but how much its effectiveness declines as atmospheric conditions deteriorate. Understanding those [limits]({{ 'limits/' | relative_url }}) helps determine when a laser is the right tool and when another weapon system may be required.

For that reason, weather is not a peripheral concern in laser warfare. It is one of the central tests separating an impressive demonstration from a dependable operational capability. A laser that works only on ideal days remains a technology demonstrator; a laser that performs predictably across changing atmospheric conditions moves much closer to becoming a practical weapon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 106717</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — For example, fog and storms can reduce laser beam range and quality.
