## Introduction

Laser weapons damage drones by depositing heat into a small area over time. That means dwell time is not determined by [laser power]({{ 'power-costs/' | relative_url }}) alone. The material being illuminated—and the specific component being hit—often decides whether failure occurs in a fraction of a second or only after several seconds of continuous exposure. A thin plastic sensor housing, a carbon-fibre rotor arm, an exposed wire, and an aluminium motor mount all respond differently to the same beam. As a result, successful [counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) laser engagements depend as much on choosing vulnerable materials and components as on generating enough power. Research on high-energy laser interactions with unmanned aircraft repeatedly shows that lightweight drone construction creates exploitable weak points, but not all parts of a drone are equally vulnerable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nps.edu/documents/10180/142489929/JDE_7-2_Johnson.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.edu">[Naval Postgraduate School+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval Postgraduate SchoolCounter Directed Energy Weapons and the Defense of...January 10, 2023 — by B Johnson · 2023 · Cited by 2 — The...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2023</span></span></span>

## Fragile Drone Parts Worth Aiming At

A laser does not necessarily need to burn through an entire drone. In many cases, disabling a single flight-critical component ends the mission. The shortest dwell times are usually achieved by targeting components that are both exposed and thermally sensitive.

Optical sensors and cameras are among the most vulnerable targets. Small lenses, image sensors, and protective covers contain materials that can be degraded by concentrated heating before major structural damage occurs elsewhere on the aircraft. Even when the airframe remains intact, loss of navigation or targeting sensors can render the drone ineffective.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/expo/directed-energy-weapons/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unmannedsystemstechnology.com">[Unmanned Systems Technology+2Nanografi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unmannedsystemstechnology.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">directed energy weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unmanned Systems TechnologyC-UAS Directed Energy Weapons25 Nov 2025 — Directed Energy Weapons for C-UAS - high-energy anti-drone lasers a...</span></span></span>

Battery systems are another attractive aim point. Most small drones rely on lithium-polymer batteries that store significant energy in a compact volume. Laser heating can damage cells, trigger thermal runaway, or degrade power delivery. Because batteries are often located near the centre of lightweight drones, direct exposure is not always possible, but when accessible they can provide a relatively fast route to mission kill.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oceanplayer.com/how-utilities-use-high-energy-lasers-to-stop-drone-attacks/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oceanplayer.com">[oceanplayer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oceanplayer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How Utilities Use High-Energy Lasers to Stop Drone Attacks</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How Utilities Use High-Energy Lasers to Stop Drone AttacksMay 2, 2026 — How laser defense systems protect power plants from ho...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2, 2026</span></span></span>

Rotor arms and support booms also present favourable targets. A multirotor aircraft depends on several slender structural members. Weakening or severing one arm may be sufficient to destabilise the aircraft and cause loss of control. Since these structures are designed for low weight rather than [battlefield]({{ 'battle-limits/' | relative_url }}) survivability, they frequently require less energy to damage than thicker central airframe sections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394748209_An_interdisciplinary_study_of_the_effect_of_laser_radiation_on_carbon_fiber-reinforced_polymer_in_the_context_of_counteracting_unmanned_aerial_vehicles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An interdisciplinary study of the effect of laser radiation on...This article presents an interdisciplinary study that combi...</span></span></span>

## Why Lightweight Materials Heat Faster

The same design choices that make drones efficient flyers often make them vulnerable to laser heating. Small unmanned aircraft are built around minimising weight, which reduces the amount of material available to absorb and distribute heat.

Thin plastics commonly used for housings and fairings have relatively low thermal mass. When laser energy is concentrated onto a small area, temperatures can rise rapidly because there is little material available to absorb the incoming heat. Instead of spreading the energy across a large structure, the material can soften, deform, char, or burn after comparatively short exposure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oceanplayer.com/how-utilities-use-high-energy-lasers-to-stop-drone-attacks/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oceanplayer.com">[oceanplayer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oceanplayer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How Utilities Use High-Energy Lasers to Stop Drone Attacks</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How Utilities Use High-Energy Lasers to Stop Drone AttacksMay 2, 2026 — How laser defense systems protect power plants from ho...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2, 2026</span></span></span>

Carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer (CFRP) structures, widely used in modern drones, present a more complex case. They are mechanically strong and lightweight, but their behaviour under laser attack differs from metals. Recent experimental work examining laser effects on CFRP drone structures found that high-energy laser exposure can damage the composite matrix and compromise structural integrity even when the material initially appears robust. The composite's layered construction can create failure mechanisms that differ from simple melting, including matrix degradation and delamination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394748209_An_interdisciplinary_study_of_the_effect_of_laser_radiation_on_carbon_fiber-reinforced_polymer_in_the_context_of_counteracting_unmanned_aerial_vehicles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An interdisciplinary study of the effect of laser radiation on...This article presents an interdisciplinary study that combi...</span></span></span>

Because rotor arms, body panels, and internal supports are often extremely thin, only a limited amount of energy may be needed to raise local temperatures to damaging levels. This is one reason why small commercial-style drones are generally considered favourable targets for laser air-defence systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nps.edu/documents/10180/142489929/JDE_7-2_Johnson.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.edu">[Naval Postgraduate School]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval Postgraduate SchoolCounter Directed Energy Weapons and the Defense of...January 10, 2023 — by B Johnson · 2023 · Cited by 2 — The...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why Hardened Targets Need More Energy

Not every drone component offers a quick kill. Dwell time increases significantly when the beam encounters materials that can absorb, conduct, or resist heat more effectively.

Metal motor housings, thicker aluminium structural parts, and reinforced mounting hardware generally require more energy before catastrophic failure occurs. Metals can conduct heat away from the illuminated spot, spreading the energy into surrounding material. This reduces peak temperature growth at the exact aim point and can extend the required exposure time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defencesciencereview.com.pl/pdf-216776-135052?filename=Laser-Based-Directed-Ener.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: com.pl">[Defence Science Review]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">com.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pdf 216776 135052</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defence Science ReviewDEFENCE SCIENCE REVIEWJanuary 17, 2026 — by A Karkadakattil · 2026 · Cited by 1 — Objectives: This review aims to e...</span></span></span>

Some military drones also incorporate more durable composite structures, protective covers, or thermal-management features. While these measures do not make a drone invulnerable, they can increase the amount of energy required to reach failure thresholds. Research into counter-directed-energy measures for UAVs has focused heavily on increasing laser dwell-time requirements through improved materials and protective design features.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nps.edu/documents/10180/142489929/JDE_7-2_Johnson.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.edu">[Naval Postgraduate School]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval Postgraduate SchoolCounter Directed Energy Weapons and the Defense of...January 10, 2023 — by B Johnson · 2023 · Cited by 2 — The...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2023</span></span></span>

Reflective and heat-resistant coatings are sometimes proposed as defensive measures. Such materials can reduce heating [efficiency]({{ 'efficiency/' | relative_url }}) or delay damage, forcing a laser to remain on target longer. ## Material Choice Turns Seconds Into Fractions of Seconds

For counter-drone [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}), the key question is rarely whether a beam can eventually damage a drone. The more important question is how long that damage takes. Material selection largely determines the answer.

A thin plastic sensor cover may fail after a brief exposure. A carbon-fibre rotor arm may lose structural strength after sustained local heating. A battery compartment may become a critical vulnerability if directly illuminated.
