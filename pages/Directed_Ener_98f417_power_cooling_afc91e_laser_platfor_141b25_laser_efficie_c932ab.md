## Introduction

Laser efficiency matters twice in a directed-energy weapon. First, it determines how much electrical power the platform must supply to generate a given laser beam. Second, it determines how much waste heat the platform must remove after each shot. A more efficient laser therefore reduces two major burdens at the same time: electrical demand and thermal management. This is why improvements in wall-plug efficiency—the percentage of input electrical energy converted into laser light—can have effects far beyond the laser itself, influencing vehicle size, [ship integration]({{ 'ship-integration/' | relative_url }}), endurance, [cooling]({{ 'cooling/' | relative_url }}) requirements and practical rate of fire. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dsiac.dtic.mil/articles/power-generation-and-storage-for-directed-energy-systems/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dsiac.dtic.mil">[DSIAC+2rp-photonics.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dsiac.dtic.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">These weapons require increased power, energy, and thermal management systems on platforms to address current and future threats.Read more...</span></span></span>

## What Wall-Plug Efficiency Means

Wall-plug efficiency is the ratio between optical output power and electrical input power. If a laser has a wall-plug efficiency of 25%, only one quarter of the electricity supplied becomes useful laser energy; the remaining three quarters are lost elsewhere in the system. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rp-photonics.com/wall_plug_efficiency.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rp-photonics.com">[rp-photonics.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rp-photonics.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wall-plug Efficiencyby R Paschotta · 2006 · Cited by 3 — High-power fiber lasers can also be very efficient, reaching around 50%, while m...</span></span></span>

For a simple illustration:

* A 100 kW laser operating at 25% efficiency requires roughly 400 kW of electrical input.
* The same 100 kW laser operating at 50% efficiency requires about 200 kW of electrical input.

The beam delivered to the target is identical, but the power-[generation]({{ 'generation-ii/' | relative_url }}) burden on the platform is cut roughly in half. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rp-photonics.com/wall_plug_efficiency.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rp-photonics.com">[rp-photonics.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rp-photonics.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wall-plug Efficiencyby R Paschotta · 2006 · Cited by 3 — High-power fiber lasers can also be very efficient, reaching around 50%, while m...</span></span></span>

This is one reason why modern fibre and advanced solid-state [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}) attracted military interest. Their efficiencies are substantially higher than many earlier laser technologies, making them more practical for ships, vehicles and potentially aircraft. High-power fibre lasers have been reported with wall-plug efficiencies around 30% and in some cases approaching 50%, while older solid-state approaches were often much lower. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://opg.optica.org/ao/abstract.cfm?uri=ao-54-31-f201" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: opg.optica.org">[Optica Publishing Group+2rp-photonics.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">opg.optica.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Optica Publishing GroupHigh-power lasers for directed-energy applicationsby P Sprangle · 2015 · Cited by 285 — Solid state lasers, in par...</span></span></span>

## Why Lost Energy Becomes Heat

The electrical energy that does not emerge as laser light does not disappear. It is converted into heat within laser diodes, gain media, power electronics and associated hardware. That heat accumulates rapidly in a compact volume and must be removed before temperatures rise enough to reduce performance, distort [beam quality]({{ 'beam-quality/' | relative_url }}) or damage components. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mobilityengineeringtech.com/component/content/article/37608-thermal-management-for-directed-energy-weapons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mobilityengineeringtech.com">[mobilityengineeringtech.com+2SAE Mobilus]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mobilityengineeringtech.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">37608 thermal management for directed energy weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Thermal Management for Directed Energy Weapons10-Sept-2020 — Advanced thermal management is one of the keys to bringing directed energy w...</span></span></span>

This creates a useful rule of thumb:

**Every improvement in efficiency reduces waste heat almost one-for-one.**

Consider a 100 kW laser:

EfficiencyElectrical InputWaste Heat25%400 kW300 kW50%200 kW100 kW

The more efficient system generates only one-third as much waste heat while producing the same beam power. The platform therefore needs smaller chillers, less coolant circulation and less [thermal-storage]({{ 'thermal-storage/' | relative_url }}) capacity. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rp-photonics.com/wall_plug_efficiency.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rp-photonics.com">[rp-photonics.com+2mobilityengineeringtech.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rp-photonics.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wall-plug Efficiencyby R Paschotta · 2006 · Cited by 3 — High-power fiber lasers can also be very efficient, reaching around 50%, while m...</span></span></span>

This is why efficiency improvements often have effects larger than their percentage increase suggests. Raising efficiency from 25% to 50% does not merely double conversion performance; it dramatically reduces the thermal-management challenge that frequently [limits]({{ 'limits/' | relative_url }}) sustained operation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mobilityengineeringtech.com/component/content/article/37608-thermal-management-for-directed-energy-weapons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mobilityengineeringtech.com">[mobilityengineeringtech.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mobilityengineeringtech.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">37608 thermal management for directed energy weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Thermal Management for Directed Energy Weapons10-Sept-2020 — Advanced thermal management is one of the keys to bringing directed energy w...</span></span></span>

## How Efficiency Changes Platform Size and Endurance

The host platform must carry not only the laser but also generators, power-conditioning equipment, batteries or energy-storage devices, cooling systems and heat exchangers. As efficiency rises, each of these supporting subsystems can become smaller or lighter. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dsiac.dtic.mil/articles/power-generation-and-storage-for-directed-energy-systems/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dsiac.dtic.mil">[DSIAC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dsiac.dtic.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">These weapons require increased power, energy, and thermal management systems on platforms to address current and future threats.Read more...</span></span></span>

For naval vessels with substantial electrical generation capacity, poor efficiency may be inconvenient but manageable. For trucks, armoured vehicles and aircraft, however, every kilowatt and every kilogram matter. A less efficient laser can require larger generators and cooling hardware than the platform can reasonably accommodate. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dsiac.dtic.mil/articles/power-generation-and-storage-for-directed-energy-systems/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dsiac.dtic.mil">[DSIAC+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dsiac.dtic.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">These weapons require increased power, energy, and thermal management systems on platforms to address current and future threats.Read more...</span></span></span>

Higher efficiency therefore affects [deployment]({{ 'deployment/' | relative_url }}) options in several ways:

* **Lower generator demand:** less electrical generation capacity is required for the same beam power.
* **Reduced cooling equipment:** smaller pumps, chillers and heat exchangers may suffice.
* **Lower fuel consumption:** platforms generating their own electricity expend less fuel per engagement.
* **Improved endurance:** systems can sustain firing for longer before thermal limits are reached.
* **Greater integration flexibility:** lasers become practical on smaller platforms that could not otherwise support the power and cooling loads. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dsiac.dtic.mil/articles/power-generation-and-storage-for-directed-energy-systems/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dsiac.dtic.mil">[DSIAC+2mobilityengineeringtech.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dsiac.dtic.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">These weapons require increased power, energy, and thermal management systems on platforms to address current and future threats.Read more...</span></span></span>

In design studies of high-energy lasers, power supply and thermal-management subsystems are often major contributors to overall size and weight. Improving laser efficiency can therefore reduce total system mass by far more than the laser module alone. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/244987980_Weight_and_Size_Model_for_Solid-State_High-Energy_Laser" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Weight and Size Model for Solid-State High-Energy LaserAppropriate evaluation of diverse SSL concepts together with concomita...</span></span></span>

## Why Efficiency Improves Effective Rate of Fire

A laser weapon's practical firing rate is often constrained by heat rather than by the laser source itself. After an engagement, accumulated thermal energy must be transported away and ultimately rejected to the environment. If heat removal cannot keep pace with firing, the weapon may have to reduce power or pause operation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mobilityengineeringtech.com/component/content/article/37608-thermal-management-for-directed-energy-weapons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mobilityengineeringtech.com">[mobilityengineeringtech.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mobilityengineeringtech.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">37608 thermal management for directed energy weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Thermal Management for Directed Energy Weapons10-Sept-2020 — Advanced thermal management is one of the keys to bringing directed energy w...</span></span></span>

Higher efficiency directly improves this situation. Because less waste heat is generated per shot, the cooling system has less work to do between engagements. ## The Double Benefit of Better Efficiency

When engineers improve wall-plug efficiency, they gain two advantages simultaneously:


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Directed_Ener_98f417_power_cooling_afc91e_laser_platfor_141b25_laser_efficie_c932ab-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Efficiency illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Directed_Ener_98f417_power_cooling_afc91e_laser_platfor_141b25_laser_efficie_c932ab-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Directed_Ener_98f417_power_cooling_afc91e_laser_platfor_141b25_laser_efficie_c932ab-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Less electrical power is required to produce a given laser output.
2. Less waste heat must be removed from the system.

</div>

That double benefit propagates throughout the weapon architecture, reducing demands on generators, power conditioning, energy storage, cooling systems and thermal-management equipment.
