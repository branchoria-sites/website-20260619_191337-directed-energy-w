## Introduction

HELIOS can interfere with hostile cameras, infrared imagers and other electro-optical sensors without necessarily destroying the platform carrying them. That makes sensor dazzling an attractive “soft-kill” option for naval forces. However, dazzling is not a guaranteed way to blind every target at sea. The same factors that affect all laser systems—weather, atmospheric distortion, range and target movement—also limit how much laser energy reaches a sensor. In addition, modern military sensors can be protected, duplicated or supplemented by other sensing methods. As a result, HELIOS sensor dazzling is best understood as a powerful but conditional capability whose effectiveness depends heavily on operational circumstances rather than a universal solution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lockheedmartin.com/en-us/news/features/2021/more-than-a-laser-helios-is-an-integrated-weapon-system.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lockheedmartin.com">[Lockheed Martin+2National Defense Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lockheedmartin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">more than a laser helios is an integrated weapon system</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lockheed MartinMore Than a Laser, HELIOS is an Integrated Weapon System9 Jan 2021 — The High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical-dazzler...</span></span></span>

## Weather, Range and Beam Delivery Problems

The maritime environment is one of the most difficult places in which to propagate laser energy. Between the laser and its target lies a constantly changing [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}) filled with moisture, salt particles, aerosols, turbulence and temperature gradients. These factors absorb, scatter and distort laser beams, reducing the amount of energy that arrives at the intended sensor. Research on naval high-energy [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}) has repeatedly identified absorption, scattering and atmospheric turbulence as major performance constraints.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nps.edu/web/research/-/propagation-of-high-energy-lasers-for-various-wavelengths-power-levels-and-atmospheric-conditions-2018" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.edu">[Naval Postgraduate School+2Laser Wars]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval Postgraduate SchoolPropagation of High Energy Lasers for Various...with Integrated Optical-dazzler and Surveillance (HELIOS) syste...</span></span></span>

[Sea conditions]({{ 'sea-conditions/' | relative_url }}) can make the problem worse. Fog, haze, sea spray and high humidity are especially troublesome because water droplets and aerosols scatter light before it reaches the target. Even when a target remains visible to the human eye, the beam may arrive less concentrated than intended, reducing dazzling effectiveness. Studies of atmospheric laser propagation show that water vapour and aerosols can significantly attenuate beam energy and alter beam shape during transmission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencepublishinggroup.com/article/10078580" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencepublishinggroup.com">[Science Publishing Group+2Semantic Scholar]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencepublishinggroup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science Publishing GroupEffect of Water and Aerosols Absorption on Laser Beam...To realize optical wireless power transmission, atmosphe...</span></span></span>

Range also matters. Sensor dazzling generally requires enough energy to be focused onto a relatively small optical aperture. As distance increases, maintaining that concentration becomes harder because atmospheric effects accumulate and tiny pointing errors translate into larger misses. A camera lens only a few centimetres wide is a far more demanding target than the body of a drone or boat.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nps.edu/web/research/-/propagation-of-high-energy-lasers-for-various-wavelengths-power-levels-and-atmospheric-conditions-2018" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.edu">[Naval Postgraduate School]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval Postgraduate SchoolPropagation of High Energy Lasers for Various...with Integrated Optical-dazzler and Surveillance (HELIOS) syste...</span></span></span>

Target motion adds another layer of difficulty. HELIOS is designed to compensate for ship movement and atmospheric conditions, but naval engagements involve moving ships, moving targets and a constantly shifting atmosphere. The laser must remain accurately aligned with a sensor that may be manoeuvring, vibrating or rapidly changing aspect angle. Any interruption in precise beam placement can reduce the dazzling effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/euronews/posts/the-high-energy-laser-with-integrated-optical-dazzler-and-surveillance-deployed-/1306875301487797/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical-dazzler and...The laser can track up to 100 targets at once, compensating instantl...</span></span></span>

## Protective Filters and Backup Sensors

Even when a laser successfully illuminates an optical system, the target may not be defenceless. Modern electro-optical protection measures include specialised filters and protective technologies intended to reduce vulnerability to laser exposure. Military sensor-protection research and commercial defence systems have long focused on filtering specific [wavelengths]({{ 'wavelengths/' | relative_url }}) and limiting the effects of laser dazzling on cameras and imaging equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gandh.com/laser-protection" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gandh.com">[G&amp;H+2ADS Absorption]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gandh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">G&amp;HLaser Protection in the FieldElectro-optical protection measures (EOPM): filters for sensor protection in advanced military infrared a...</span></span></span>

This creates a contest between the dazzler and the sensor designer. A sensor optimised for a particular mission may incorporate:

* Optical filters that reduce the intensity of laser light reaching the detector.
* Automatic exposure controls that react to sudden brightness spikes.
* Protective shutters or electronic safeguards.
* Sensor architectures designed to tolerate temporary [saturation]({{ 'saturation/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010SPIE.7834E..04R/abstract" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ui.adsabs.harvard.edu">[ADS Absorption]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ui.adsabs.harvard.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ADS AbsorptionSensor protection against laser dazzlingby G Ritt · 2010 · Cited by 17 — In this paper we discuss a novel concept to protec...</span></span></span>

Another limitation is redundancy. A drone or surveillance platform may carry multiple cameras, infrared sensors, navigation systems or data links. Even if one optical sensor is degraded, the platform may continue operating using another sensor or guidance method. Military systems increasingly combine electro-optical, infrared, radar and other sensing modes precisely because reliance on a single sensor creates a vulnerability. A dazzled camera does not necessarily mean a mission has failed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaldefensemagazine.org/articles/2022/10/19/navy-destroyer-adds-helios-laser-to-arsenal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaldefensemagazine.org">[National Defense Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaldefensemagazine.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">navy destroyer adds helios laser to arsenal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Defense MagazineNavy Destroyer Adds HELIOS Laser to Arsenal (UPDATED)19 Oct 2022 — The Optical Dazzling Interdictor Navy, or ODI...</span></span></span>

The challenge becomes even greater when the target relies primarily on radar, inertial navigation or pre-programmed flight paths rather than continuous optical observation. In such cases, dazzling may reduce intelligence-gathering capability without fully neutralising the threat.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationalinterest.org/blog/reboot/us-navys-answer-sea-launched-drones-lasers-199479" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalinterest.org">[The National Interest]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalinterest.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Interest The U.S</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Navy&#x27;s Answer To Sea-launched Drones: Lasers“The laser is meant to zap electro-optical sensors, like digital video cameras and infrared c...</span></span></span>

## Temporary Disruption Versus Lasting Damage

A common misunderstanding is that dazzling automatically destroys a sensor. In reality, outcomes exist on a spectrum. Depending on beam intensity, exposure duration, sensor design and engagement conditions, effects may range from glare and image washout to temporary saturation, degraded imagery or permanent detector damage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationalinterest.org/blog/reboot/us-navys-answer-sea-launched-drones-lasers-199479" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalinterest.org">[The National Interest]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalinterest.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Interest The U.S</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Navy&#x27;s Answer To Sea-launched Drones: Lasers“The laser is meant to zap electro-optical sensors, like digital video cameras and infrared c...</span></span></span>

Temporary disruption can still be operationally valuable. A reconnaissance drone that loses visual contact during a critical period may fail to identify targets or transmit useful intelligence. Yet temporary effects also mean the sensor may recover once illumination stops. This limits the certainty of the outcome compared with physically destroying the target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationalinterest.org/blog/reboot/us-navys-answer-sea-launched-drones-lasers-199479" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalinterest.org">[The National Interest]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalinterest.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Interest The U.S</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Navy&#x27;s Answer To Sea-launched Drones: Lasers“The laser is meant to zap electro-optical sensors, like digital video cameras and infrared c...</span></span></span>

Achieving lasting damage is generally more demanding than causing temporary interference. It often requires greater energy delivery, more precise beam placement and sufficient dwell time on the target sensor. Weather, target manoeuvres and protective measures all work against maintaining those conditions. ## Why Sensor Dazzling Remains Useful Despite These Limits

The limitations do not make HELIOS ineffective; they define the conditions under which it works best. Against exposed electro-optical and infrared sensors in favourable atmospheric conditions, dazzling can impose mission-killing effects without expending missiles or causing physical destruction. However, real-world naval operations involve imperfect weather, moving targets, [protected sensors]({{ 'protected-sensors/' | relative_url }}) and redundant sensing systems.
