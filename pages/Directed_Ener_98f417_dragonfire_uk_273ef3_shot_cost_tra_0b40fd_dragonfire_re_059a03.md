## Introduction

DragonFire’s much-publicised “£10 per shot” figure only becomes a genuine financial advantage when the system destroys enough suitable targets to offset the far larger costs of buying, integrating, maintaining and operating the weapon. The economic argument is therefore not about electricity prices. It is about whether a naval laser can replace expensive missile engagements often enough, and reliably enough, to improve the overall cost of air defence. DragonFire’s value rises sharply when it defeats low-cost drones that would otherwise consume costly interceptors, preserves limited missile inventories during prolonged operations, and remains effective under real [battlefield]({{ 'battle-limits/' | relative_url }}) conditions. If those conditions are not met, the headline cost-per-shot figure becomes far less significant. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/boost-for-armed-forces-as-new-laser-weapon-takes-down-high-speed-drones" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK+2Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">boost for armed forces as new laser weapon takes down high speed drones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The cutting-edge DragonFire...Read more...</span></span></span>

## The Break-Even Logic Behind Cheap Engagements

A laser engagement is economically attractive because it changes the cost-exchange ratio between attacker and defender. Modern militaries increasingly face inexpensive drones that can cost a few thousand or tens of thousands of pounds, while the missiles used to intercept them may cost hundreds of thousands of pounds each. DragonFire is intended to reverse that imbalance by replacing some missile shots with engagements costing roughly £10 in electrical energy. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/boost-for-armed-forces-as-new-laser-weapon-takes-down-high-speed-drones" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK+2Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">boost for armed forces as new laser weapon takes down high speed drones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The cutting-edge DragonFire...Read more...</span></span></span>

The problem is that the laser itself is not free. The UK has committed £316 million to deliver operational DragonFire systems for Royal Navy service, following years of development and testing. The weapon therefore has to generate savings over many years of service before it can be judged economically successful. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/uk/uk-beefs-up-royal-navy-counter-drone-tech-with-413-mln-laser-contract-2025-11-20/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters+2MBDA Systems]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This initiative is part of Britain&#x27;s efforts to enhance its naval defenses, particularly against drone threats. The DragonFire system is...</span></span></span>

In simplified terms, the break-even calculation looks like this:

* How many missile engagements can DragonFire replace?
* How often can it destroy targets successfully on the first attempt?
* How much does the fleet save in interceptor purchases and replenishment?
* What additional support, [maintenance]({{ 'maintenance/' | relative_url }}) and integration costs does the laser create?

The laser does not need to replace every missile. Even substituting for a fraction of engagements against drones and other low-cost aerial threats could produce substantial savings if missile expenditure would otherwise be high. This is why defence planners focus on sustained operational use rather than the electricity bill of an individual shot. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rand.org/pubs/commentary/2024/02/directed-energy-dilemmas-industrial-implications-of.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rand.org">[RAND Corporation+2Drone Warfare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rand.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RAND CorporationDirected Energy Dilemmas: Industrial Implications of...February 20, 2024 — 20 Feb 2024 — DEWs offer a way of dealing wit...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 20, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why Reliability and Target Choice Decide the Savings

The cheapest engagement is not necessarily the cheapest defence outcome. If a laser fails to neutralise a threat and a missile must then be fired anyway, the defender may incur both the laser engagement cost and the missile cost.

For that reason, DragonFire's economics depend heavily on reliability. Official trials have demonstrated successful interceptions of aerial targets, including [high-speed]({{ '650-km-h/' | relative_url }}) drones, and the system is promoted as being capable of highly precise targeting. Those achievements are important because cost savings only emerge when successful laser kills replace conventional interceptors rather than supplement them. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uk.leonardo.com/en/innovation/dragonfire" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uk.leonardo.com">[Leonardo UK+2GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uk.leonardo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UKDragon Fire – Laser Directed Energy Weapon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Leonardo UKDragonFire – Laser Directed Energy Weapon - Leonardo UKThe DragonFire laser directed energy weapon (LDEW) system achieved the...</span></span></span>

Target selection is equally important. DragonFire is most economically attractive against threats such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* Small and medium drones.
* Reconnaissance UAVs.
* Loitering munitions.
* Other relatively low-cost airborne systems.

</div>

These are precisely the targets that can create unfavourable economic exchanges when expensive missiles are used against them. Research on drone warfare repeatedly highlights how defenders can spend vastly more money on interception than attackers spend on the attacking systems themselves. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/6375919.pdf?abstractid=6375919&amp;mirid=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: papers.ssrn.com">[SSRN+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">papers.ssrn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">a quantitative analysis of low-cost drone warfareKey findings reveal profound cost-exchange ratios that favor drone operators: Patrio...</span></span></span>

The laser becomes less economically decisive when facing targets that require extremely high confidence of destruction, operate beyond effective laser conditions, or demand engagement at longer ranges. In such situations conventional missiles may still be preferred, reducing the number of opportunities for the laser to generate savings. Weather, atmospheric interference and [line-of-sight]({{ 'dwell-time/' | relative_url }}) constraints remain recognised limitations of high-energy laser systems. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/uk-dragonfire-laser-downs-high-speed-drones" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tomshardware.com">[Tom&#x27;s Hardware+2Navy Lookout]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tomshardware.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A $413 million (£316 million) contract has been awarded to MBDA UK to deploy DragonFire on the Royal Navy’s Type 45 destroyers starting i...</span></span></span>

## How Missile Preservation Changes the Value Calculation

One of DragonFire’s most important economic benefits may not appear in a simple cost-per-shot comparison at all.

Warships carry only a finite number of missiles. Once those missiles are expended, replenishment may require returning to port or arranging complex resupply operations. During prolonged deployments or [saturation]({{ 'saturation/' | relative_url }}) attacks, preserving missile stocks can become as important as reducing expenditure. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://interestingengineering.com/military/uk-dragonfire-laser-2027-deployment" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: interestingengineering.com">[Interesting Engineering+2UK Defence Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">interestingengineering.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uk dragonfire laser 2027 deployment</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Interesting EngineeringUK sets DragonFire laser for 2027 with near-zero cost per...2 Apr 2026 — DragonFire&#x27;s near-zero cost per shot cou...</span></span></span>

A laser offers what is often described as an extremely deep or effectively renewable magazine, provided sufficient electrical power is available. Instead of consuming a physical interceptor every time a drone appears, the ship can engage many suitable targets without depleting its missile inventory. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://interestingengineering.com/military/uk-dragonfire-laser-2027-deployment" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: interestingengineering.com">[Interesting Engineering+2TechRadar]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">interestingengineering.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uk dragonfire laser 2027 deployment</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Interesting EngineeringUK sets DragonFire laser for 2027 with near-zero cost per...2 Apr 2026 — DragonFire&#x27;s near-zero cost per shot cou...</span></span></span>

This changes the value equation in three ways:

**First, missile stocks are reserved for harder targets.** High-performance aircraft, anti-ship missiles and other demanding threats can continue to receive priority access to the ship’s conventional interceptors. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rand.org/pubs/commentary/2024/02/directed-energy-dilemmas-industrial-implications-of.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rand.org">[RAND Corporation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rand.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RAND CorporationDirected Energy Dilemmas: Industrial Implications of...February 20, 2024 — 20 Feb 2024 — DEWs offer a way of dealing wit...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 20, 2024</span></span></span>

**Second, logistics costs may fall.** Fewer missile expenditures mean fewer replacement rounds need to be procured, transported and stored over the system’s lifetime. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ukdefencejournal.org.uk/laser-gun-confirmed-for-british-warship-next-year/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ukdefencejournal.org.uk">[UK Defence Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ukdefencejournal.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">laser gun confirmed for british warship next year</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK Defence JournalLaser gun confirmed for British warship next year31 Mar 2026 — One of the key features highlighted by the Ministry of D...</span></span></span>
