## Introduction

The USS *Preble* HELIOS test is one of the most important public data points in the modern history of naval directed-energy weapons because it moved the discussion beyond laboratory demonstrations and into an operational destroyer environment. What the public record confirms is relatively narrow but still significant: the Navy used the HELIOS laser aboard the Arleigh Burke-class destroyer *[USS Preble]({{ 'preble-test-2ed0d5/' | relative_url }})* against an unmanned aerial vehicle target and reported that the system successfully demonstrated its intended capability. What remains unknown is almost as important as what was revealed. The Navy has disclosed very little about range, weather conditions, target type, engagement duration, kill mechanism, or operational limitations. As a result, the test proved that a shipboard laser can engage drone targets at sea from an operational warship, but it did not prove that [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}) are ready to replace conventional naval air-defence weapons. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navytimes.com/news/your-navy/2025/02/04/us-navy-hits-drone-with-helios-laser-in-successful-test/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navytimes.com">[Navy Times+2Maritime Executive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navytimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">us navy hits drone with helios laser in successful test</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Navy TimesUS Navy hits drone with HELIOS laser in successful testFeb 4, 2025 — The destroyer Preble successfully test-fired its HELIOS sy...</span></span></span>

## What the Public Test Record Confirms

Public awareness of the event emerged through the U.S. Department of Defense's Fiscal Year 2024 operational testing reporting. According to the released material, the Navy conducted a demonstration aboard USS *Preble* to “verify and validate” the functionality, performance and capability of the High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical Dazzler and Surveillance (HELIOS) system against an unmanned aerial vehicle target. The U.S. Center for Countermeasures collected imagery during the engagement to support performance evaluation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spacewar.com/reports/US_Navy_intercepted_test_drone_with_HELIOS_directed-energy_weapon_999.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacewar.com">[spacewar.com+2Navy Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacewar.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">US Navy intercepted test drone with HELIOS directed energy weapon 999</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Navy intercepted test drone with HELIOS directed-...5 Feb 2025 —... 2024 Operational Test &amp; Evaluation report says on page 388. The test also validated a broader concept that naval planners have pursued for years: using a laser as an additional defensive layer against relatively inexpensive drones. Traditional interceptors and missiles can cost hundreds of thousands or millions of dollars per shot, while a laser engagement primarily consumes electrical power and [cooling]({{ 'cooling/' | relative_url }}) capacity. The *Preble* demonstration therefore provided evidence that HELIOS can participate in the [counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) mission for which it was designed. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.upi.com/Science_News/2025/02/05/navy-helios-directed-energy-weapon/3101738794087/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upi.com">[UPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">navy helios directed energy weapon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Navy intercepted test drone with HELIOS directed-energy...5 Feb 2025 — The US Navy has developed a directed-energy weapon that can inter...</span></span></span>

## What the Reports Do Not Reveal

The most striking feature of the *Preble* test is how much information remains classified or undisclosed.

The public record does not identify:

* The exact date of the engagement.
* The location of the test.
* The drone model used as the target.
* The target's speed, altitude or manoeuvring profile.
* The engagement range.
* The weather and atmospheric conditions.
* The laser dwell time required to achieve the effect. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.twz.com/news-features/navy-helios-laser-aboard-uss-preble-zaps-drone-in-latest-test" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: twz.com">[twz.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">twz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">navy helios laser aboard uss preble zaps drone in latest test</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The War ZoneNavy HELIOS Laser Aboard USS Preble Zaps Drone In...3 Feb 2025 — Laser weapons can only engage one target at a time and they...</span></span></span>

These omissions matter because laser performance is highly dependent on circumstances. A laser defeating a slow, predictable drone at short range in favourable weather is a very different achievement from defeating a fast, manoeuvring target in rain, haze, sea spray or rough maritime conditions.

The released imagery is similarly limited. The widely circulated photograph shows a visible beam-like effect extending from the ship, but it provides no meaningful technical information about [laser power]({{ 'power-costs/' | relative_url }}), engagement geometry or combat effectiveness. The image demonstrates that a firing event occurred; it does not allow outside observers to evaluate operational performance. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navytimes.com/news/your-navy/2025/02/04/us-navy-hits-drone-with-helios-laser-in-successful-test/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navytimes.com">[Navy Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navytimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">us navy hits drone with helios laser in successful test</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Navy TimesUS Navy hits drone with HELIOS laser in successful testFeb 4, 2025 — The destroyer Preble successfully test-fired its HELIOS sy...</span></span></span>

Another unanswered question concerns repeatability. A single successful engagement proves a capability exists. It does not establish how often that capability works, how quickly targets can be engaged in sequence, or how the system performs during prolonged operations. ## Why the Missing Details Matter

The withheld details are not minor technical curiosities. They determine whether HELIOS should be viewed as an experimental success, a useful niche weapon, or a major transformation in naval defence.

### Range Determines Strategic Value

A laser's usefulness increases dramatically if it can engage threats at tactically significant distances. Without disclosed range data, observers cannot determine whether HELIOS is primarily a close-in defensive system or a weapon capable of engaging threats far from the ship.

A laser that defeats drones only at relatively short distances may still be valuable, but it occupies a different operational role than a system capable of long-range air defence. Shipboard lasers operate in one of the most challenging environments possible. Humidity, rain, fog, sea spray and atmospheric turbulence can degrade [beam quality]({{ 'beam-quality/' | relative_url }}) and reduce effectiveness.

## The Real Significance of the Preble Test

The most defensible conclusion is that the USS *Preble* demonstration proved integration rather than dominance.

The test showed that a 60-kilowatt-class naval laser could be installed on an operational destroyer, connected to the ship's combat systems, track an aerial target, and achieve a successful engagement under real maritime conditions.
