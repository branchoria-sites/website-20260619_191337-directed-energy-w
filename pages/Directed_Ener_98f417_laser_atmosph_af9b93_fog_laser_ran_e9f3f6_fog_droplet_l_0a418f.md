## Introduction

Not all fog degrades a laser beam in the same way. Two fog banks can produce similar visibility for a human observer yet impose very different losses on a high-energy laser. The key reason is droplet size distribution: the number of droplets present, their diameters, and how those diameters are spread across the population. For directed energy systems, these microphysical details determine how much laser energy is scattered away from the target, how much is absorbed by water, and how much remains concentrated on the aim point. Studies of fog optics consistently show that attenuation depends not merely on how much water is suspended in the air, but on the size spectrum of the droplets themselves. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10546-021-00675-5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[Springer+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microphysics and Optical Attenuation in Fog</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microphysics and Optical Attenuation in Fog - Springer Natureby Q Wang · 2021 · Cited by 17 — For fog/mist conditions, the main a...</span></span></span>

## Which Fog Hurts Laser Range Most?

The fogs that most severely shorten laser range are generally those containing large numbers of droplets whose sizes interact efficiently with the laser [wavelength]({{ 'wavelengths/' | relative_url }}). In practice, this means that droplet distributions often matter more than a simple visibility measurement.

Meteorological observations show that fog droplets typically range from fractions of a micrometre to several tens of micrometres in diameter. Different fog types can have markedly different size distributions even when they appear similarly opaque to the eye. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4433/11/6/596" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mdpi.com">[MDPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mdpi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fog Classification by Their Droplet Size Distributionsby P Duthon · 2020 · Cited by 39 — All the studies show that fog droplet size r...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because laser attenuation is governed by the combined optical cross-section of all droplets along the beam path. A fog composed of many medium-sized droplets can remove more energy from a beam than a fog containing fewer but larger droplets, even if both produce comparable visual obscuration. Researchers studying atmospheric optical propagation have repeatedly found that extinction is highly sensitive to particle-size distribution, not just visibility or liquid water content. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7755092_Empirical_relationships_between_extinction_coefficient_and_visibility_in_fog" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Empirical relationships between extinction coefficient and...Correspondingly, the relationship between extinction coefficien...</span></span></span>

## Mie Scattering in Water Droplets

The dominant mechanism is Mie scattering, which occurs when particles are roughly comparable to or larger than the wavelength of the light. Most fog droplets fall into exactly this regime for the near-infrared wavelengths commonly discussed for high-power laser systems. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10546-021-00675-5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[Springer+2MDPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microphysics and Optical Attenuation in Fog</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microphysics and Optical Attenuation in Fog - Springer Natureby Q Wang · 2021 · Cited by 17 — For fog/mist conditions, the main a...</span></span></span>

Unlike Rayleigh scattering, which strongly favours short wavelengths, Mie scattering is controlled by the relationship between wavelength and droplet size. Water droplets act as tiny lenses and scattering centres that redirect energy away from the beam core. Even when much of the scattered energy remains in the forward direction, it is no longer concentrated in the tight spot required for efficient target heating. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/9/14/2843" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mdpi.com">[MDPI+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mdpi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Light Transmission in Fog: The Influence of Wavelength on...by P Duthon · 2019 · Cited by 80 — The scattering phenomenon is caused b...</span></span></span>

As droplet diameter increases, scattering becomes increasingly forward-peaked. This can create a misleading situation in which some beam energy continues travelling generally toward the target while the [beam quality]({{ 'beam-quality/' | relative_url }}) deteriorates. For a laser weapon, the critical metric is not simply transmitted power but power density on target. Forward-scattered light that spreads beyond the intended spot contributes little to damaging effects. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2023/9715482" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[Wiley Online Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiley Online LibraryThe Multiple Scattering of Laser Beam Propagation in...10 Jan 2023 — Based on the size distribution of fog, the scat...</span></span></span>

Research using Mie-theory calculations and Monte Carlo propagation models shows that extinction coefficients, scattering phase functions, and multiple-scattering behaviour all change as droplet sizes change. Consequently, two fogs with similar visibility can produce substantially different delivered energy at the target. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2023/9715482" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[Wiley Online Library+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiley Online LibraryThe Multiple Scattering of Laser Beam Propagation in...10 Jan 2023 — Based on the size distribution of fog, the scat...</span></span></span>

## Dense Fog Versus Lighter Obscuration

A common assumption is that denser fog always causes proportionally greater laser losses. While denser fog generally increases attenuation, droplet size can alter the outcome significantly.

Consider two simplified cases:

* **Many small droplets:** The beam encounters a large number of scattering events over a short distance. Multiple scattering becomes important, and energy is repeatedly redirected away from the beam axis.
* **Fewer larger droplets:** Individual droplets may scatter strongly, but the beam encounters fewer obstacles. Depending on the distribution, extinction may be lower than expected from visibility alone.
* **Mixed populations:** Real fogs often contain both small and larger droplets, producing attenuation characteristics that cannot be inferred from average droplet size alone. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355781230_Microphysics_and_Optical_Attenuation_in_Fog_Observations_from_Two_Coastal_Sites" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate+2MDPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The visibility correlated with the droplet number concentration and liquid water content. ## Why Droplet Distributions Matter More Than Simple Visibility

Visibility is an integrated measurement of how strongly the [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}) extinguishes ordinary light. It does not uniquely describe the underlying droplet population.

Several studies have found that the relationship between visibility and extinction becomes increasingly sensitive to particle-size distribution as fog thickens. In other words, identical visibility values can arise from different combinations of droplet size and concentration. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7755092_Empirical_relationships_between_extinction_coefficient_and_visibility_in_fog" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Empirical relationships between extinction coefficient and...Correspondingly, the relationship between extinction coefficien...</span></span></span>

This becomes especially important when comparing different fog types. Advection fog and radiation fog often exhibit different droplet spectra. ## The Mechanism That Ultimately Reduces Range

Fog shortens laser range because every droplet removes energy from the tightly focused beam through scattering and, to a lesser extent, absorption. Droplet size determines how efficiently this happens and how the scattered energy is distributed. When the atmosphere contains a droplet population that strongly interacts with the laser wavelength, attenuation rises, beam quality degrades, and less energy reaches the target in a useful form. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10546-021-00675-5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[Springer+2Chinese Physics B]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microphysics and Optical Attenuation in Fog</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microphysics and Optical Attenuation in Fog - Springer Natureby Q Wang · 2021 · Cited by 17 — For fog/mist conditions, the main a...</span></span></span>
