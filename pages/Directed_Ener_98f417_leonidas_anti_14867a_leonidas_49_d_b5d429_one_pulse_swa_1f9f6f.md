## Introduction

The most important aspect of the Leonidas demonstration was not the number 49 by itself. It was the fact that dozens of drones were reportedly defeated with a single microwave engagement rather than through dozens of separate intercepts. In the [counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) world, that distinction addresses one of the hardest problems facing modern air defence: how to stop large numbers of inexpensive drones arriving at the same time. ## What One-to-Many Defeat Means

Traditional air-defence systems are generally designed around a one-to-one logic. A missile engages one target. A gun fires at one target area. Even many electronic-warfare systems focus on individual links or specific aircraft.

A high-power microwave system operates differently. It projects electromagnetic energy into a volume of space, creating an effect that can potentially disrupt the electronics of multiple drones within that area at nearly the same moment. The concept is often described as a "one-to-many" capability because a single engagement can affect numerous targets rather than requiring a separate shot for each one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.twz.com/land/army-puts-50m-bet-on-next-gen-leonidas-high-power-microwave-counter-drone-tech" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: twz.com">[The War Zone]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">twz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">army puts 50m bet on next gen leonidas high power microwave counter drone tech</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The War ZoneArmy Puts $43M Bet On Next Gen Leonidas High Power...17 Jul 2025 — Epirus describes IFPC-HPM as an effective, cost-efficient...</span></span></span>

That matters because swarms are designed to exploit the opposite condition. Their strength comes from numbers. If defenders must spend one interceptor per drone, attackers can force a costly exchange simply by launching enough aircraft. A system capable of affecting dozens of drones simultaneously changes that calculation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defencefinancemonitor.com/p/non-kinetic-counter-uas-with-high" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defencefinancemonitor.com">[Defence Finance Monitor+2DroneShield]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defencefinancemonitor.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Defence Finance Monitor Non-Kinetic Counter-UAS with High Power Microwave</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defence Finance MonitorNon-Kinetic Counter-UAS with High Power MicrowaveDecember 8, 2025 — 8 Dec 2025 — They can be overwhelmed by sheer...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why Swarms Stress Traditional Defences

The challenge posed by drone swarms is often described as a [saturation]({{ 'saturation/' | relative_url }}) problem.

A defender may be able to stop one drone, five drones, or even ten drones without difficulty. The problem emerges when dozens arrive together. Sensors must track them, command systems must assign targets, and weapons must engage quickly enough to prevent penetration of the defended area.

Several pressures emerge simultaneously:

* **Time pressure:** Operators have less time to react when many targets appear together.
* **Magazine pressure:** Missile and gun systems carry finite ammunition.
* **Cost pressure:** Expensive interceptors may be used against very [cheap drones]({{ 'missile-mismatch/' | relative_url }}).
* **Tracking pressure:** Large numbers of moving objects increase command-and-control complexity.

Military analysts and defence organisations increasingly point to mass-produced drones as a means of overwhelming traditional defences through sheer quantity. The objective is not necessarily that every drone survives, but that enough get through after defenders exhaust capacity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aspistrategist.org.au/a-counter-to-drone-swarms-high-power-microwave-weapons/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aspistrategist.org.au">[The Strategist+2DroneShield]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aspistrategist.org.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a counter to drone swarms high power microwave weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The StrategistA counter to drone swarms: high-power microwave weapons12 May 2025 — Military forces must prioritise a counter to drone swa...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2025</span></span></span>

This is why the Leonidas result attracted attention. The demonstration targeted the capacity problem directly. ## What the Camp Atterbury Result Actually Showed

The strongest conclusion from the demonstration is narrower than some headlines suggested.

The test did not prove that every future drone swarm can be defeated with a single microwave pulse. Nor did it establish universal effectiveness across all drone types, flight profiles, environments, or operational conditions.

What it did show was that a high-power microwave system demonstrated a large-scale simultaneous engagement against airborne drones under live-fire test conditions. According to Epirus, the event culminated in the defeat of a 49-drone swarm consisting of two representative drone types, while the broader demonstration involved 61 drones across five scenarios. Observers included representatives from the US Department of Defense and allied nations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.epirusinc.com/press-releases/epirus-leonidas-high-power-microwave-defeats-49-drone-swarm-100-of-drones-flown-at-live-fire-demonstration" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: epirusinc.com">[Epirus+2Axios]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">epirusinc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tap to unmute.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EpirusEpirus&#x27; Leonidas High-Power Microwave Defeats 49-Drone...10 Sept 2025 — Leonidas neutralized 61-of-61 drones, culminating in a 49...</span></span></span>

The result is significant because it moves beyond laboratory claims. ## Why the Debate Changed

The counter-swarm debate has increasingly shifted from asking whether microwave weapons work at all to asking how well they work, at what range, against which drones, and under what conditions.

The 49-drone engagement mattered because it provided a visible example of the capability advocates have argued for over many years: using directed electromagnetic energy to impose effects across an entire swarm rather than fighting each aircraft individually. That does not settle every operational question, but it changes the burden of proof. The discussion is no longer centred solely on theory.
