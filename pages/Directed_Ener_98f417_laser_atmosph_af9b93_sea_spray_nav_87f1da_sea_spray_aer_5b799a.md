## Introduction

For naval directed-energy weapons, the atmosphere just above the sea surface can be a greater problem than obvious [bad weather]({{ 'bad-weather/' | relative_url }}). Even when visibility appears good and there is no fog or rain, breaking waves continuously inject salt particles and microscopic droplets into the marine boundary layer, the lowest layer of air above the ocean. These sea-spray aerosols scatter light, absorb some energy, and interact with humidity and turbulence in ways that can degrade laser [beam quality]({{ 'beam-quality/' | relative_url }}). As a result, a low-altitude laser shot skimming the sea surface often faces a harsher propagation environment than a shot directed through higher, cleaner air. Studies of maritime optical propagation and naval high-energy laser modelling consistently identify marine aerosols, humidity and near-surface turbulence as key factors limiting beam performance and engagement range.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navysbir.com/n23_A/N23A-T012.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navysbir.com">[navysbir.com+2Semantic Scholar]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navysbir.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atmospheric Aerosol Model and Data Collection Over the...OBJECTIVE: Develop a periscope imaging, electronic warfare, and High Energy Las...</span></span></span>

## How Waves Load the Air with Salt

Sea spray is produced primarily when waves break and entrain air beneath the surface. As bubbles rise and burst, they eject droplets into the [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}). Larger droplets fall back quickly, but many smaller particles remain suspended and become sea-salt aerosols. These aerosols are a major component of the marine atmosphere and can dominate light-scattering behaviour over the open ocean.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_spray" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2tos.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sea spray</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sea spray</span></span></span>

The concentration of these particles is not constant. Wind speed, wave state and whitecap formation all influence how much spray enters the air. Research has shown a clear dependence of marine aerosol concentrations on sea state and surface conditions, meaning that rougher seas generally create a denser aerosol environment above the water.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/phoc/47/1/jpo-d-16-0058.1.xml" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.ametsoc.org">[American Meteorological Society Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.ametsoc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Despite evidence of the importance of surface...Read more...</span></span></span>

For a naval laser, this matters because the beam does not encounter a single obstacle. Instead, it travels through a long path filled with countless particles of varying sizes. Each interaction may be small, but the cumulative effect can reduce transmitted energy and increase beam spreading before the beam reaches its target. Naval research programmes focused on high-energy laser propagation in the marine aerosol boundary layer specifically identify aerosol scattering, absorption and aerosol–turbulence interactions as critical operational concerns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navysbir.com/n23_A/N23A-T012.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navysbir.com">[navysbir.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navysbir.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atmospheric Aerosol Model and Data Collection Over the...OBJECTIVE: Develop a periscope imaging, electronic warfare, and High Energy Las...</span></span></span>

## Why Low Beam Paths Are Exposed

The lowest practical engagement paths are often the most attractive tactically. Anti-ship missiles, fast attack craft and many drones operate close to the sea surface, encouraging defenders to engage them along similarly low lines of sight. Unfortunately, these paths spend the greatest possible distance inside the marine boundary layer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navysbir.com/n23_A/N23A-T012.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navysbir.com">[navysbir.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navysbir.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atmospheric Aerosol Model and Data Collection Over the...OBJECTIVE: Develop a periscope imaging, electronic warfare, and High Energy Las...</span></span></span>

The marine boundary layer is not simply ordinary air above water. It contains elevated humidity, suspended salt particles, residual spray droplets and strong optical turbulence generated by heat exchange between ocean and atmosphere. The closer a beam remains to the surface, the longer it remains immersed in these conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navysbir.com/n23_A/N23A-T012.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navysbir.com">[navysbir.com+2Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navysbir.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atmospheric Aerosol Model and Data Collection Over the...OBJECTIVE: Develop a periscope imaging, electronic warfare, and High Energy Las...</span></span></span>

This creates an important geometric penalty. A laser aimed at a target only a few metres above the sea may spend nearly its entire propagation path inside the most contaminated part of the atmosphere. By contrast, a beam directed at a higher-altitude target can leave the near-surface layer more quickly and travel through cleaner air for much of its journey. The difference is one reason why maritime laser performance cannot be predicted solely from power output or laboratory testing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navysbir.com/n23_A/N23A-T012.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navysbir.com">[navysbir.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navysbir.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atmospheric Aerosol Model and Data Collection Over the...OBJECTIVE: Develop a periscope imaging, electronic warfare, and High Energy Las...</span></span></span>

## Why Salt Aerosols Matter Even When the Air Looks Clear

A common misconception is that laser degradation requires visible fog, mist or heavy spray. In reality, many of the particles affecting propagation are too small to be easily seen by the human eye. The atmosphere can appear clear while still containing large numbers of salt aerosols capable of altering optical transmission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tos.org/oceanography/article/climate-roles-of-non-sea-salt-sulfate-and-sea-spray-aerosol-in-the-atmospheric-marine-boundary-layer-highlights-of-40-years-of-pmel-research" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tos.org">[tos.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Climate Roles of Non-Sea Salt Sulfate and Sea Spray...30 Oct 2023 — Sea salt dominates aerosol mass and light scattering in the remote m...</span></span></span>

Sea-salt aerosols are especially important because they are highly hygroscopic, meaning they readily absorb moisture from the surrounding air. As relative humidity rises, these particles can grow in size through water uptake. Larger particles generally scatter more light and can increase optical losses compared with the same aerosol population in drier conditions. Atmospheric propagation models therefore treat marine aerosols as strongly dependent on humidity and weather conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_salt_aerosol" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sea salt aerosol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sea salt aerosol</span></span></span>

This means that a laser operating over the ocean may encounter a dynamic environment in which aerosol size and optical behaviour change over time, even if the number of particles remains similar. The result is a propagation channel that can degrade unexpectedly as humidity increases near the surface.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a00d/0f25b849c5790ac84f50f297a60ad57102f3.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pdfs.semanticscholar.org">[Semantic Scholar]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pdfs.semanticscholar.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Semantic ScholarLaser Beam Atmospheric Propagation Modelling for...by T Fahey · 2021 · Cited by 109 — Boundary layer aerosol models incl...</span></span></span>

## Humidity and Turbulence Near the Surface

Sea spray is only part of the challenge. The same processes that produce marine aerosols also contribute to a turbulent atmospheric environment. Heat, moisture and momentum exchange between ocean and atmosphere generate fluctuations in air density and refractive index. These fluctuations distort the wavefront of a laser beam as it propagates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.whoi.edu/science/aope/dept/r5.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whoi.edu">[Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whoi.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">spray droplets near the sea surface...Read more...</span></span></span>

For high-energy [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}), turbulence is often as important as direct scattering losses. A distorted beam can spread its energy over a larger spot on the target, reducing heating [efficiency]({{ 'efficiency/' | relative_url }}). Naval and defence researchers have therefore devoted significant effort to modelling the combined effects of marine aerosols and turbulence rather than treating them as separate problems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navysbir.com/n23_A/N23A-T012.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navysbir.com">[navysbir.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navysbir.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atmospheric Aerosol Model and Data Collection Over the...OBJECTIVE: Develop a periscope imaging, electronic warfare, and High Energy Las...</span></span></span>

The interaction between aerosols and turbulence is particularly important because both tend to increase near the ocean surface under windy conditions. Rough seas can simultaneously raise aerosol concentrations, elevate humidity and strengthen turbulent mixing. ## What This Means for Naval Laser Engagements

The practical lesson is not that naval lasers become unusable over the sea. Rather, the invisible spray environment reduces performance margins. A weapon that can achieve a desired effect at one range in clean air may require a shorter engagement distance, longer dwell time or more favourable geometry when operating through dense marine aerosols.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navysbir.com/n23_A/N23A-T012.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navysbir.com">[navysbir.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navysbir.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atmospheric Aerosol Model and Data Collection Over the...OBJECTIVE: Develop a periscope imaging, electronic warfare, and High Energy Las...</span></span></span>

Because sea spray concentrations vary with wind, wave state and humidity, the challenge is also highly variable. Two apparently similar days at sea can present very different optical conditions. This is why naval programmes increasingly incorporate marine boundary-layer measurements, atmospheric modelling and [adaptive optics]({{ 'adaptive-optics/' | relative_url }}) into laser system development. The goal is not merely to generate more power, but to preserve beam quality while propagating through the invisible mixture of salt, moisture and turbulence that exists just above the ocean surface.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navysbir.com/n23_A/N23A-T012.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navysbir.com">[navysbir.com+2mlml.sjsu.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navysbir.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atmospheric Aerosol Model and Data Collection Over the...OBJECTIVE: Develop a periscope imaging, electronic warfare, and High Energy Las...</span></span></span>
