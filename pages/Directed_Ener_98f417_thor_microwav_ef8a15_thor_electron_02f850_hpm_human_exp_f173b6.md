## Introduction

High-power microwave (HPM) weapons such as THOR are designed to disable electronics, not to engage people. Nevertheless, the electromagnetic fields that make these systems effective against drones can also present hazards to personnel if they enter areas where field strengths exceed established exposure limits. For this reason, military operators treat human safety as a separate planning problem alongside protection of friendly electronics. The practical question is not simply whether a microwave weapon can defeat a [drone swarm]({{ 'swarm-limits/' | relative_url }}), but how to ensure that operators, [maintenance]({{ 'maintenance/' | relative_url }}) personnel, airfield workers and other nearby personnel remain outside hazardous exposure zones whenever the system is active. Public information about THOR's exact operating parameters remains limited, but the safety framework used around high-power microwave systems is shaped by well-established radiofrequency exposure standards, military force-health-protection rules and operational exclusion-zone procedures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://afresearchlab.com/counter-swarm-high-power-weapon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afresearchlab.com">[Air Force Research Laboratory+2GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afresearchlab.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It operates from a wall plug and uses energy to disable drones. ## Why Personnel Exclusion Zones Matter

High-power microwave weapons generate intense electromagnetic pulses intended to couple energy into electronic circuits. While humans are not affected in the same way as electronic systems, sufficiently strong radiofrequency fields can deposit energy in biological tissue and cause heating. Modern exposure standards are therefore primarily based on preventing excessive temperature increases in the body and in local tissues.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.icnirp.org/cms/upload/publications/ICNIRPrfgdl2020.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Operational procedures">[ICNIRP+2MTT-S]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Operational procedures</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on icnirp.org.</span></span></span>

The challenge for military planners is that the field strength produced by an HPM weapon varies dramatically with distance, direction and antenna geometry. The area directly in front of an operating system is typically the most tightly controlled because that is where energy is intentionally concentrated. Personnel exclusion zones ensure that nobody is present in locations where exposure could exceed authorised [limits]({{ 'limits/' | relative_url }}) during firing or testing operations. These zones also account for uncertainties such as [reflections]({{ 'reflections/' | relative_url }}) from nearby structures, vehicles or aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/63d7a5d3d3bf7f25213352e8/JSP_392_Chp_35_web_version.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK+2MTT-S]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GOV.UK35 Electromagnetic Fields 0 Hz – 300 GHz (Including...January 27, 2023 — The limits used to demonstrate compliance with the legisl...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 27, 2023</span></span></span>

Unlike many conventional hazards, electromagnetic exposure may not provide immediate sensory warning. A worker cannot reliably judge field strength simply by standing near a system. Consequently, safety procedures rely on [engineering]({{ 'engineering/' | relative_url }}) controls, signage, restricted-access areas and formal operating rules rather than individual judgement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/63d7a5d3d3bf7f25213352e8/JSP_392_Chp_35_web_version.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GOV.UK35 Electromagnetic Fields 0 Hz – 300 GHz (Including...January 27, 2023 — The limits used to demonstrate compliance with the legisl...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 27, 2023</span></span></span>

## Who May Be Exposed During Base Defence

A system such as [THOR]({{ 'thor/' | relative_url }}) is intended for airbase defence, which means it may operate in environments containing many categories of personnel. These include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Weapon operators and maintenance crews.
* Airfield personnel working near defended facilities.
* Communications and radar technicians.
* Security forces assigned to perimeter defence.
* Aircrew and ground crews supporting flight operations.
* Contractors and civilian personnel working on the installation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://afresearchlab.com/counter-swarm-high-power-weapon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afresearchlab.com">[Air Force Research Laboratory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afresearchlab.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It operates from a wall plug and uses energy to disable drones. In practice, the highest concern is usually not the general population outside a military installation but authorised personnel working within the operating area. Military safety programmes therefore distinguish between unrestricted environments, where the general public may be present, and restricted environments, where trained personnel understand the hazard and operate under controlled procedures. Exposure limits and administrative controls often differ between these categories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kolegite.com/EE_library/standards/IEEE%20Standards%20for%20Safety%20Levels%200%20to%20300%20GHz.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kolegite.com">[Kolegite]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kolegite.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">IEEE Standards for Safety Levels 0 to 300 GHz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">IEEE Standards for Safety Levels 0 to 300 GHz.pdf4 Oct 2019 — These exposure limits are intended to apply generally to persons pe...</span></span></span>

Another consideration is the presence of people with implanted or body-worn medical devices. Electromagnetic safety standards acknowledge that general exposure limits are not necessarily sufficient to guarantee protection of all implants or specialised medical equipment. ## How Exposure Standards Guide Operating Procedures

The most influential exposure frameworks for radiofrequency energy are produced by the International Commission on Non-Ionizing Radiation Protection (ICNIRP) and by IEEE standards bodies. These documents establish limits across frequencies ranging from kilohertz to hundreds of gigahertz and are widely used as the basis for civilian and military safety programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.icnirp.org/cms/upload/publications/ICNIRPrfgdl2020.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Operational procedures">[ICNIRP+2MTT-S]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Operational procedures</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on icnirp.org.</span></span></span>

Modern standards focus on measurable biological effects rather than the presence of electromagnetic energy alone. Key concepts include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Whole-body exposure limits**, intended to prevent excessive heating of the body as a whole.
* **Local exposure limits**, intended to prevent excessive heating in specific tissues.
* **Power density and absorbed energy density limits**, particularly important at higher frequencies and for short-duration pulses.
* **Restricted versus unrestricted exposure categories**, reflecting different assumptions about training and hazard awareness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.icnirp.org/cms/upload/publications/ICNIRPrfgdl2020.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Operational procedures">[ICNIRP+2arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Operational procedures</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on icnirp.org.</span></span></span>

</div>

For military systems, compliance is typically demonstrated through modelling, measurement and operational planning. Rather than continuously measuring personnel exposure during combat operations, planners determine safe distances and operating geometries in advance. These calculations become part of approved operating procedures and training requirements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/63d7a5d3d3bf7f25213352e8/JSP_392_Chp_35_web_version.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GOV.UK35 Electromagnetic Fields 0 Hz – 300 GHz (Including...January 27, 2023 — The limits used to demonstrate compliance with the legisl...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 27, 2023</span></span></span>

## How Close Is Too Close to THOR?

Publicly available information does not disclose the detailed safety envelope, exposure limits or exclusion distances associated with THOR. That information is operationally sensitive and depends on factors such as power output, pulse characteristics, antenna gain and local environmental conditions. As a result, no reliable public source provides a universal "safe distance" for THOR itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://afresearchlab.com/counter-swarm-high-power-weapon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afresearchlab.com">[Air Force Research Laboratory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afresearchlab.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It operates from a wall plug and uses energy to disable drones. THOR, a first of its kind system...</span></span></span>

What can be said is that military microwave systems are not normally operated as if people can freely move around the transmitter while it is active. Instead, exclusion zones are calculated using recognised exposure standards and are enforced through procedures similar to those used around powerful radar installations and other high-energy radiofrequency emitters. Personnel are positioned outside designated hazard areas before operation begins, and access controls are maintained while the system is energised.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/63d7a5d3d3bf7f25213352e8/JSP_392_Chp_35_web_version.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK+2MTT-S]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GOV.UK35 Electromagnetic Fields 0 Hz – 300 GHz (Including...January 27, 2023 — The limits used to demonstrate compliance with the legisl...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 27, 2023</span></span></span>

The exact boundary depends on the specific system. A compact test setup, a shipboard microwave system and a [counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) weapon may all require different safety distances even if they operate within the same broad frequency range. Consequently, responsible operators do not rely on generic rules of thumb; they rely on system-specific hazard analyses approved by safety authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/63d7a5d3d3bf7f25213352e8/JSP_392_Chp_35_web_version.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GOV.UK35 Electromagnetic Fields 0 Hz – 300 GHz (Including...January 27, 2023 — The limits used to demonstrate compliance with the legisl...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 27, 2023</span></span></span>

## What Is Known About Human Health Effects?

Public discussion of microwave weapons often blurs the distinction between documented exposure effects and speculative claims. Established radiofrequency safety standards are built around a large body of research indicating that excessive exposure can cause thermal effects—heating of tissue—which is the principal hazard addressed by current limits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.icnirp.org/cms/upload/publications/ICNIRPrfgdl2020.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Operational procedures">[ICNIRP+2arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Operational procedures</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on icnirp.org.</span></span></span>

Researchers continue to debate aspects of radiofrequency health policy, including how conservative exposure limits should be and how uncertainty should be managed. However, mainstream international standards bodies and military safety programmes generally use thermal mechanisms as the basis for operational exposure limits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1619781/full" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: frontiersin.org">[Frontiers+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">frontiersin.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Frontiers Health and safety practices and policies concerning human</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FrontiersHealth and safety practices and policies concerning human...July 21, 2025 — by JC Lin · 2025 · Cited by 14 — This paper: (1) co...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 21, 2025</span></span></span>

For high-power microwave weapons specifically, the practical implication is straightforward: operators are expected to keep personnel outside calculated hazard zones rather than relying on assumptions that brief exposure is harmless. The existence of exclusion areas, controlled access procedures and force-health-protection standards reflects the recognition that human exposure must be actively managed whenever very powerful electromagnetic systems are employed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/63d7a5d3d3bf7f25213352e8/JSP_392_Chp_35_web_version.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK+2MTT-S]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GOV.UK35 Electromagnetic Fields 0 Hz – 300 GHz (Including...January 27, 2023 — The limits used to demonstrate compliance with the legisl...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 27, 2023</span></span></span>

## The Governance Logic Behind Human Safety Rules

The most important point is that human-safety governance around HPM weapons is preventative rather than reactive. Safety programmes are designed so that personnel never approach exposure thresholds in normal operations. This approach mirrors practices used for high-power radar systems, satellite uplinks and other intense radiofrequency sources.

For a system such as THOR, successful deployment therefore depends on three layers working together:

1. **Technical design**, which controls where energy is directed.
2. **Exposure standards**, which define acceptable limits for personnel.
