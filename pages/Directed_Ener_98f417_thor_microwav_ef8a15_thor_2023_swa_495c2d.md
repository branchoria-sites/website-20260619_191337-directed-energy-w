## Introduction

The Air Force Research Laboratory’s April 2023 THOR demonstration at Kirtland Air Force Base was presented as a major step forward for microwave [counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) technology. The test matters because it moved beyond earlier demonstrations and attempted to simulate a more realistic drone-swarm attack, using multiple targets and a larger-scale engagement than AFRL had previously disclosed. Yet the event did not prove that microwave weapons have solved the [swarm problem]({{ 'swarm-problem/' | relative_url }}). What it demonstrated was narrower but still significant: THOR could detect, track and disrupt a group of drones under controlled test conditions. Key operational questions—including range, effectiveness against diverse drone types, reliability under combat conditions and performance against sophisticated swarms—remain unanswered in public sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afrl.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/3396995/afrl-conducts-swarm-technology-demonstration/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afrl.af.mil">[Air Force Research Laboratory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afrl.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">This was the first test of this scale in AFRL history.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Research LaboratoryAFRL conducts swarm technology demonstrationMay 16, 2023 — 16 May 2023 — AFRL completed a successful demonst...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 16, 2023</span></span></span>

## The Chestnut Test Site Demonstration

On 5 April 2023, AFRL conducted a [THOR]({{ 'thor/' | relative_url }}) test at the Chestnut Test Site on Kirtland Air Force Base in New Mexico. According to AFRL, the event simulated a “real-world swarm attack” and represented the first test of this scale in the laboratory’s history. The demonstration involved THOR engaging multiple airborne targets rather than a single drone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afrl.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/3396995/afrl-conducts-swarm-technology-demonstration/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afrl.af.mil">[Air Force Research Laboratory+2DSIAC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afrl.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">This was the first test of this scale in AFRL history.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Research LaboratoryAFRL conducts swarm technology demonstrationMay 16, 2023 — 16 May 2023 — AFRL completed a successful demonst...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 16, 2023</span></span></span>

That distinction is important. THOR was never designed primarily as a one-on-one anti-drone system. Its purpose is to use high-power microwave energy to affect several drones within an engagement area. The April 2023 event therefore tested the central concept behind the programme: whether a microwave weapon could deal with multiple small unmanned aircraft quickly enough to remain useful against a swarm.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://afresearchlab.com/counter-swarm-high-power-weapon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afresearchlab.com">[Air Force Research Laboratory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afresearchlab.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It operates from a wall plug and uses energy to disable drones. AFRL photographs and official descriptions show the system operating against numerous airborne targets while being manually aimed and tracked. Public reporting also emphasised that the drones used were not identical to those from earlier demonstrations, suggesting some effort to move beyond a narrowly scripted test configuration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.airandspaceforces.com/air-forces-thor-drone-swarm-demo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: airandspaceforces.com">[Air &amp; Space Forces Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">airandspaceforces.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">air forces thor drone swarm demo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This was the first test of this scale in AFRL history.Read more...</span></span></span>

AFRL personnel highlighted the system’s wide microwave beam, high peak power and gimballed tracking capability as factors contributing to the outcome. ## What the Test Actually Proved

The strongest conclusion supported by the public evidence is that THOR demonstrated a credible “one-to-many” engagement capability. Unlike many laser systems, which generally must dwell on one target at a time, THOR appears capable of affecting multiple drones within its engagement sector during a short time window. The 2023 event provided the clearest public indication yet that the concept works outside a laboratory bench test. The demonstration also showed progress in system integration. A microwave source by itself is not a usable weapon. A practical system must detect targets, track them, point accurately and coordinate the microwave effect. Just as importantly, the test suggested that AFRL was confident enough in THOR's maturity to conduct a larger public demonstration rather than relying solely on controlled laboratory experiments. ## The Missing Details That Limit Conclusions

The most important feature of the 2023 demonstration is not what AFRL revealed but what it did not reveal.

No public source disclosed the number of drones involved. AFRL described the engagement as a swarm attack and referred to multiple targets, but did not provide target counts. Without those figures, observers cannot determine whether the scenario represented a small cluster of drones or a large, [saturation]({{ 'saturation/' | relative_url }})-style swarm. * The engagement range.
* The microwave frequency or waveform.
* The percentage of drones successfully disabled.
* Whether any targets survived the engagement.
* The duration of the attack scenario.
* The resilience of the drones against electromagnetic attack.
* The extent of any [collateral]({{ 'collateral-risk/' | relative_url }}) electromagnetic effects. Commercial quadcopters, custom-built drones and military unmanned systems may respond very differently to microwave energy. A demonstration can therefore prove effectiveness against one class of targets without proving effectiveness against all likely threats.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://afresearchlab.com/counter-swarm-high-power-weapon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afresearchlab.com">[Air Force Research Laboratory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afresearchlab.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It operates from a wall plug and uses energy to disable drones. They rarely include the full set of battlefield complications such as terrain masking, electronic warfare, mixed drone types, simultaneous attacks from multiple directions, or pressure to avoid disrupting friendly electronics. None of those factors can be assessed from the information AFRL released.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afrl.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/3396995/afrl-conducts-swarm-technology-demonstration/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afrl.af.mil">[Air Force Research Laboratory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afrl.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">This was the first test of this scale in AFRL history.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Research LaboratoryAFRL conducts swarm technology demonstrationMay 16, 2023 — 16 May 2023 — AFRL completed a successful demonst...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 16, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why the Demonstration Still Matters

Despite those gaps, the 2023 test remains one of the most significant public microwave counter-drone demonstrations conducted by the United States.

The value of the event lies less in the exact number of drones disabled and more in what it says about the maturity of the concept. AFRL demonstrated that a transportable high-power microwave system could be brought to a [test range]({{ 'test-range/' | relative_url }}), aimed at an airborne swarm scenario and produce effects that the laboratory considered operationally meaningful. That is a stronger claim than merely showing a microwave pulse disabling a single drone in a controlled experiment. Air Force Research Laboratory+2Air Force Research Laboratory<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afrl.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/3396995/afrl-conducts-swarm-technology-demonstration/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afrl.af.mil">[afrl.af.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afrl.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">This was the first test of this scale in AFRL history.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Research LaboratoryAFRL conducts swarm technology demonstrationMay 16, 2023 — 16 May 2023 — AFRL completed a successful demonst...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 16, 2023</span></span></span>

The demonstration also helped explain why the US military continues to invest in follow-on microwave programmes. If future conflicts involve large numbers of inexpensive drones, a weapon that can potentially affect several targets at once offers a fundamentally different defensive approach from missiles or guns. The 2023 THOR event did not prove that such systems are ready to replace conventional air defences, but it did provide evidence that the underlying idea deserves continued development. ## What Did the THOR Swarm Test Prove?

The April 2023 Chestnut Test Site demonstration proved that THOR could engage a realistic multi-drone scenario and that AFRL's high-power microwave approach remained effective enough to justify further investment. It demonstrated technical progress in tracking, aiming and countering multiple airborne targets in a single event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afrl.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/3396995/afrl-conducts-swarm-technology-demonstration/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afrl.af.mil">[Air Force Research Laboratory+2DSIAC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afrl.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">This was the first test of this scale in AFRL history.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Research LaboratoryAFRL conducts swarm technology demonstrationMay 16, 2023 — 16 May 2023 — AFRL completed a successful demonst...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 16, 2023</span></span></span>
