## Introduction

DragonFire’s most persuasive military case is not that it can replace missiles, but that it can change the economics of defending against drones. Modern armed forces increasingly face unmanned aircraft that are cheap to build, can be launched in large numbers, and often force defenders to expend far more expensive interceptor missiles. Recent UK trials suggest DragonFire is moving beyond laboratory demonstrations into a practical [counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) role, including the defeat of [high-speed]({{ '650-km-h/' | relative_url }}) aerial targets. The significance lies not only in whether the laser can destroy drones, but whether it can do so repeatedly, rapidly and at a cost that preserves scarce missile inventories for more demanding threats.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/boost-for-armed-forces-as-new-laser-weapon-takes-down-high-speed-drones" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Boost for Armed Forces as new laser weapon takes down...20 Nov 2025 — DragonFire achieves UK first in latest testing, taking down high-s...</span></span></span>

## Can DragonFire Change Drone Defence?

The answer increasingly appears to be yes—within a specific mission set. The Royal Navy and wider UK defence establishment are positioning DragonFire as a defensive layer against aerial threats where conventional missile use can become economically and operationally inefficient. Recent government announcements linked successful high-speed drone trials directly to a £316 million production contract and accelerated naval [deployment]({{ 'deployment/' | relative_url }}) plans.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/boost-for-armed-forces-as-new-laser-weapon-takes-down-high-speed-drones" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK+2Royal Navy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Boost for Armed Forces as new laser weapon takes down...20 Nov 2025 — DragonFire achieves UK first in latest testing, taking down high-s...</span></span></span>

The attraction is straightforward. A missile magazine contains a fixed number of interceptors that can be exhausted during prolonged attacks. A laser draws electrical power instead of consuming a stored missile. As long as a ship can generate power and manage [cooling]({{ 'cooling/' | relative_url }}) requirements, it can potentially engage many more targets than a traditional missile-only defence system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/uk/britain-equip-ships-with-lasers-take-down-drones-2027-2024-04-11/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Britain to equip ships with lasers to take down drones from</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Britain to equip ships with lasers to take down drones from...April 12, 2024 — 11 Apr 2024 — Britain&#x27;s defence ministry on Friday...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why Drones Are the Natural First Target Set

Drones expose one of the most uncomfortable realities of modern air defence: the attacker often spends far less money than the defender.

Many unmanned aircraft used in contemporary conflicts cost a fraction of the interceptor missiles launched against them. Even when a missile successfully destroys a drone, the exchange can favour the attacker if enough inexpensive aircraft are launched. This challenge is particularly acute for naval forces, which carry limited numbers of air-defence missiles and may operate far from resupply.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/uk/uk-beefs-up-royal-navy-counter-drone-tech-with-413-mln-laser-contract-2025-11-20/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This initiative is part of Britain&#x27;s efforts to enhance its naval defenses, particularly against drone threats. ## What the High-Speed Drone Trials Suggest

The most important recent DragonFire evidence is not merely that it hit drones, but that it reportedly defeated fast-moving ones.

In November 2025, the UK government announced that DragonFire had taken down high-speed drones during testing at the Hebrides range. The Ministry of Defence described the trials as achieving a UK first, including above-the-[horizon tracking]({{ 'horizon-tracking/' | relative_url }}), targeting and destruction of drones travelling at up to 650 km/h. The success of those trials was a key factor behind the decision to move toward operational naval deployment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://des.mod.uk/boost-for-armed-forces-as-new-laser-weapon-takes-down-high-speed-drones/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: des.mod.uk">[Defence Equipment &amp; Support+2GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">des.mod.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">boost for armed forces as new laser weapon takes down high speed drones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defence Equipment &amp; SupportBoost for Armed Forces as new laser weapon takes down...20 Nov 2025 — Britain&#x27;s ground-breaking DragonFire la...</span></span></span>

## The Real Value: Preserving Missile Stocks

The strongest argument for DragonFire is often misunderstood. Its value is not simply the low cost of a single shot; it is the ability to reserve expensive missiles for threats that genuinely require them.

A naval commander confronting a mixed attack may face reconnaissance drones, one-way attack drones, cruise missiles and other aerial threats simultaneously. Using a sophisticated missile against every target can quickly deplete available interceptors. A laser creates another option within the defensive architecture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/uk/britain-equip-ships-with-lasers-take-down-drones-2027-2024-04-11/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Britain to equip ships with lasers to take down drones from</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Britain to equip ships with lasers to take down drones from...April 12, 2024 — 11 Apr 2024 — Britain&#x27;s defence ministry on Friday...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2024</span></span></span>

In practical terms, the ideal engagement sequence might look like this:

* Laser engages slower or more vulnerable drones.

## Where Laser Defence Still Needs Backup Weapons

The recent drone defeats are significant, but they do not remove the limitations that have accompanied laser weapons for decades.

DragonFire remains a [line-of-sight]({{ 'dwell-time/' | relative_url }}) system. It can only engage targets that its sensors and beam director can see. Atmospheric conditions such as rain, fog, spray and turbulence can reduce laser effectiveness. Targets may also require different amounts of energy depending on size, construction and flight profile.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DragonFire_%28weapon%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dragon Fire (weapon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dragon Fire (weapon</span></span></span>
