## Introduction

A laser can be the cheapest shot in an air-defence system, but the cheapest shot is not always the best shot. Directed-energy weapons are attractive because they can engage targets at the speed of light and may cost only a few pounds or dollars in electrical power per engagement. Yet commanders are not trying to minimise the cost of a shot; they are trying to maximise the probability of stopping a threat before it causes harm. When range, weather, target hardness, reaction time or the consequences of failure become critical, missiles and guns often remain the preferred option. Modern air defence therefore treats [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}) as an additional layer rather than a universal replacement for kinetic weapons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2RAND Corporation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy WeaponsMay 25, 2023 — Directed energy weapons—such as lasers—use energy fired at the speed o...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 25, 2023</span></span></span>

The practical question is not whether a laser can destroy a target. It is when a laser should be trusted with the engagement and when commanders should spend a missile instead.

## Why Cheap Shots Are Not Always Good Shots

The strongest argument for lasers is the cost-exchange ratio. Systems such as the UK's DragonFire have been promoted as costing roughly £10 per shot, dramatically less than many air-defence missiles. That makes lasers especially attractive against inexpensive drones and other mass threats.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.forcesnews.com/technology/weapons-and-kit/dragonfire-cutting-edge-laser-air-and-sea-weapon-achieves-mod-testing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: forcesnews.com">[Forces News+2Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">forcesnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forces NewsDragonFire: £10-a-shot laser weapon achieves UK first19 Jan 2024 — Its operating cost is typically less than £10 per shot...</span></span></span>

However, cost is only one variable in a defensive decision. A missile usually carries its own destructive mechanism to the target and can engage at longer distances. A laser must maintain sufficient energy on a vulnerable point for long enough to produce damage. If atmospheric conditions weaken the beam, if the target manoeuvres, or if the engagement window is very short, the economic advantage becomes less important than the certainty of a successful intercept.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2National Defense Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy WeaponsMay 25, 2023 — Directed energy weapons—such as lasers—use energy fired at the speed o...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 25, 2023</span></span></span>

This creates a simple rule used in many layered-defence concepts: low-cost threats with favourable engagement conditions are candidates for laser engagement, while high-consequence threats are more likely to justify a missile regardless of cost. A commander who loses a missile but saves a defended ship, base or city has achieved the mission. A commander who saves money but allows a threat through has not.

## Targets That Still Favour Missiles and Guns

Several categories of targets remain difficult cases for laser systems and often justify kinetic engagement.

**Threats at long range.**<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-105868" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[gao.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 105868</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Directed Energy Weapons: DOD Should Focus on...Apr 17, 2023 — The Department of Defense (DOD) is currently developing directed energy we...</span></span></span>

Missiles can engage threats far from the defended asset, creating more opportunities for a successful intercept and allowing additional defensive layers if the first attempt fails. Lasers are fundamentally [line-of-sight]({{ 'dwell-time/' | relative_url }}) systems and generally operate most effectively at shorter ranges.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aspistrategist.org.au/war-at-the-speed-of-light-the-emerging-role-of-directed-energy-weapons/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aspistrategist.org.au">[The Strategist]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aspistrategist.org.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">war at the speed of light the emerging role of directed energy weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Strategistthe emerging role of directed-energy weapons15 Apr 2026 — directed-energy weapons are short-range, line-of-sight weapons an...</span></span></span>

**Threats in poor weather.**

Rain, fog, cloud, dust and atmospheric turbulence can reduce beam effectiveness by scattering or distorting laser energy. In such conditions, a missile may provide a more predictable outcome. Weather dependence is one of the most frequently cited operational limitations of high-energy lasers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaldefensemagazine.org/articles/2025/8/11/government-perspective-directed-energy-in-air-base-defense-can-save-the-arsenal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaldefensemagazine.org">[National Defense Magazine+2RealClearDefense]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaldefensemagazine.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Defense MagazineDirected Energy in Air Base Defense Can Save the ArsenalAug 11, 2025 — Weather and atmospheric conditions can li...</span></span></span>

**High-speed, high-consequence targets.**<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.onr.navy.mil/organization/departments/code-35/division-353/directed-energy-weapons-cdew-and-high-energy-lasers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onr.navy.mil">[onr.navy.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onr.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The intent is to increase accuracy in all weather conditions against high speed...Read more...</span></span></span>

Even though modern laser programmes have demonstrated engagements against fast drones, some threats leave little room for uncertainty. [Cruise missiles]({{ 'cruise-missiles/' | relative_url }}), anti-ship missiles and other high-value threats may justify missile interception because commanders want the highest achievable confidence and often prefer to engage them as far away as possible. Directed-energy advocates themselves generally describe lasers as complements to existing missile defences rather than replacements for them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://csbaonline.org/uploads/documents/CSBA_ChangingTheGame_ereader.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csbaonline.org">[CSBA+2RAND Corporation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csbaonline.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">anti-air warfare defensive systems could engage an incoming cruise missile while it is...Read more...</span></span></span>

**Large raids from multiple directions.**

A laser may shift rapidly between targets, but power generation, cooling and dwell-time requirements still impose limits. When numerous threats arrive simultaneously from different directions and altitudes, commanders may need a mix of interceptors, guns, electronic warfare and directed energy rather than relying on a single engagement method.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaldefensemagazine.org/articles/2025/8/11/government-perspective-directed-energy-in-air-base-defense-can-save-the-arsenal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaldefensemagazine.org">[National Defense Magazine+2Laser Wars]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaldefensemagazine.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Defense MagazineDirected Energy in Air Base Defense Can Save the ArsenalAug 11, 2025 — Weather and atmospheric conditions can li...</span></span></span>

**Targets where failure is unacceptable.**

The value of the defended asset matters. A drone approaching a remote training area may be an ideal laser target. A missile heading toward a warship, command centre or critical infrastructure may justify expending an expensive interceptor because the consequences of a leak-through are so severe.

## How Commanders Sequence Layers Under Pressure

Real-world air defence is usually a sequence problem rather than a weapon-selection problem. Commanders rarely choose between a laser and a missile in isolation. They choose the order in which defensive layers are employed.

A common logic is:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Detect and classify the threat.**
2. **Determine the threat's danger and time-to-impact.**
3. **Use the least costly option that still provides acceptable confidence.**
4. **Retain higher-end interceptors for threats that genuinely require them.**

</div>

This means a small reconnaissance drone with a clear line of sight may receive a laser engagement first. A sophisticated cruise missile approaching through difficult weather may move immediately into the missile-engagement category. A final inner layer of guns may remain available if earlier attempts fail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2Drone Warfare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy WeaponsMay 25, 2023 — Directed energy weapons—such as lasers—use energy fired at the speed o...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 25, 2023</span></span></span>

The approach mirrors the logic behind current laser deployments. The Royal Navy's DragonFire programme is being integrated alongside existing Sea Viper and Sea Ceptor missile systems rather than replacing them. ## The Real Measure of a Good Shot

The most mature view of directed energy treats lasers as a resource-management tool inside a broader defensive architecture. They are valuable because they can preserve scarce missile inventories, reduce engagement costs and strengthen point defence against drones and similar threats. Yet the very existence of layered air defence reflects a long-standing military reality: different threats demand different weapons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rand.org/pubs/commentary/2024/01/directed-energy-the-focus-on-laser-weapons-intensifies.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rand.org">[RAND Corporation+2GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rand.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">If the technology can be matured,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RAND CorporationDirected Energy: The Focus on Laser Weapons IntensifiesJan 25, 2024 — Directed energy weapons offer a more cost-efficient...</span></span></span>

A laser becomes the wrong shot when weather degrades the beam, when engagement range is too short, when the target is too dangerous to risk a marginal intercept, or when commanders need the greater certainty provided by a missile or gun.
