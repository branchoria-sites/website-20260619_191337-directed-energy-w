## Introduction

The famous claim that DragonFire can fire for “less than £10 per shot” is real, but it describes only a very specific part of the weapon’s economics. The figure refers primarily to the electrical energy consumed during a firing event, not to the cost of buying, integrating, maintaining, crewing, [cooling]({{ 'cooling/' | relative_url }}), upgrading, or supporting the system throughout its service life. UK defence officials have compared a DragonFire engagement to running a household heater for about an hour, emphasising that the number represents operating energy rather than total capability cost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/advanced-future-military-laser-achieves-uk-first" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">advanced future military laser achieves uk first</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">cost alternative to certain tasks missiles currently carry out. Understanding this distinction is essential when evaluating claims about directed-energy weapons. A £10 laser shot may be extraordinarily cheap compared with a missile launch, but that does not mean DragonFire itself is a £10 weapon.

## Energy Cost Versus Total Ownership Cost

The Ministry of Defence has consistently described DragonFire’s firing cost as typically below £10 per shot. In official explanations, the cost is tied to electricity consumption during the engagement. A ten-second firing sequence reportedly uses energy worth roughly the same as operating a domestic electric heater for an hour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/advanced-future-military-laser-achieves-uk-first" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">advanced future military laser achieves uk first</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">cost alternative to certain tasks missiles currently carry out. The cost of operating the laser is typically less than £10 per shot. Drag...</span></span></span>

That figure excludes a wide range of costs that military planners must still pay for:

* Research and development.
* Laser [generation]({{ 'generation-ii/' | relative_url }}) equipment.
* Precision optics and beam-control systems.
* Sensors and target-tracking hardware.
* Cooling and power-conditioning equipment.
* [Ship integration]({{ 'ship-integration/' | relative_url }}) and combat-system software.
* Maintenance, repairs, testing and upgrades.
* Crew training and operational support.

The distinction is similar to the difference between the cost of petrol burned during a single car journey and the total cost of owning the vehicle. The fuel expense is real, but it does not capture the purchase price, servicing, insurance, infrastructure, or depreciation.

DragonFire illustrates this gap clearly. In 2025, the UK awarded a £316 million contract to MBDA UK to deliver operational systems for Royal Navy service beginning in 2027. That procurement spending exists separately from years of earlier development and testing work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/uk/uk-beefs-up-royal-navy-counter-drone-tech-with-413-mln-laser-contract-2025-11-20/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Tom&#x27;s Hardware+3Reuters+3GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK beefs up Royal Navy counter-drone tech with $413 million laser contract</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This initiative is part of Britain&#x27;s efforts to enhance its naval defenses, particularly against drone threats. ## Why Laser Shots Differ From Missile Launches

The £10 figure becomes meaningful when compared with conventional air-defence weapons.

A missile engagement consumes a physical interceptor. Once launched, that missile is gone and must be replaced from stockpiles. Depending on the system, an interceptor may cost hundreds of thousands or even more than a million pounds. Royal Navy materials have specifically contrasted DragonFire’s approximate £10 firing cost with Sea Viper missile engagements costing well over £1 million each.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/news/2025/november/20/20251120-dragonfire-trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royalnavy.mod.uk">[Royal Navy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royalnavy.mod.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">20251120 dragonfire trials</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Navy£316m deal for Royal Navy&#x27;s first laser weapon after...20 Nov 2025 — DragonFire offers a cheaper (£10 per shot compared with...</span></span></span>

A laser engagement works differently. The weapon does not expend a missile body, rocket motor, guidance package or warhead. Instead, it converts stored electrical power into a focused beam. As long as sufficient electrical power and cooling capacity are available, the system can continue engaging targets without consuming expensive interceptors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/advanced-future-military-laser-achieves-uk-first" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">advanced future military laser achieves uk first</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">cost alternative to certain tasks missiles currently carry out. The cost of operating the laser is typically less than £10 per shot. Drag...</span></span></span>

This economic model is particularly attractive against:

* Small drones.
* Large drone [swarms]({{ 'swarms/' | relative_url }}).
* Cheap one-way attack systems.
* Repeated harassment attacks intended to drain missile inventories.

In those scenarios, the military problem is often not whether a target can be destroyed, but whether the defender can afford to keep destroying large numbers of inexpensive threats. DragonFire’s low marginal firing cost is intended to address that imbalance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/boost-for-armed-forces-as-new-laser-weapon-takes-down-high-speed-drones" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK+2Navy Lookout]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">boost for armed forces as new laser weapon takes down high speed drones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Boost for Armed Forces as new laser weapon takes down...Nov 20, 2025 — Britain&#x27;s ground-breaking DragonFire laser has shot down high-spe...</span></span></span>

## What the £10 Number Leaves Out

The headline figure can create an impression that the entire engagement costs only £10. In reality, several hidden elements sit behind every shot.

### Power Generation and Cooling

High-energy [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}) require substantial electrical power and generate significant waste heat. Naval installations must provide power-management systems, energy storage, cooling equipment and supporting infrastructure. Analysts have noted that advanced laser systems may draw hundreds of kilowatts and that heat management becomes a major [engineering]({{ 'engineering/' | relative_url }}) challenge because laser conversion is not perfectly efficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navylookout.com/dragonfire-directed-energy-weapon-to-be-fitted-to-four-royal-navy-warships-by-2027/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navylookout.com">[Navy Lookout]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navylookout.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Navy LookoutDragonFire directed energy weapon to be fitted to four...March 26, 2025 — 26 Mar 2025 — The smallest prototype laser weapon...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2025</span></span></span>

Those systems continue to incur costs whether or not the laser is firing.

### Sensor and Tracking Systems

The destructive beam is only one component of DragonFire. The system also depends on electro-optical sensors, tracking lasers, stabilised mounts, targeting software and fire-control equipment. Hitting a fast-moving drone requires finding, identifying, tracking and maintaining precise aim before energy can be delivered to the target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uk.leonardo.com/en/innovation/dragonfire" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uk.leonardo.com">[Leonardo UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uk.leonardo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UKDragon Fire – Laser Directed Energy Weapon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Leonardo UKDragonFire – Laser Directed Energy Weapon - Leonardo UKThe DragonFire laser directed energy weapon (LDEW) system achieved the...</span></span></span>

The electricity consumed by the laser beam itself is only a fraction of the broader capability.

### Availability and Sustainment

Military systems require spare parts, technical support, software updates, calibration and [maintenance]({{ 'maintenance/' | relative_url }}). These costs accumulate over decades of service. A laser may avoid spending missiles, but it does not eliminate sustainment expenses.

Consequently, the true cost of an operational DragonFire engagement is higher than the electricity bill alone, even though the marginal energy component remains remarkably low.

## How the Headline Number Can Mislead Readers

The phrase “£10 per shot” is accurate within the narrow definition used by the Ministry of Defence. Problems arise when readers unconsciously interpret it as a complete cost figure.

Three different questions are often merged together:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **What does one additional firing event cost in electricity?**
2. **What does it cost to operate and sustain the system over time?**
3. **What does it cost to acquire the capability in the first place?**

</div>

The £10 figure answers only the first question.
