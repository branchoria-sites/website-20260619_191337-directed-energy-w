## Introduction

The strongest economic argument for laser weapons such as DragonFire is not that they replace missiles, but that they prevent missiles from being wasted on targets that do not justify their cost. Modern air defence increasingly faces a cost-exchange problem: attackers can launch relatively [cheap drones]({{ 'missile-mismatch/' | relative_url }}) in large numbers, while defenders often respond with interceptors costing hundreds of thousands or even millions of pounds. In that environment, the defender may win tactically yet lose economically by exhausting expensive missile inventories. DragonFire is intended to change that equation by using electrical power to destroy suitable drone targets at a reported operating cost of roughly £10 per engagement, preserving scarce missile stocks for threats that genuinely require them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/advanced-future-military-laser-achieves-uk-first" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">advanced future military laser achieves uk first</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">19 Jan 2024 — Firing it for 10 seconds is the cost equivalent missiles currently carry out. ## The Cost Exchange Problem

Air defence has traditionally been designed around defeating high-value threats such as aircraft, [cruise missiles]({{ 'cruise-missiles/' | relative_url }}) and ballistic missiles. The rapid spread of low-cost drones has created a different challenge. A drone costing tens of thousands of pounds—or in some cases considerably less—can force a defender to launch an interceptor worth many times more. Analysts increasingly describe this as a cost-exchange battle in which the attacker attempts to impose disproportionate defensive costs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.publicspendforum.net/blogs/ash-bedi/2026/04/20/cheap-drones-are-reshaping-the-economics-of-counter-uas-and-air-defense/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publicspendforum.net">[Public Spend Forum+2CSIS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publicspendforum.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cheap drones are reshaping the economics of counter uas and air defense</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">· Defensive responses can sit on a completely different cost curve. · This is not...Read more...</span></span></span>

Recent conflicts have repeatedly exposed this imbalance. Reporting on drone warfare in the Middle East and Ukraine notes that sophisticated missile systems are sometimes being used against mass-produced drones costing only a fraction of the interceptor price. The concern is not merely financial. Missile inventories are finite, production lines are strained, and replenishment can take months or years. Every missile used against a low-end drone is one less available for a more dangerous threat.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thetimes.com/business/wsj/article/america-downs-cheap-drones-with-million-dollar-missiles-a-fix-is-in-the-works-vqqjrr0b9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thetimes.com">[The Times+2rand.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thetimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Times America Downs Cheap Drones With Million-Dollar Missiles</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Fix Is In the Works.As drone warfare escalates with the mass deployment of low-cost Iranian drones like the Shahed, the U.S. and allies...</span></span></span>

DragonFire directly targets this problem. The UK government states that operating the system typically costs less than £10 per shot, while official statements and defence reporting contrast this with traditional interceptor missiles that can cost hundreds of thousands of pounds per engagement. The economic gap is therefore measured not in percentages but in orders of magnitude.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/advanced-future-military-laser-achieves-uk-first" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK+2Defence Equipment &amp; Support]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">advanced future military laser achieves uk first</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">19 Jan 2024 — Firing it for 10 seconds is the cost equivalent missiles currently carry out. The value of such a gap becomes clearer during [saturation]({{ 'saturation/' | relative_url }}) attacks. If dozens of drones arrive simultaneously, a missile-only defence risks rapidly consuming expensive munitions. A laser weapon offers a way to absorb part of that attack without drawing from missile stocks, improving the defender's long-term sustainability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.csis.org/analysis/drone-saturation-russias-shahed-campaign" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csis.org">[CSIS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csis.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">drone saturation russias shahed campaign</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Drone Saturation: Russia&#x27;s Shahed Campaign13 May 2025 — Ukraine urgently requires layered, cost-effective air defenses, including hig...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2025</span></span></span>

## Layered Air Defence Choices

The practical role of DragonFire is best understood as one layer within a larger defensive system rather than as a universal replacement for missiles.

Modern air defence increasingly relies on layered responses. Long-range missiles engage the most dangerous threats at distance. Short-range missiles, guns, electronic warfare systems and interceptor drones provide additional defensive layers closer to the protected asset. High-energy [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}) are being developed as another layer within this architecture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.csis.org/analysis/cost-and-value-air-and-missile-defense-intercepts" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csis.org">[CSIS+2CSIS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csis.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cost and Value in Air and Missile Defense Intercepts13 Feb 2024 — Shorter-range interceptors are cheaper, but also can only provide d...</span></span></span>

In this framework, a laser's contribution is selective missile preservation:

* Engage inexpensive drones when conditions permit.
* Reserve missile magazines for cruise missiles, ballistic missiles and other difficult targets.
* Reduce pressure on ammunition resupply and production.
* Increase the number of engagements that can be sustained during prolonged attacks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rand.org/pubs/commentary/2024/01/directed-energy-the-focus-on-laser-weapons-intensifies.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rand.org">[rand.org+2Sentrycs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rand.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">directed energy the focus on laser weapons intensifies</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Directed Energy: The Focus on Laser Weapons Intensifies25 Jan 2024 — Directed energy weapons offer a more cost-efficient means of dealing...</span></span></span>

This preservation effect is often more important than the direct savings from any individual engagement. A destroyer carrying a fixed number of air-defence missiles cannot simply reload at sea. If a swarm of drones forces dozens of launches, missile depletion becomes an operational risk even if every interception succeeds. A laser system draws on shipboard power [generation]({{ 'generation-ii/' | relative_url }}) instead of consuming a physical interceptor, creating what defence analysts call a "[deep magazine]({{ 'deep-magazine/' | relative_url }})" constrained mainly by electricity and cooling capacity rather than stored ammunition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sentrycs.com/glossary/directed-energy-weapons-dew/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sentrycs.com">[Sentrycs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sentrycs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Directed Energy Weapons (DEW)Directed Energy Weapons use electromagnetic energy to disable drones. Recent defence thinking reflects this shift. Analysts examining drone campaigns in Ukraine and the Gulf increasingly emphasise lower-cost defensive layers—including interceptor drones and high-energy lasers—not because missiles are obsolete, but because preserving expensive interceptors has become a strategic requirement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.csis.org/analysis/unpacking-irans-drone-campaign-gulf-early-lessons-future-drone-warfare" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csis.org">[CSIS+2CSIS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csis.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">unpacking irans drone campaign gulf early lessons future drone warfare</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unpacking Iran&#x27;s Drone Campaign in the Gulf10 Mar 2026 — Interceptor drones have become a core layer of air defense because they are...</span></span></span>

## Why Missile Preservation Matters More Than Shot Cost

Focusing only on the advertised cost per laser shot risks missing the larger mechanism.

The true economic benefit is inventory preservation. Missile shortages can constrain operations long before defence budgets are exhausted. RAND researchers argue that directed-energy weapons could relieve pressure on stretched munitions stockpiles and free conventional weapons for missions where they remain essential.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rand.org/pubs/commentary/2024/01/directed-energy-the-focus-on-laser-weapons-intensifies.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rand.org">[rand.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rand.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">directed energy the focus on laser weapons intensifies</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Directed Energy: The Focus on Laser Weapons Intensifies25 Jan 2024 — Directed energy weapons offer a more cost-efficient means of dealing...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because military effectiveness depends on having the right weapon available at the right time. A navy that spends valuable interceptors defeating cheap drones may find itself with fewer options when confronted by anti-ship missiles or more sophisticated threats later in the same operation. The value of a laser engagement therefore includes both the cost avoided and the missile retained for future use.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.csis.org/analysis/cost-and-value-air-and-missile-defense-intercepts" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csis.org">[CSIS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csis.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cost and Value in Air and Missile Defense Intercepts13 Feb 2024 — Shorter-range interceptors are cheaper, but also can only provide d...</span></span></span>

Recent efforts to develop low-cost interceptor missiles and interceptor drones demonstrate that militaries increasingly view inventory management as a central air-defence challenge. ## Limits of Cost-Based Advantages

The economic case for lasers is compelling, but it is not universal.

A favourable cost exchange does not automatically make a laser the correct engagement choice. High-energy lasers remain dependent on line of sight, atmospheric conditions, target tracking quality, available power and sufficient dwell time on the target. ## The Strategic Mechanism

The central mechanism behind DragonFire's counter-drone value is straightforward: convert part of the air-defence burden from a missile-consumption problem into a power-generation problem.

If the system can reliably destroy suitable drones at a cost measured in pounds rather than hundreds of thousands of pounds, each successful laser engagement preserves both money and missile inventory. In an era increasingly defined by mass drone attacks and saturation tactics, that preservation effect may prove more strategically important than the laser itself.
