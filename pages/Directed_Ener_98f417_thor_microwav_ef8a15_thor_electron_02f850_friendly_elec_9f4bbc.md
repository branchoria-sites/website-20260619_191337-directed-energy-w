## Introduction

High-power microwave (HPM) systems such as [THOR]({{ 'thor/' | relative_url }}) are designed to defeat drones by injecting disruptive electromagnetic energy into their electronics. The same physical mechanism that makes this effective against hostile unmanned aircraft can also affect friendly equipment if the electromagnetic environment is not carefully controlled. The key issue is not that a microwave weapon automatically damages everything nearby. Rather, microwave energy can find pathways into radios, sensors, cables and electronic control systems, creating effects that range from brief glitches and system resets to more serious hardware failures under sufficiently intense exposure. Managing those pathways is therefore a central part of safe operation around any [counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) HPM system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.onr.navy.mil/organization/departments/code-35/division-353/directed-energy-weapons-high-power-microwaves" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navy.mil">[Office of Naval Research+2GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of Naval Research Directed Energy Weapons: High Power Microwaves HPM weapons create beams of electromagnetic energy over a broad s</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Naval ResearchDirected Energy Weapons: High Power MicrowavesHPM weapons create beams of electromagnetic energy over a broad spe...</span></span></span>

## How Microwave Energy Couples Into Electronics

The most important concept is electromagnetic coupling. A microwave pulse does not need to strike a circuit board directly to create problems. Instead, energy can enter equipment through conductive structures that act as unintended receivers.

Common [coupling paths]({{ 'coupling-paths/' | relative_url }}) include:

* [Radio antennas]({{ 'radio-risk/' | relative_url }}) and antenna feeds.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* External cables carrying power or data.
* Wiring harnesses inside vehicles or facilities.
* Sensor apertures and exposed electronic interfaces.
* Metallic structures that reradiate energy into connected systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.waru.edu/sites/default/files/Migrated/CopDocuments/EMP%20Overview.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: waru.edu">[Waru University+2Enercon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">waru.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">EMP Overview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Man-made EMP (Weapons)... In practical terms, a communications antenna intended to receive radio signals can also collect energy from an HPM pulse. That energy may travel into receivers, amplifiers or processing electronics. Likewise, long cables can behave as unintended antennas, picking up transient energy and conducting it into sensitive equipment. EMP and HPM survivability studies repeatedly identify cables, power lines and antenna connections as major entry routes for damaging electromagnetic effects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.waru.edu/sites/default/files/Migrated/CopDocuments/EMP%20Overview.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: waru.edu">[Waru University+2Enercon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">waru.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">EMP Overview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Man-made EMP (Weapons)... ### Why Small Electronics Can Be Sensitive

Modern electronics rely on densely packed semiconductor components operating at low voltages. These devices are highly capable but often less tolerant of unexpected voltage spikes than older analogue systems.

When microwave energy enters a circuit, several outcomes are possible:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Logic errors in processors.
* Corrupted memory states.
* Sensor misreadings.
* Communication dropouts.
* Automatic system reboots.
* Electrical overstress of components in severe cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doiserbia.nb.rs/ft.aspx?id=0353-36701604621S" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doiserbia.nb.rs">[doiSerbia+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doiserbia.nb.rs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Key words:...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Which Base Systems Are Most Vulnerable During Engagement?

Not every electronic device on a base faces the same level of risk. Vulnerability depends heavily on exposure, shielding, cable length, antenna configuration and system hardening.

### Communications Networks

Radios are among the most obvious concerns because they are intentionally designed to receive electromagnetic energy. Base communications infrastructure may include fixed antennas, relay links, satellite terminals and tactical radio networks.

If strong microwave energy reaches these systems, operators could experience:

* Temporary receiver overload.
* Loss of communications links.
* Reduced signal quality.
* Equipment resets requiring operator intervention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.onr.navy.mil/organization/departments/code-35/division-353/directed-energy-weapons-high-power-microwaves" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navy.mil">[Office of Naval Research+2NATO Store]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of Naval Research Directed Energy Weapons: High Power Microwaves HPM weapons create beams of electromagnetic energy over a broad s</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Naval ResearchDirected Energy Weapons: High Power MicrowavesHPM weapons create beams of electromagnetic energy over a broad spe...</span></span></span>

For a base defending itself against drones, even a brief communications interruption can complicate command and control during a rapidly evolving engagement.

### Sensors and Surveillance Equipment

Air-defence operations depend on sensors. Radar systems, optical tracking networks and electronic surveillance equipment often contain highly sensitive front-end electronics.

Many of these systems are designed to detect weak signals. As a result, they may be particularly susceptible to powerful electromagnetic transients entering through antenna paths or supporting infrastructure. NATO discussions of HPM effects have noted that unprotected electronic systems can be disturbed or damaged even when located inside structures, depending on coupling conditions and protection measures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sto.nato.int/document/tactical-implications-of-high-power-microwaves-2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sto.nato.int">[NATO Store]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sto.nato.int</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NATO StoreTactical Implications of High Power MicrowavesThe Workshop pro-vided clear evidence of how unprotected electronic systems locat...</span></span></span>

### Data and Power Infrastructure

The risk is not limited to traditional military hardware. Modern bases depend on servers, network switches, power-conversion equipment and battery systems.

Research into intentional electromagnetic interference has shown that power converters and sensor-controlled electrical systems can be affected by electromagnetic injection, creating operational anomalies even when the equipment is not the primary target. Such findings highlight why support infrastructure is part of electromagnetic safety planning around HPM systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06901" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Assault and Battery: Evaluating the Security of Power Conversion Systems Against Electromagnetic Injection AttacksMay 11, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 11, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why Disruption Can Matter Even Without Permanent Damage

A common misconception is that microwave weapons are only a concern if they physically destroy electronics. Operationally, temporary disruption may be just as important.

Military systems are often interconnected. A short-lived outage in one component can propagate through larger networks. For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* A communications reset can delay warning messages.
* A sensor glitch can create temporary tracking gaps.
* A network interruption can affect command decisions.

### The Timing Problem

The critical factor is often timing rather than permanence. A radar that resumes normal operation five minutes later may still have failed at the moment it was needed most.

This is why electromagnetic compatibility and deconfliction procedures are central to HPM [deployment]({{ 'deployment/' | relative_url }}). Operators must consider not only whether equipment survives exposure, but whether it remains available throughout the engagement window.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2Navy SBIR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 106717</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — These weapons include high energy lasers and other high power electroma...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span>
