## Introduction

In [counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) laser engagements, wavelength can matter as much as power. A laser that easily overwhelms one sensor may have little effect on another, even at similar energy levels. The reason is straightforward: cameras, infrared imagers, protective windows and optical coatings do not respond equally to all wavelengths. Whether a laser merely dazzles a drone’s sensor, wastes most of its energy, or causes permanent damage depends heavily on how the target optics absorb, transmit or reflect that specific part of the spectrum. Research on electro-optical sensors consistently shows that laser effects are strongly wavelength-dependent because detector materials are designed to be sensitive only within particular spectral bands.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researching.cn/articles/OJ1020b3e8e481ecfc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researching.cn">[Researching]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researching.cn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research progress of laser dazzle and damage CMOS image...26 Jul 2023 — As the core component of an optoelectronic imaging sy...</span></span></span>

Within the broader distinction between dazzling sensors and destroying hardware, wavelength selection is one of the key factors that determines where on that spectrum an engagement will fall.

## Why Sensors Absorb Some Wavelengths Better

A [drone camera]({{ 'drone-cameras/' | relative_url }}) is not a generic light detector. It is built around materials that convert photons into electrical signals only within certain wavelength ranges.

Most visible-light drone cameras use silicon-based CMOS or CCD sensors. Silicon responds strongly from the visible spectrum into the near infrared, but its sensitivity drops sharply beyond roughly 1.1 micrometres because photons at longer wavelengths do not carry enough energy to cross silicon’s bandgap. As a result, wavelengths that are highly effective against silicon sensors may be largely ineffective against detectors designed for longer-wave infrared imaging.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pembrokeinstruments.com/resources/swir-cameras-guide/the-physics-of-swir/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pembrokeinstruments.com">[Pembroke Instruments]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pembrokeinstruments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pembroke InstrumentsThe Physics of SWIR Camera ImagingMarch 17, 2026 — Standard silicon sensors lose sensitivity as wavelengths move into...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 17, 2026</span></span></span>

This creates an important asymmetry:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A visible or near-infrared laser can strongly affect a conventional daylight camera.
* The same laser may have much less effect on a thermal imager operating in a different spectral band.
* Conversely, a wavelength selected for a thermal sensor may pass through a visible camera system with limited interaction.

</div>

Modern drones increasingly employ multiple sensor types. A platform may carry a visible camera, a short-wave infrared (SWIR) sensor and a thermal imager simultaneously. Each detector technology is tuned to different wavelength ranges and often uses different semiconductor materials such as silicon, indium gallium arsenide (InGaAs), mercury cadmium telluride, or related infrared-sensitive compounds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lontenoe.com/visible-swir-dual-band-camera-specifications-comparison/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lontenoe.com">[lontenoe+2SCD - SemiConductor Devices]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lontenoe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visible + SWIR Dual-Band Camera: Specifications</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visible + SWIR Dual-Band Camera: Specifications...May 30, 2025 — 30 May 2025 — This camera is suitable for the spectral range of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 30, 2025</span></span></span>

The practical consequence is that “laser colour” determines whether energy reaches the detector efficiently. A wavelength that aligns with the detector’s peak sensitivity can produce strong dazzling effects at comparatively modest energy levels, whereas a poorly matched wavelength may require far more power for the same operational result.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researching.cn/articles/OJ1020b3e8e481ecfc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researching.cn">[Researching]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researching.cn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research progress of laser dazzle and damage CMOS image...26 Jul 2023 — As the core component of an optoelectronic imaging sy...</span></span></span>

## How Coatings and Windows Change Exposure

The detector is only one part of the optical chain. Before laser energy reaches the sensor, it must pass through lenses, protective windows, filters and coatings.

These components can dramatically alter the outcome.

### The Window May Be the Real Target

Many electro-optical systems place a transparent protective window in front of the sensor. The material is chosen according to the wavelengths the system is expected to observe.

A window that transmits visible light efficiently may strongly absorb infrared wavelengths. Another may be designed specifically for infrared transmission while rejecting much of the visible spectrum. If a laser wavelength falls into an absorption region of the window material, substantial energy can be deposited in the window itself rather than the detector behind it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lasercomponents.com/en/photonics-portal/trends-and-innovation/trends/high-laser-power-high-damage-thresholds/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lasercomponents.com">[Laser Components+2Shimadzu Europe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lasercomponents.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Laser ComponentsHigh Laser Power – High Damage Thresholds9 Nov 2015 — The damage threshold of a laser optic depends on many factors: The...</span></span></span>

This changes the mechanism entirely. Instead of dazzling the detector, the laser may primarily heat optical components.

### Coatings Can Help or Hurt

Optical coatings are frequently the most wavelength-sensitive elements in the system.

Anti-reflection coatings are designed to maximise transmission in specific bands. Interference filters may intentionally block certain wavelengths. Laser-protection concepts often rely on spectral filtering because the effectiveness of dazzling varies strongly across the spectrum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publica.fraunhofer.de/bitstreams/99efb61e-6caa-4395-bd8e-46d9ae30992a/download" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publica.fraunhofer.de">[Fraunhofer Publica]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publica.fraunhofer.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fraunhofer PublicaElectro-optical sensor with automatic suppression of laser...October 25, 2012 — by G Ritt · Cited by 14 — We present a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 25, 2012</span></span></span>

The same coating can therefore produce very different outcomes depending on wavelength:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* High transmission may allow energy to reach the detector efficiently, increasing dazzling effectiveness.
* High reflectivity may reject much of the incoming laser energy.
* Absorptive behaviour can create localised heating and potentially lower damage thresholds.

</div>

Research on laser-resistant optics consistently identifies coating design as one of the primary determinants of laser survivability. In many cases, the coating reaches its damage limit before the bulk optical material does.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lasercomponents.com/en/photonics-portal/trends-and-innovation/trends/high-laser-power-high-damage-thresholds/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lasercomponents.com">[Laser Components]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lasercomponents.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Laser ComponentsHigh Laser Power – High Damage Thresholds9 Nov 2015 — The damage threshold of a laser optic depends on many factors: The...</span></span></span>

### Filters Are Not Universal Protection

A common misconception is that fitting a filter solves the laser threat problem.

In practice, filters are highly wavelength-specific. A filter designed to suppress one laser line may provide little protection against another. Researchers investigating anti-dazzle systems note that the growing variety of commercially available laser wavelengths complicates fixed-filter approaches because protection effective in one spectral region may leave others exposed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publica.fraunhofer.de/bitstreams/99efb61e-6caa-4395-bd8e-46d9ae30992a/download" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publica.fraunhofer.de">[Fraunhofer Publica]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publica.fraunhofer.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fraunhofer PublicaElectro-optical sensor with automatic suppression of laser...October 25, 2012 — by G Ritt · Cited by 14 — We present a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 25, 2012</span></span></span>

## Why Power Alone Is a Misleading Measure

Discussions of laser weapons often focus on power output, but power alone does not predict sensor effects.

The crucial question is how much of that energy is actually absorbed by vulnerable components.

A lower-power laser operating in a sensor’s preferred spectral band can sometimes produce stronger operational disruption than a higher-power laser at a poorly matched wavelength. The detector effectively acts as a wavelength-selective receiver.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researching.cn/articles/OJ1020b3e8e481ecfc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researching.cn">[Researching]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researching.cn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research progress of laser dazzle and damage CMOS image...26 Jul 2023 — As the core component of an optoelectronic imaging sy...</span></span></span>

This explains why two [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}) delivering similar irradiance can create very different results:

* One wavelength may produce immediate [saturation]({{ 'saturation/' | relative_url }}) and [blooming]({{ 'blooming/' | relative_url }}).
* Another may generate only a modest reduction in image quality.
* A third may deposit energy into optics rather than the sensor itself.

Experimental work on imaging sensors has shown that both dazzling thresholds and damage thresholds vary with wavelength. ## When Dazzling Becomes Damage

The transition from temporary interference to permanent degradation is also wavelength-dependent.

For a given detector, the most effective dazzling wavelength is often one that the sensor is designed to detect efficiently. ## What This Means for Counter-Drone Laser Design

The most effective wavelength is not necessarily the one that carries the most energy. It is the one that interacts most strongly with the target sensor architecture.

Designers must consider:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The spectral band in which the sensor operates.
* The transmission properties of protective windows.
* The reflectivity and absorption of optical coatings.
* Whether the goal is temporary sensor denial or permanent damage.

</div>

As drone payloads become more diverse, a wavelength optimised against one camera may perform poorly against another.
