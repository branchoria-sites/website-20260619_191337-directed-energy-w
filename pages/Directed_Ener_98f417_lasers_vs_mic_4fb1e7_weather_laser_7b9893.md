## Introduction

Directed-energy weapons do not operate in a vacuum. In air defence, the [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}) becomes part of the engagement itself. The central weather-related distinction between lasers and high-power [microwaves]({{ 'microwaves/' | relative_url }}) is that lasers must deliver a tightly focused beam of light through the air to a precise point on a target, while microwaves use longer-wavelength radio-frequency energy that generally tolerates atmospheric disruption more effectively. As a result, rain, fog, dust, smoke, sea spray and turbulence can dramatically alter laser performance, whereas microwave systems are often less sensitive to those same conditions, though they face their own propagation, attenuation and safety constraints. The practical consequence is that a laser's advertised range on a clear day is rarely its guaranteed combat range in all environments. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defencesciencereview.com.pl/pdf-216776-135052?filename=Laser-Based-Directed-Ener.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: com.pl">[Defence Science Review+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">com.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pdf 216776 135052</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defence Science ReviewDEFENCE SCIENCE REVIEWJanuary 17, 2026 — by A Karkadakattil · 2026 · Cited by 1 — Objectives: This review aims to e...</span></span></span>

## When the Air Itself Becomes a Shield

The atmosphere is not empty space. It contains water vapour, aerosols, dust, pollution, temperature gradients and constantly moving pockets of air. Every one of these factors can interfere with energy transmission.

For laser weapons, the challenge is fundamental. A high-energy laser must keep enough power concentrated on a small spot for long enough to heat, melt or structurally weaken part of the target. Any atmospheric process that scatters, absorbs or distorts the beam reduces the energy arriving at that spot. Researchers studying military laser propagation consistently identify atmospheric attenuation, turbulence and thermal effects as among the most important operational constraints. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defencesciencereview.com.pl/pdf-216776-135052?filename=Laser-Based-Directed-Ener.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: com.pl">[Defence Science Review+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">com.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pdf 216776 135052</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defence Science ReviewDEFENCE SCIENCE REVIEWJanuary 17, 2026 — by A Karkadakattil · 2026 · Cited by 1 — Objectives: This review aims to e...</span></span></span>

Microwave systems face atmospheric effects too, but the mechanisms differ. Because microwave [wavelengths]({{ 'wavelengths/' | relative_url }}) are much longer than laser wavelengths, many atmospheric particles that strongly affect optical beams have less influence on microwave propagation. The result is often greater tolerance to haze, smoke and moderate weather disruption, although heavy rainfall, atmospheric absorption and propagation losses can still reduce effectiveness. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publicationsdrdo.in/index.php/dsj/article/download/21114/8787/92344" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publicationsdrdo.in">[DRDO Publications+2K-State Libraries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publicationsdrdo.in</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This structured narrative review analyses how High-Power Microwave (HPM) Directed Energy Weapons. The key operational lesson is that weather is not merely a background condition. It can become a decisive factor in weapon selection.

## How Scattering and Turbulence Affect Laser Energy

### Fog, Rain and Aerosols

Water droplets are especially troublesome for lasers. Fog, clouds, mist and heavy rain introduce large numbers of particles that scatter light away from the intended target. Dust, smoke, sea spray and [battlefield]({{ 'battle-limits/' | relative_url }}) obscurants can produce similar effects.

The problem is not simply that the beam becomes less accurate. Every scattered photon represents energy that no longer contributes to heating the target. A laser that can disable a drone quickly in clear conditions may require much longer dwell times in degraded visibility, or may fail to achieve the necessary energy density altogether. Studies of high-energy laser propagation repeatedly identify aerosol scattering and water-droplet interactions as major range-limiting mechanisms. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.files.ethz.ch/isn/31415/forden.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ethz.ch">[ETH Zurich Files+2deps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ethz.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ETH Zurich Files The Airborne Laser</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ETH Zurich FilesThe Airborne LaserMay 29, 2007 — 12 Nov 1996 — Even clear air attenuates, or reduces, the power of the laser beams. This is one reason naval laser programmes attract particular attention from atmospheric researchers. Marine environments combine humidity, sea spray, temperature gradients and turbulent air layers that can complicate beam propagation even when visibility appears acceptable to the human eye. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/apme/50/1/2010jamc2482.1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.ametsoc.org">[American Meteorological Society Journals+2Laser Wars]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.ametsoc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Meteorological Society JournalsClimate Change: Anticipated Effects on High-Energy Laser...by ST Fiorino · 2011 · Cited by 10 —...</span></span></span>

### Heat Shimmer and Atmospheric Turbulence

Even apparently clear air can degrade a laser beam.

Air of different temperatures has slightly different refractive properties. As sunlight heats terrain, runways, rooftops or ocean surfaces, pockets of warm and cool air mix together. These changing refractive conditions bend and distort laser beams, creating effects similar to the visual shimmer seen above a hot road.

For a high-energy laser, turbulence can enlarge the beam spot, reduce intensity on target and complicate precision tracking. The atmosphere effectively acts as a constantly changing optical lens. Military laser programmes therefore invest heavily in [adaptive optics]({{ 'adaptive-optics/' | relative_url }}), wavefront sensing and atmospheric compensation technologies designed to correct beam distortions in real time. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403676537_Laser-Based_Directed_Energy_Weapons_Technological_Capabilities_Material_Interaction_and_Strategic_Deployment_Pathways" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">mitigated through feedback from wavefront and atmospheric sensors to the adaptive...Read more...</span></span></span>

### Thermal Blooming

An additional laser-specific problem is thermal blooming. As the beam travels, absorbed energy heats the air through which it passes. The heated air changes density and refractive properties, causing the beam to spread and lose focus.

In effect, the laser can partially create its own propagation problem. Thermal blooming becomes more significant as power levels rise and atmospheric absorption increases. ## Why Clear-Test Range Is Not a Fixed Combat Radius

Laser weapon demonstrations often occur under carefully selected conditions. Such tests are valuable, but they can unintentionally create the impression that range is a fixed number.

In reality, atmospheric transmission is highly variable. A laser engagement envelope may expand dramatically in dry, stable air and contract significantly during fog, dust storms, rain or intense turbulence. The same weapon can therefore exhibit different effective ranges across seasons, climates and times of day. Defence researchers studying operational laser deployment repeatedly emphasise that environmental conditions strongly influence achievable power on target. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defencesciencereview.com.pl/pdf-216776-135052?filename=Laser-Based-Directed-Ener.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: com.pl">[Defence Science Review+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">com.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pdf 216776 135052</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defence Science ReviewDEFENCE SCIENCE REVIEWJanuary 17, 2026 — by A Karkadakattil · 2026 · Cited by 1 — Objectives: This review aims to e...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters for air-defence planning. A missile carries its destructive mechanism to the target. A laser must transmit destructive energy through the atmosphere for the entire engagement path. Consequently, commanders cannot assume that a published test range represents a guaranteed all-weather defensive perimeter. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drillanddefense.com/laser-weapons-the-new-layer-in-modern-air-defense/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drillanddefense.com">[Drill &amp; Defense+2ETH Zurich Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drillanddefense.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Weather and atmosphere can reduce effectiveness. The practical result is that many military concepts treat laser range as a weather-dependent variable rather than a constant performance figure.

## Why Microwaves Usually Tolerate Weather Better

High-power microwave systems operate in a different part of the electromagnetic spectrum and therefore interact differently with the atmosphere.

Because microwave wavelengths are much larger than typical fog droplets, dust particles and smoke particles, these obstacles generally produce less scattering than they do for lasers. This gives microwave systems a reputation for greater all-weather robustness, particularly in counter-drone roles. Several military and technical reviews identify lower sensitivity to atmospheric disturbances as a major comparative advantage of microwave weapons. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kstatelibraries.pressbooks.pub/drone-delivery/chapter/11-microwaves/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kstatelibraries.pressbooks.pub">[K-State Libraries+2DRDO Publications]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kstatelibraries.pressbooks.pub</span><span class="citation-popover-title">K-State Libraries11</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DE Weapons &amp; Microwavesby C Carter · 2022 — Microwave weapons are far less sensitive to atmospheric disturbances than lasers, making them...</span></span></span>

However, "less sensitive" does not mean immune.

Heavy rainfall can attenuate microwave signals, atmospheric absorption can reduce energy delivery, and propagation losses still increase with distance. Some analyses also note that severe atmospheric turbulence and weather effects can influence microwave effectiveness, although typically to a lesser degree than comparable optical systems. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://securityanddefence.pl/Analysis-of-the-power-of-drones-and-limitations-of-the-anti-drone-solutions-on-the%2C208347%2C0%2C2.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: securityanddefence.pl">[Security and Defence+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">securityanddefence.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">To evaluate the heating phenomenon surrounding the HMP system, a...Read more...</span></span></span>

Microwave weapons also face a separate operational challenge: their broader-area effects can create concerns about friendly electronics, nearby communications systems and other unintended electromagnetic interactions. In other words, the atmosphere may be less of a barrier, but the wider electromagnetic environment becomes more important. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 106717</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — Directed energy weapons—such as lasers—use energy fired at the speed of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span>

## Which Environments Favour Each Defensive Layer?

The comparison is not simply "lasers in good weather, microwaves in [bad weather]({{ 'bad-weather/' | relative_url }})." Real deployments are more nuanced.

**Environments that generally favour lasers:**


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Dry climates with low humidity.
* Clear visibility and limited aerosol content.
* Situations requiring precise engagement of individual drones or sensors.
* Areas where collateral electromagnetic effects must be minimised. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defencesciencereview.com.pl/pdf-216776-135052?filename=Laser-Based-Directed-Ener.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: com.pl">[Defence Science Review+2Lockheed Martin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">com.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pdf 216776 135052</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defence Science ReviewDEFENCE SCIENCE REVIEWJanuary 17, 2026 — by A Karkadakattil · 2026 · Cited by 1 — Objectives: This review aims to e...</span></span></span>

</div>

**Environments that often favour microwave systems:**


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Conditions involving haze, smoke, dust or moderate weather degradation.
* Counter-swarm missions where many drones may appear simultaneously.
* Situations where exact aim-point heating is less important than disrupting electronics across an area. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kstatelibraries.pressbooks.pub/drone-delivery/chapter/11-microwaves/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kstatelibraries.pressbooks.pub">[K-State Libraries+2DRDO Publications]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kstatelibraries.pressbooks.pub</span><span class="citation-popover-title">K-State Libraries11</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DE Weapons &amp; Microwavesby C Carter · 2022 — Microwave weapons are far less sensitive to atmospheric disturbances than lasers, making them...</span></span></span>

**Mixed or challenging environments:**


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Coastal zones with sea spray and marine boundary-layer turbulence.
* Urban areas with heat shimmer and pollution.
* Regions experiencing seasonal fog or sandstorms.
* Rapidly changing weather conditions. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/apme/50/1/2010jamc2482.1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.ametsoc.org">[American Meteorological Society Journals+2Laser Wars]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.ametsoc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Meteorological Society JournalsClimate Change: Anticipated Effects on High-Energy Laser...by ST Fiorino · 2011 · Cited by 10 —...</span></span></span>

## The Historical Trend: From Weather Vulnerability to Layered Defence

The history of directed-energy development has repeatedly reinforced the importance of atmospheric effects. Early high-energy laser programmes often revealed that atmospheric propagation was as difficult a challenge as generating the beam itself. In practice, the atmosphere rarely determines whether directed-energy weapons can be used. Instead, it often determines which directed-energy layer offers the more reliable option at a particular place, time and weather condition.
