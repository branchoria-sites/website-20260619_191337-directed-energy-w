## Introduction

[Counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) lasers are often advertised as having a “[deep magazine]({{ 'deep-magazine/' | relative_url }})” because they do not rely on a finite stock of missiles. However, drone swarms expose a different limit: heat. A laser that can destroy a single drone may still struggle when dozens of targets arrive within a few minutes. Each engagement deposits waste heat into the weapon system, and if that heat accumulates faster than cooling systems can remove it, firing rates must slow, power may be reduced, or the weapon may need to pause entirely. In this sense, drone swarms are not merely a targeting challenge; they are a thermal stress test that probes the practical endurance of directed-energy weapons under sustained operational demand.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — Directed energy weapons (DEW) use concentrated electromagnetic energy t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span>

## Can Lasers Keep Up With Drone Swarms?

The answer depends less on whether a laser can destroy a drone and more on how many drones it can defeat before thermal saturation occurs.

Unlike a missile battery that expends a physical interceptor, a laser expends time. The beam must remain on a target long enough to heat and damage critical components. This period, known as dwell time, can range from fractions of a second to several seconds depending on laser power, range, atmospheric conditions and target design. During a swarm attack, every additional second spent on one drone is a second unavailable for another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.electronicsforu.com/technology-trends/directed-energy-weapons-laser-weapons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: electronicsforu.com">[electronicsforu.com+2defencefinancemonitor.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">electronicsforu.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">directed energy weapons laser weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Directed Energy Weapons: High-Energy Laser Weapons25 Jan 2017 — Unlike projectile weapons that instantly destroy the target upon impact...</span></span></span>

As a result, swarm defence becomes a throughput problem. Even if a laser has electrical power available, it must maintain beam quality, tracking performance and acceptable component temperatures across a sequence of engagements. The more targets that appear in a compressed period, the greater the cumulative thermal burden.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — Directed energy weapons (DEW) use concentrated electromagnetic energy t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span>

## Why Many Small Targets Create Peak Thermal Load

A common misconception is that large aircraft or missiles are the hardest thermal challenge for laser weapons. In practice, a dense swarm of small drones can be more demanding because it forces continuous operation.

A single engagement may not significantly stress a cooling system. A swarm, however, can require dozens of consecutive shots with little opportunity for recovery. Every engagement adds waste heat from laser generation, beam control systems, power electronics and cooling equipment. The thermal load therefore accumulates across the engagement sequence rather than being determined by a single shot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — Directed energy weapons (DEW) use concentrated electromagnetic energy t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span>

The mathematics can become unforgiving. If a laser requires even one or two seconds of effective dwell time per target, a swarm of dozens of drones can rapidly consume available engagement time. Studies examining directed-energy defence against UAV [swarms]({{ 'swarms/' | relative_url }}) emphasise that swarm defeat probability depends heavily on target arrival rates and the time required to service each target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15485129251340992" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsEffect of dwell time, distance, and reflecting area on UAV...9 Jul 2025 — This paper is concerned with the operational effe...</span></span></span>

This is one reason military organisations increasingly evaluate layered counter-drone architectures rather than assuming a laser can handle all incoming threats. Swarms test not only weapon lethality but also the sustainability of repeated engagements under operational conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lockheedmartin.com/en-us/news/features/2025/the-counter-uas-challenge-closing-the-gap-in-drone-swarm-defense.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lockheedmartin.com">[Lockheed Martin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lockheedmartin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lockheed MartinC-UAS Challenge: Closing the Gap in Drone Swarm Defense5 Aug 2025 — The system is designed to quickly detect, track, ID an...</span></span></span>

## How Repeated Dwell Times Shrink Firing Margins

The thermal challenge is magnified because dwell time and heat generation are linked.

Longer dwell times mean the laser remains at high output for longer periods, generating more cumulative waste heat. If atmospheric conditions reduce beam effectiveness, if targets manoeuvre, or if drones incorporate protective measures that delay damage, required dwell times increase further. That directly raises the thermal burden imposed on the system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nps.edu/documents/10180/142489929/JDE_7-2_Johnson.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.edu">[Naval Postgraduate School]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">HEL laser beam, which will thus increase the dwell time</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval Postgraduate SchoolCounter Directed Energy Weapons and the Defense of...January 10, 2023 — by B Johnson · 2023 · Cited by 2 — Usin...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2023</span></span></span>

Swarm tactics can exploit this relationship. Defence analyses have noted that manoeuvres, decoys and other tactics can increase the time required for a successful engagement. Even modest increases in dwell time can significantly reduce the number of drones defeated within a given interval.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nps.edu/documents/10180/142489929/JDE_7-2_Johnson.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.edu">[Naval Postgraduate School]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">HEL laser beam, which will thus increase the dwell time</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval Postgraduate SchoolCounter Directed Energy Weapons and the Defense of...January 10, 2023 — by B Johnson · 2023 · Cited by 2 — Usin...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2023</span></span></span>

* More heat reduces available firing margin before cooling limits are reached.

## Real-World Indicators of the Throughput Problem

Public demonstrations often highlight the number of drones a system can defeat per minute rather than simply whether it can destroy a drone at all. That emphasis reflects the operational importance of sustained engagement rates.

Manufacturers of counter-UAS laser systems increasingly advertise metrics such as defeating more than 20 drones per minute or maintaining high engagement rates against swarm attacks. Such claims implicitly recognise that the key challenge is repeated engagement throughput rather than individual-shot lethality.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eos-aus.com/defence/high-energy-laser-weapon/apollo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eos-aus.com">[EOS AUS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eos-aus.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EOS AUSApollo High Energy Laser WeaponIts increased laser power and reduced dwell time between engagements enable it to disable more than...</span></span></span>

## What Cooling Limits Mean for Air-Defence Planning

For air-defence planners, the critical question is not whether a laser can destroy a drone but whether it can sustain enough engagements during the peak period of an attack.

A laser that performs well against sporadic threats may encounter difficulties when confronted with dozens of drones arriving nearly simultaneously. In such scenarios, cooling capacity effectively becomes part of the weapon's magazine depth. The system's endurance depends on how rapidly it can reject heat and restore thermal margins between shots.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — Directed energy weapons (DEW) use concentrated electromagnetic energy t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span>
