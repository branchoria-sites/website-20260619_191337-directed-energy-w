## Introduction

When a high-power microwave (HPM) system such as [THOR]({{ 'thor/' | relative_url }}) is described as “disabling” a drone, the crucial mechanism is usually not a visible strike against the airframe. Instead, the microwave energy must find a way into the drone’s electronics. Engineers refer to this process as electromagnetic coupling: the transfer of electromagnetic energy from the external field into conductive structures and circuits inside the target. High-power microwave weapons are specifically designed to exploit these coupling paths, creating disruptive voltages and currents in electronics that were never intended to handle them. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.onr.navy.mil/organization/departments/code-35/division-353/directed-energy-weapons-high-power-microwaves" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onr.navy.mil">[Office of Naval Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onr.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Naval ResearchDirected Energy Weapons: High Power MicrowavesHPM weapons create beams of electromagnetic energy over a broad spe...</span></span></span>

## Conductive Pathways Into Vulnerable Electronics

Microwave energy does not need a direct opening into a drone's processor. Instead, it can exploit conductive structures that naturally interact with electromagnetic fields.

The most obvious coupling path is through antennas. Communication links, telemetry systems, GPS receivers, video transmitters, and command-and-control radios all depend on antennas designed to receive electromagnetic signals. Under sufficiently intense microwave illumination, these antennas can act as highly efficient entry points that channel energy into radio-frequency front ends and connected circuitry. Studies of UAV electromagnetic vulnerability consistently identify communication and navigation systems among the most exposed subsystems. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/21/4332" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mdpi.com">[MDPI+2radioeng.cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mdpi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Investigation on Electromagnetic Immunity of Unmanned...by R Kubacki · 2025 · Cited by 2 — Microwave field incidents on a drone can...</span></span></span>

A second major pathway is wiring. Power cables, signal harnesses, motor-control wiring, and interconnects can behave like unintended receiving antennas. Even though they were not designed for radio-frequency reception, long conductors can collect energy from an external microwave field and transport induced currents into sensitive electronics. Experimental research on UAV exposure to high-power [microwaves]({{ 'microwaves/' | relative_url }}) has repeatedly identified cables and power-distribution paths as dominant energy-coupling routes. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362260176_Investigation_on_Falling_and_Damage_Mechanisms_of_UAV_Illuminated_by_HPM_Pulses" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Investigation on Falling and Damage Mechanisms of UAV...13 Oct 2022 — Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV), when illuminated by hig...</span></span></span>

Circuit traces on printed circuit boards provide another route. Once energy enters through an antenna or cable, it can propagate into processors, memory chips, sensor interfaces, power-management circuits, and motor controllers. The result may be transient faults, software crashes, logic errors, latch-up conditions in semiconductors, or permanent component damage depending on the intensity and duration of the pulse. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.onr.navy.mil/organization/departments/code-35/division-353/directed-energy-weapons-high-power-microwaves" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onr.navy.mil">[Office of Naval Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onr.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Naval ResearchDirected Energy Weapons: High Power MicrowavesHPM weapons create beams of electromagnetic energy over a broad spe...</span></span></span>

## Why Orientation and Shielding Change the Result

Microwave coupling is highly dependent on geometry. The same drone may experience very different effects depending on how it is positioned relative to the incoming electromagnetic field.

Orientation matters because antennas and conductors interact differently with fields arriving from different directions and polarisations. A wire that is aligned favourably with the incident field can collect substantially more energy than one oriented at an unfavourable angle. Research on drone antenna behaviour shows that orientation can significantly alter received electromagnetic power, even when the transmitter and receiver remain in the same general location. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01442" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Impact of 3D UWB Antenna Radiation Pattern on Air-to-Ground Drone ConnectivityOctober 2, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 2, 2018</span></span></span>

Shielding also changes the outcome. Metallic structures, conductive coatings, filtered connectors, shielded cables, and hardened radio-frequency components can reduce the amount of energy reaching sensitive electronics. However, shielding is rarely perfect. Practical systems still require apertures, seams, sensors, antennas, [cooling]({{ 'cooling/' | relative_url }}) paths, and electrical interfaces. These openings can become entry points for electromagnetic energy. Defence analysts frequently describe microwave systems as exploiting the "seams" through which energy can infiltrate electronics rather than penetrating a completely sealed enclosure. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaldefensemagazine.org/articles/2026/1/20/counterdrone-mission-seen-as-killer-app-for-directed-energy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaldefensemagazine.org">[National Defense Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaldefensemagazine.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">counterdrone mission seen as killer app for directed energy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Defense MagazineCounter-UAS Mission Seen as Killer App for Directed EnergyJan 20, 2026 — High-powered microwaves can blast more...</span></span></span>

This helps explain why two outwardly similar drones can react differently under the same exposure. Small design differences in wiring layout, cable length, shielding quality, antenna placement, grounding, and electronic architecture may substantially alter the coupling [efficiency]({{ 'efficiency/' | relative_url }}). <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/21/4332" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mdpi.com">[MDPI+2Preprints]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mdpi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Investigation on Electromagnetic Immunity of Unmanned...by R Kubacki · 2025 · Cited by 2 — Microwave field incidents on a drone can...</span></span></span>

## Why a Drone Can Fail Without Visible Damage

One reason microwave defeat mechanisms are difficult to assess from photographs or videos is that coupling targets electronics rather than structure.

Unlike a laser, which may leave burn marks or visible physical damage, a microwave pulse can induce electrical effects inside the vehicle while leaving the airframe largely intact. A drone may simply stop responding, enter a failsafe mode, reboot unexpectedly, lose navigation data, or suffer corrupted control signals. External observers may only see a drone descend, drift, or fall from the sky. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy WeaponsMay 25, 2023 — These weapons include high energy lasers and other high power electrom...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 25, 2023</span></span></span>

Research on UAV exposure to high-power electromagnetic environments describes a spectrum of outcomes ranging from temporary upset to complete burnout. The distinction depends on how much energy couples into the electronics and which components receive it.
