## Introduction

The low cost of a laser shot is one of the most frequently cited advantages of directed energy weapons. Yet a laser does not become a practical weapon simply because it can generate a powerful beam. Before a target can be damaged, the system must detect it, identify it, track it, predict its movement and keep the beam precisely focused on the same point for long enough to produce an effect. Those functions depend on sensors, software, stabilised optics and combat-system integration rather than the laser itself.

In many modern programmes, the hidden cost lies in turning the beam into part of a complete sensing and engagement network. The result is a paradox: the electricity used for a shot may cost only a few pounds, but the supporting systems required to make that shot effective can account for a large share of the weapon's total cost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/integrated-air-and-missile-defense/lasers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rtx.com">[RTX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rtx.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">High-Energy Lasers &#124; RaytheonThis directed energy technology enables detection of threats, tracking during maneuvers, and positive vis...</span></span></span>

## Why the Beam Is Only One Part of the Weapon

A military laser cannot simply point in the general direction of a threat and expect success. Unlike a missile, which carries its own guidance system after launch, a laser must continuously maintain energy on a specific spot on the target. Even a small drone moving unpredictably can break that engagement if tracking is lost for a fraction of a second.

To achieve this, modern laser weapons combine multiple subsystems:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Electro-optical cameras for visual tracking.
* Infrared sensors for detecting heat signatures.
* Laser range-finding and target-location equipment.
* Beam directors and steering mirrors.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.dia.mil/FOIA/FOIA-Electronic-Reading-Room/FileId/237622/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dia.mil">[dia.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dia.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">State of the Art and Evolution of High-Energy Laser WeaponsApril 28, 2025 — 31 Mar 2010 — Laser beam sensors and steering mirrors are req...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 28, 2025</span></span></span>
* Stabilised turrets that compensate for platform movement.
* Fire-control software that predicts target motion.
* Links to wider command-and-control networks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dia.mil/FOIA/FOIA-Electronic-Reading-Room/FileId/237622/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dia.mil">[dia.mil+2ndia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dia.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">State of the Art and Evolution of High-Energy Laser WeaponsApril 28, 2025 — 31 Mar 2010 — Laser beam sensors and steering mirrors are req...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 28, 2025</span></span></span>

The laser itself is therefore only one component within a larger targeting architecture. Raytheon, for example, describes high-energy laser systems as requiring the ability to detect threats, track them during manoeuvres and positively identify them before engagement. Those sensing and tracking functions are fundamental to weapon effectiveness rather than optional add-ons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/integrated-air-and-missile-defense/lasers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rtx.com">[RTX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rtx.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">High-Energy Lasers &#124; RaytheonThis directed energy technology enables detection of threats, tracking during maneuvers, and positive vis...</span></span></span>

## Tracking Drones Long Enough to Defeat Them

### The Dwell-Time Problem

Many threats targeted by laser weapons, especially drones, are not destroyed instantly. The beam often needs to remain on a vulnerable location for several seconds before structural damage, sensor failure or overheating occurs.

That requirement creates a demanding tracking challenge. A small quadcopter may change direction rapidly, present a limited target area and operate against a cluttered background. Wind, atmospheric distortion and platform vibration can further complicate the engagement. If the beam wanders, the heating effect may disperse and the target may survive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sentrycs.com/glossary/directed-energy-weapons-dew/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sentrycs.com">[Sentrycs+2ndia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sentrycs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Directed Energy Weapons (DEW)Directed Energy Weapons use electromagnetic energy to disable drones. As a result, significant [engineering]({{ 'engineering/' | relative_url }}) effort goes into keeping the beam centred on a moving target. Defence analyses of high-energy laser systems highlight the importance of beam sensors, steering mirrors, pointing telescopes and precision tracking loops that constantly adjust aim during an engagement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dia.mil/FOIA/FOIA-Electronic-Reading-Room/FileId/237622/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dia.mil">[dia.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dia.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">State of the Art and Evolution of High-Energy Laser WeaponsApril 28, 2025 — 31 Mar 2010 — Laser beam sensors and steering mirrors are req...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 28, 2025</span></span></span>

### Why Sensors Become a Cost Driver

The sensor package often has to operate across different conditions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* Day and night.
* Clear weather and haze.
* Multiple target types.
* Different ranges and speeds.
* Electronic warfare environments.

</div>

A military-grade electro-optical and infrared suite capable of performing these tasks is not inexpensive. It requires high-performance optics, image processing hardware, stabilisation systems and software capable of distinguishing threats from background clutter. The more demanding the mission, the more sophisticated the sensor architecture becomes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndia.org/-/media/ndia-eti/reports/directed-energy-weapon-supply-chains/directedenergyweaponsreportdeeti.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndia.org">[ndia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DIRECTED ENERGY WEAPON SUPPLY CHAINS1 Jan 2024 — Tracking: The tracking system, often integrated into the beam director, employs various...</span></span></span>

This is one reason why defeating drones is not simply a matter of increasing [laser power]({{ 'power-costs/' | relative_url }}). A more powerful beam is of limited value if the system cannot maintain precise target tracking long enough to exploit that power.

The practical lesson is that operational effectiveness emerges from sensor performance and tracking accuracy as much as from raw beam energy.

## Why Platform-Specific Integration Raises Costs

A laser weapon rarely operates independently. Modern militaries increasingly expect it to share information with radars, command networks, threat databases and other defensive systems.

That requirement creates expensive integration work. On a warship, for example, the laser may need to receive target tracks from shipboard radar, exchange information with combat-management software and operate within existing engagement procedures. HELIOS was developed specifically for integration with the Navy's Aegis combat system, allowing sensor data and engagement information to become part of a wider defensive network.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaldefensemagazine.org/articles/2021/2/15/navy-to-fully-integrate-laser-into-aegis-combat-system" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaldefensemagazine.org">[nationaldefensemagazine.org+2PR Newswire]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaldefensemagazine.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">navy to fully integrate laser into aegis combat system</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">15 Feb 2021 — HELIOS is a 60-kilowatt solid-state laser capable of scalable effects, which can “dazzle” and blind sensors, but at high po...</span></span></span>

This integration effort involves far more than installing hardware. Engineers must:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Connect sensors to existing combat systems.
* Validate software interfaces.
* Ensure cybersecurity compliance.
* Test performance under operational conditions.
* Certify safe operation alongside other sensors and weapons.

## The Hidden Economics of Sensor Fit

The economics of laser weapons are often presented as a comparison between a cheap laser shot and an expensive missile. That comparison is real, but it overlooks the systems needed to make the shot count.

A laser can only exploit its low marginal firing cost when it is embedded within a sophisticated network of sensors, tracking algorithms, beam-control hardware and combat-management software. Programmes such as HELIOS illustrate this reality by combining surveillance, target tracking, [counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) functions and weapon effects into a single integrated package. Development contracts and integration efforts have therefore focused as much on sensing and control as on the laser source itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lockheed-martin-receives-150-million-contract-to-deliver-integrated-high-energy-laser-weapon-systems-to-us-navy-300606676.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prnewswire.com">[PR Newswire]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prnewswire.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PR NewswireLockheed Martin Receives $150 Million Contract to Deliver...1 Mar 2018 — HELIOS will be the first Department of Defense contr...</span></span></span>
