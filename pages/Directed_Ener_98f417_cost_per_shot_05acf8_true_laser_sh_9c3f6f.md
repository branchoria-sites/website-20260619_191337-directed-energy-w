## Introduction

Laser weapons are often advertised with strikingly low firing costs: a shot may consume only a few pounds' worth of electricity, compared with missiles that can cost hundreds of thousands of pounds. That comparison is real, but it is incomplete. The headline figure describes the marginal cost of firing the beam, not the total cost of buying, integrating, powering, [cooling]({{ 'cooling/' | relative_url }}), maintaining and operating the weapon system over its life. A laser may be cheap to shoot, yet expensive to own. The key question for defence planners is therefore not whether a laser shot costs £10 or £100, but whether the entire system can defeat enough threats over time to justify its acquisition and support costs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.forcesnews.com/technology/weapons-and-kit/dragonfire-cutting-edge-laser-air-and-sea-weapon-achieves-mod-testing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: forcesnews.com">[Forces News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">forcesnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forces NewsDragonFire: £10-a-shot laser weapon achieves UK first19 Jan 2024 — Its operating cost is typically less than £10 per shot...</span></span></span>

## Marginal Firing Cost Versus Ownership Cost

The appeal of directed energy weapons rests on a genuine economic advantage. Once installed and powered, a laser can engage targets without consuming missiles, shells or other physical interceptors. The UK's DragonFire programme has repeatedly highlighted an operating cost typically below £10 per shot, while earlier US [Navy laser]({{ 'laser-lineage/' | relative_url }}) programmes promoted engagements measured in dollars rather than thousands of dollars.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.forcesnews.com/technology/weapons-and-kit/dragonfire-cutting-edge-laser-air-and-sea-weapon-achieves-mod-testing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: forcesnews.com">[Forces News+2GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">forcesnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forces NewsDragonFire: £10-a-shot laser weapon achieves UK first19 Jan 2024 — Its operating cost is typically less than £10 per shot...</span></span></span>

However, the cost-per-shot figure captures only one part of the balance sheet. A deployable military laser is not merely a beam generator. It is a complete weapon system that includes:

* High-energy laser modules.(#endnote-3 "Endnote 3")<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Energy_Laser_with_Integrated_Optical-dazzler_and_Surveillance" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical-dazzler and Surveillance</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical-dazzler and Surveillance</span></span></span>
* Precision beam directors and stabilised turrets.
* Electro-optical and infrared tracking sensors.
* Fire-control software.
* Power-[generation]({{ 'generation-ii/' | relative_url }}) and power-conditioning equipment.
* Cooling and thermal-management systems.
* [Maintenance]({{ 'maintenance/' | relative_url }}) infrastructure, spare parts and specialist personnel.

In many cases, these supporting elements represent a large share of the [programme cost]({{ 'programme-cost/' | relative_url }}). For example, the US Navy's HELIOS programme was awarded under a contract worth roughly $150 million for development and integration, reflecting the reality that turning a laboratory laser into a combat-capable naval weapon requires extensive engineering beyond the laser itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Energy_Laser_with_Integrated_Optical-dazzler_and_Surveillance" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical-dazzler and Surveillance</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical-dazzler and Surveillance</span></span></span>

This distinction is familiar in other military technologies. A fighter aircraft may burn only a few thousand pounds' worth of fuel during a sortie, yet nobody evaluates the aircraft solely by its fuel bill. Likewise, a laser's electricity consumption is only one operating input within a much larger ownership cost structure.

## Why Power and Cooling Become Major Expenses

### The Hidden Infrastructure Behind the Beam

A high-energy laser converts electrical power into concentrated light, but no laser is perfectly efficient. Significant energy is lost as heat. The more powerful the beam, the greater the challenge of removing that heat without degrading performance or damaging components.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defencesciencereview.com.pl/pdf-216776-135052?filename=Laser-Based-Directed-Ener.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: com.pl">[Defence Science Review]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">com.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cooling</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defence Science ReviewDEFENCE SCIENCE REVIEWJanuary 17, 2026 — by A Karkadakattil · 2026 · Cited by 1 — Laser DEWs provide a critical lay...</span></span></span>

The result is that a laser weapon often demands substantial supporting equipment. Naval [platforms]({{ 'platforms/' | relative_url }}) have advantages because ships possess larger electrical generation capacity and more space for thermal-management equipment. Even so, integration remains complex. HELIOS was designed specifically to work with the Navy's Aegis combat system rather than simply being bolted onto a deck as an independent device.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Energy_Laser_with_Integrated_Optical-dazzler_and_Surveillance" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical-dazzler and Surveillance</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical-dazzler and Surveillance</span></span></span>

For land vehicles, the challenge can be even more significant. Designers must find room and weight allowances for generators, batteries, cooling loops and control electronics while preserving mobility and protection.

### Integration Costs Are Real Weapon Costs

Laser discussions often separate the beam from the rest of the system, but military users cannot. A laser that cannot find, track and maintain aim on a moving drone is not a useful air-defence asset.

Modern directed energy weapons therefore require integration with:<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.lockheedmartin.com/en-us/capabilities/directed-energy.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lockheedmartin.com">[lockheedmartin.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lockheedmartin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Directed Energy &#124; Lockheed MartinExperience the potential of our laser weapons and directed energy weapon technologies, providing afforda...</span></span></span>


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* Radar systems.
* Electro-optical tracking sensors.
* Combat-management software.
* Target identification systems.
* Existing air-defence command networks.

</div>

HELIOS, for example, was developed as an integrated weapon and sensor package connected directly to shipboard combat systems. DragonFire similarly depends on sophisticated target tracking and beam-control technologies rather than raw [laser power]({{ 'power-costs/' | relative_url }}) alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Energy_Laser_with_Integrated_Optical-dazzler_and_Surveillance" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical-dazzler and Surveillance</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical-dazzler and Surveillance</span></span></span>

These integration efforts are expensive, time-consuming and often platform-specific. A laser installed on one class of ship may require substantial redesign before it can be fitted to another vessel or vehicle.

## Maintenance Can Erase Simple Cost Comparisons

The phrase "cheap shot" can unintentionally suggest a weapon that is cheap to operate overall. Yet advanced lasers contain highly sensitive optical components that must remain precisely aligned and protected from contamination.

Operational experience has shown that maintenance can become a major consideration. Reporting on US military directed energy programmes has highlighted cases where laser systems experienced cooling and battery issues during deployment testing and required specialist support. Government assessments have also noted that some repairs may require highly controlled environments rather than routine field servicing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/laser-wars-us-military-laser-weapons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arriving overseas in 2022 and actively engaging enemy drones, this marks a significant milestone in laser weapon technology for air defen...</span></span></span>

This does not mean lasers are impractical. It means that maintenance costs, spare components and technical expertise must be included when comparing lasers with missiles, guns or electronic-warfare systems.

A missile interceptor may be expensive to fire, but its storage, handling and maintenance processes are generally well understood after decades of operational use. Directed energy systems are still building that institutional experience.

## Why the Cost Exchange Can Still Favour Lasers

Despite these caveats, the low firing cost remains strategically important.

The crucial comparison is not between one laser shot and one missile shot. It is between the lifetime cost of the laser system and the number of threats it can engage over years of service.

Consider a ship facing repeated drone attacks. If the vessel must otherwise use expensive interceptors against every low-cost target, the cumulative cost of those missile engagements can become substantial. In that environment, a laser does not need to replace every defensive weapon. ### The Value of Magazine Depth

One of the strongest economic arguments for lasers is not the electricity bill but the ability to keep firing.

A missile launcher carries a finite number of interceptors. Once those interceptors are expended, reloading may be difficult or impossible during combat. ## What Does a Cheap Laser Shot Really Cost?

The answer depends on which cost is being measured.

A laser shot may genuinely consume only a few pounds' worth of electricity. That figure is useful because it highlights the potential economic advantage of defeating inexpensive drones without spending expensive interceptors. Yet the real military investment includes procurement contracts, platform modifications, power generation, cooling equipment, software integration, maintenance support and operator training.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.forcesnews.com/technology/weapons-and-kit/dragonfire-cutting-edge-laser-air-and-sea-weapon-achieves-mod-testing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: forcesnews.com">[Forces News+2GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">forcesnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forces NewsDragonFire: £10-a-shot laser weapon achieves UK first19 Jan 2024 — Its operating cost is typically less than £10 per shot...</span></span></span>

The most accurate way to view laser economics is therefore as a two-level calculation. The marginal cost of firing is exceptionally low, but the ownership cost is substantial. Whether the technology delivers value depends on how often it is used, what threats it replaces, and how effectively it reduces pressure on more expensive defensive systems.
