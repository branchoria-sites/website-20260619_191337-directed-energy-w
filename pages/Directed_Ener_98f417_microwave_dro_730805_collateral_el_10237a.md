## Introduction

High-power microwave (HPM) defences are attractive against drone [swarms]({{ 'swarms/' | relative_url }}) because a single burst of electromagnetic energy can affect multiple drones at once. The same characteristic, however, creates one of the technology’s most important governance and operational challenges: electronics other than the intended drones may also be exposed. Unlike a missile that strikes a specific object, an HPM system projects radio-frequency energy into a volume of space, creating the possibility of disruption to nearby radios, sensors, vehicles, communications equipment, and civilian electronics if the system is used without adequate controls. Military organisations therefore treat collateral electronic effects as a core design and [deployment]({{ 'deployment/' | relative_url }}) issue rather than a secondary concern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.onr.navy.mil/organization/departments/code-35/division-353/directed-energy-weapons-high-power-microwaves" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onr.navy.mil">[onr.navy.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onr.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Directed Energy Weapons: High Power Microwaves</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">March 19, 2022 — HPM weapons create beams of electromagnetic energy over a broad spectrum of radio and microwave frequencies causing dama...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 19, 2022</span></span></span>

## Why Susceptible Electronics May Be Affected

High-power microwave weapons work by injecting electromagnetic energy into electronic systems. Rather than relying on physical impact, they seek to couple energy into antennas, wiring, circuit traces, sensors, and semiconductor components. The intended result is disruption or damage to drone electronics, but the underlying physics does not inherently distinguish between hostile and friendly equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.onr.navy.mil/organization/departments/code-35/division-353/directed-energy-weapons-high-power-microwaves" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onr.navy.mil">[onr.navy.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onr.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Directed Energy Weapons: High Power Microwaves</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">March 19, 2022 — HPM weapons create beams of electromagnetic energy over a broad spectrum of radio and microwave frequencies causing dama...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 19, 2022</span></span></span>

Modern electronics are often interconnected through conductive pathways that can act as unintended receivers. Wires, antennas, cables, and exposed electronic interfaces may absorb electromagnetic energy and conduct it into sensitive components. This is one reason military electromagnetic-effects standards place heavy emphasis on shielding, grounding, filtering, and electromagnetic compatibility. The increasing density and complexity of modern electronics makes these concerns more significant rather than less.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.waru.edu/sites/default/files/Migrated/CopDocuments/MIL-STD-464D.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: waru.edu">[waru.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">waru.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MIL STD 464D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MIL-STD-464D1 Dec 2010 — High power microwave (HPM).... criticality of electronics demand that effective electromagnetic effects control...</span></span></span>

The actual effect depends on many variables:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Distance from the microwave source.
* Frequency and pulse characteristics.
* Antenna directionality and beam shaping.
* Shielding quality of the exposed equipment.
* Orientation of wiring and antennas.
* Electronic design margins and surge protection.

</div>

As a result, collateral effects exist on a spectrum. Equipment may experience temporary interference, corrupted data, degraded performance, forced resets, or, in more severe cases, permanent component damage. Not every exposed device will fail, but uncertainty itself becomes an operational concern. ## Why the Swarm Advantage Creates a Collateral-Risk Trade-Off

The principal advantage of microwave counter-swarm systems is their ability to engage many targets simultaneously. Wider-area electromagnetic effects allow a defender to avoid the one-target-at-a-time limitation that affects many kinetic systems and [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}). However, that same area effect means the engagement volume can include electronics other than the drones.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 23 106717</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons25 May 2023 — These weapons include high energy lasers and other high power electroma...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span>

This creates a fundamental trade-off. A highly focused beam may reduce unintended effects but also narrow the defended area. A broader beam may improve swarm-defeat performance while increasing the number of electronic systems potentially exposed. Defence planners therefore face a balancing problem: achieving sufficient electromagnetic coverage to defeat multiple drones without creating unacceptable risk to surrounding infrastructure and equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://d-fendsolutions.com/cuas-mitigation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: d-fendsolutions.com">[D-Fend Solutions]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">d-fendsolutions.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Key challenge when mitigation drones with EMP/HPM: Significant collateral damage to electronics in.Read more...</span></span></span>

The challenge becomes especially acute around installations that are themselves dense with electronics, such as airbases, ports, command centres, communications hubs, radar sites, and critical infrastructure facilities. These locations are often the very places most likely to require protection from drone attacks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.waru.edu/sites/default/files/Migrated/CopDocuments/MIL-STD-464D.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: waru.edu">[waru.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">waru.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MIL STD 464D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MIL-STD-464D1 Dec 2010 — High power microwave (HPM).... criticality of electronics demand that effective electromagnetic effects control...</span></span></span>

## Friendly-Force Exposure Concerns

Military forces increasingly depend on networked electronics. Tactical radios, satellite communications terminals, radar systems, electronic warfare equipment, navigation systems, vehicle electronics, and battlefield sensors all operate within the electromagnetic environment.

An HPM engagement conducted too close to friendly systems could potentially create several operational problems:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Temporary disruption of communications.
* Degradation of surveillance or sensor performance.
* Unexpected resets or faults in electronic equipment.
* Increased electromagnetic interference within command networks.
* Damage to inadequately protected legacy systems.

</div>

These risks are one reason military organisations invest heavily in electromagnetic hardening and electromagnetic compatibility testing. Standards governing military platforms explicitly recognise the need to control electromagnetic effects across entire force structures rather than evaluating individual systems in isolation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.waru.edu/sites/default/files/Migrated/CopDocuments/MIL-STD-464D.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: waru.edu">[waru.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">waru.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MIL STD 464D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MIL-STD-464D1 Dec 2010 — High power microwave (HPM).... criticality of electronics demand that effective electromagnetic effects control...</span></span></span>

The concern extends beyond fixed installations. Mobile formations may include vehicles from different generations, commercial off-the-shelf electronics, contractor-operated systems, and allied equipment with varying levels of protection. A microwave weapon effective against a [drone swarm]({{ 'swarm-limits/' | relative_url }}) may therefore interact differently with each nearby system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Meeting%20Proceedings/STO-MP-SET-315/MP-SET-315-24.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.sto.nato.int">[publications.sto.nato.int]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.sto.nato.int</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">more...</span></span></span>

## Civilian Infrastructure and Public Exposure Concerns

Civilian environments present an even more complicated governance challenge. Urban areas contain vast numbers of interconnected electronic devices, including telecommunications equipment, industrial control systems, transportation infrastructure, medical technology, consumer electronics, and commercial networks.

Researchers and analysts studying electromagnetic weapons have long noted that modern societies rely on highly interconnected electronic systems that can be vulnerable to electromagnetic disturbance. The more digitally dependent an environment becomes, the more potential pathways exist for unintended consequences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.inss.org.il/publication/electromagnetic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: inss.org.il">[INSS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">inss.org.il</span><span class="citation-popover-title">INSSElectromagnetic Bombs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">11 Sept 2025 — Electromagnetic weapons can disable command-and-control centers, including those located underground, by shutting down...</span></span></span>

## The Central Governance Question

The appeal of microwave drone defences comes from their ability to create one-to-many effects against large groups of unmanned aircraft. Yet the same mechanism that enables that efficiency also creates uncertainty about what else may be affected.

As a result, the central governance question is not whether microwave weapons can disrupt electronics—they are designed to do exactly that. The question is how precisely those effects can be confined to hostile drones while avoiding unacceptable consequences for friendly forces, civilian infrastructure, and nearby electronic systems. The future practicality of high-power microwave counter-swarm defence will depend as much on electromagnetic discipline, system hardening, and operational controls as on the weapon’s ability to bring drones down.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.onr.navy.mil/organization/departments/code-35/division-353/directed-energy-weapons-high-power-microwaves" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onr.navy.mil">[onr.navy.mil+2GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onr.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Directed Energy Weapons: High Power Microwaves</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">March 19, 2022 — HPM weapons create beams of electromagnetic energy over a broad spectrum of radio and microwave frequencies causing dama...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 19, 2022</span></span></span>
