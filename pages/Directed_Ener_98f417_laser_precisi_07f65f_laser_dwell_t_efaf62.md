## Introduction

High-energy laser weapons strike at the speed of light, but destroying a drone is usually not instantaneous. The critical constraint is **dwell time**: the length of time the laser must keep enough energy concentrated on the same vulnerable point for heat to accumulate and cause failure. A laser can reach a target immediately, yet the target may survive if the beam wanders, atmospheric effects spread the energy, or the heating period is interrupted before critical components fail. This requirement to maintain a stable aim point is one of the central reasons why laser air-defence systems excel against some drones while struggling against others, particularly when many targets arrive at once. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dia.mil/FOIA/FOIA-Electronic-Reading-Room/FileId/237622/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dia.mil">[Defense Intelligence Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dia.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Intelligence AgencyState of the Art and Evolution of High-Energy Laser WeaponsApril 28, 2025 — 31 Mar 2010 — The purpose of an HE...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 28, 2025</span></span></span>

## What dwell time means in a laser engagement

A high-energy laser damages most drone targets through heating. Rather than delivering a single burst of destructive energy like a missile warhead, the weapon continuously deposits energy into a small area until a threshold is reached. The US Defense Intelligence Agency described the purpose of a high-energy laser weapon as placing and maintaining a focused beam on a target aim point for sufficient time to create the desired heating effect. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dia.mil/FOIA/FOIA-Electronic-Reading-Room/FileId/237622/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dia.mil">[Defense Intelligence Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dia.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Intelligence AgencyState of the Art and Evolution of High-Energy Laser WeaponsApril 28, 2025 — 31 Mar 2010 — The purpose of an HE...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 28, 2025</span></span></span>

The required dwell time depends on several factors:

* Laser power output. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://inss.ndu.edu/news/Article/4412653/laser-focusing-defense-capabilities/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: inss.ndu.edu">[inss.ndu.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">inss.ndu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ndu.eduLaser-focusing Defense CapabilitiesHigh-power lasers can defeat UAS by rapidly heating and structurally compromising control surfa...</span></span></span>
* Distance to the target.
* Atmospheric conditions such as haze, dust, or humidity.
* The material being illuminated.
* The size of the laser spot on the target.
* Whether the beam remains on a single vulnerable location. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2Security and Defence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy Weapons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science &amp; Tech Spotlight: Directed Energy WeaponsMay 25, 2023 — Directed energy weapons—such as lasers—use energy fired at the speed o...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 25, 2023</span></span></span>

A useful way to think about the problem is with a blowtorch. Touching metal briefly may warm it, but not damage it. Holding the flame on one point long enough eventually melts or weakens the material. Laser weapons operate on the same basic thermal principle, although with far greater precision and power density.

This explains why tracking performance is often as important as raw [laser power]({{ 'power-costs/' | relative_url }}). A beam that slips across a target spreads its energy over a larger area, reducing the temperature rise at any one location. Even a powerful laser may fail to achieve a kill if it cannot maintain stable illumination. Studies of [counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) laser systems repeatedly identify accurate tracking and sustained focus on a fixed point as key requirements for successful engagement. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://securityanddefence.pl/Testing-the-efficiency-of-laser-technology-to-destroy-the-rogue-drones%2C127360%2C0%2C2.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: securityanddefence.pl">[Security and Defence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">securityanddefence.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">High energy and high accuracy tracking mechanism are needed.Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why drones are easier targets than hardened threats

Dwell time matters for all laser engagements, but not all targets require the same amount of heating.

Small drones are among the most attractive laser targets because they are typically built from lightweight plastics, composites, thin metals, exposed wiring, compact electric motors, batteries, and fragile sensors. These components can be damaged after relatively short periods of concentrated heating. Research examining counter-directed-energy measures notes that small unmanned aircraft are often susceptible to destruction with shorter laser dwell times because of their lightweight construction. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nps.edu/documents/10180/142489929/JDE_7-2_Johnson.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.edu">[Naval Postgraduate School]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval Postgraduate SchoolCounter Directed Energy Weapons and the Defense of...January 10, 2023 — by B Johnson · 2023 · Cited by 2 — Thei...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2023</span></span></span>

By contrast, hardened military targets present a much more demanding thermal problem. Thicker materials absorb more energy before failing. Protective coatings can reduce heating [efficiency]({{ 'efficiency/' | relative_url }}). Fast-moving missiles may expose any given point to the beam for only a short period. As a result, achieving a kill often requires either higher power, longer dwell time, better tracking, or some combination of all three. This is one reason military planners frequently view current high-energy [lasers]({{ 'lasers/' | relative_url }}) as especially valuable for counter-drone missions rather than as universal replacements for all air-defence weapons. ## Why holding the beam steady is harder than it sounds

The dwell-time requirement turns a seemingly simple shot into a demanding tracking challenge.

A drone in flight is constantly moving, vibrating, banking, and changing orientation. The laser director must compensate for these movements while also correcting for platform motion, atmospheric turbulence, and optical distortions. The target's vulnerable components may occupy only a small fraction of the visible airframe. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://securityanddefence.pl/Testing-the-efficiency-of-laser-technology-to-destroy-the-rogue-drones%2C127360%2C0%2C2.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: securityanddefence.pl">[Security and Defence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">securityanddefence.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">High energy and high accuracy tracking mechanism are needed.Read more...</span></span></span>

Several effects can lengthen the required dwell time:

**Target manoeuvres.** Sudden turns or changes in speed force the beam-control system to continuously update its aim point.

**Atmospheric distortion.** Turbulence and thermal effects can spread or distort the beam, reducing energy density on the target.

**Rotating structures.** Propellers and spinning components may briefly expose and then hide vulnerable areas.

**Jitter and vibration.** Small [tracking errors]({{ 'tracking-errors/' | relative_url }}) can move the beam away from the optimum aim point, reducing heating effectiveness. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://securityanddefence.pl/Testing-the-efficiency-of-laser-technology-to-destroy-the-rogue-drones%2C127360%2C0%2C2.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: securityanddefence.pl">[Security and Defence+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">securityanddefence.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">High energy and high accuracy tracking mechanism are needed.Read more...</span></span></span>

## How saturation attacks stress one-beam-at-a-time weapons

Dwell time creates a second limitation that becomes apparent during drone [swarms]({{ 'swarms/' | relative_url }}) and mass attacks.

A conventional missile interceptor destroys a target and immediately becomes available for another engagement if additional rounds are ready. A laser, however, generally must devote its beam to a target for the entire dwell period required to achieve defeat. During those seconds, the beam is largely unavailable for other threats. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eos-aus.com/news/magic-bullet-sci-fi-laser-weapons-are-now-an-anti-drone-reality/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eos-aus.com">[EOS AUS+2Air Force Research Laboratory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eos-aus.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">EOS AUSMagic bullet?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sci-Fi laser weapons are now an anti-drone...10 Mar 2026 — Keller points to &quot;dwell time&quot; as a primary constraint of laser weapons. ## The precision trade-off at the heart of laser defence

Dwell time illustrates the central precision trade-off of high-energy laser weapons. The same focused beam that allows remarkably accurate engagements also requires sustained precision to create meaningful damage. A laser can place energy exactly where defenders want it, but only if it remains locked onto a vulnerable spot long enough for heat to matter.

Against small drones, that requirement is often manageable because the targets are relatively fragile and can fail after short periods of concentrated heating. Against tougher targets or large drone raids, however, the need to maintain dwell time becomes a limiting factor that shapes both weapon design and [battlefield]({{ 'battle-limits/' | relative_url }}) tactics.
