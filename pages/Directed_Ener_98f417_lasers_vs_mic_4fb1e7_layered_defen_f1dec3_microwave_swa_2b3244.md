## Introduction

High-power microwave (HPM) weapons are attractive for [drone swarm]({{ 'swarm-limits/' | relative_url }}) defence because they attack a problem that lasers and missiles struggle with: many targets arriving at once. Instead of focusing on one drone at a time, a microwave system can project electromagnetic energy across a wider area and potentially disrupt multiple drones in a single engagement. That makes [microwaves]({{ 'microwaves/' | relative_url }}) one of the most promising directed-energy responses to saturation attacks by large groups of small unmanned aircraft. However, promising is not the same as guaranteed. Real-world performance depends on how energy couples into a target's electronics, how the drones are built, and whether all members of a swarm are equally vulnerable. As a result, military organisations increasingly treat microwaves as a valuable layer within a broader defensive network rather than a stand-alone solution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/integrated-air-and-missile-defense/phaser-high-power-microwave" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rtx.com">[RTX+2Army]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rtx.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phaser High-Power Microwave System &#124; RaytheonDefending at the speed of light. ## Can Microwaves Really Stop a Drone Swarm?

The short answer is yes—sometimes dramatically so—but not with perfect certainty.

Unlike a laser, which generally must dwell on an individual target long enough to damage it, a high-power microwave system attempts to flood an area with electromagnetic energy. If that energy reaches vulnerable electronic components, it can disrupt processors, sensors, power-management systems or communications equipment. The appeal is obvious: a single pulse may affect multiple drones within the beam rather than requiring sequential engagements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/integrated-air-and-missile-defense/phaser-high-power-microwave" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rtx.com">[RTX+2Military Embedded Systems]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rtx.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phaser High-Power Microwave System &#124; RaytheonDefending at the speed of light. Recent demonstrations have reinforced this attraction. Raytheon has repeatedly presented its Phaser system as a means of engaging both individual drones and [swarms]({{ 'swarms/' | relative_url }}) with a wide microwave beam. Earlier Army exercises reported multiple-drone defeats during swarm scenarios.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/integrated-air-and-missile-defense/phaser-high-power-microwave" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rtx.com">[RTX+2Raytheon News Release Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rtx.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phaser High-Power Microwave System &#124; RaytheonDefending at the speed of light. ## Why Microwaves Appeal Against Groups of Drones

The key advantage is not merely speed. It is area effect.

A laser concentrates energy onto one spot. A microwave weapon spreads electromagnetic energy across a volume of space. If multiple drones occupy that volume, several may experience disruptive effects during the same engagement. This characteristic directly addresses the saturation challenge posed by swarms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/integrated-air-and-missile-defense/phaser-high-power-microwave" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rtx.com">[RTX+2Military Embedded Systems]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rtx.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phaser High-Power Microwave System &#124; RaytheonDefending at the speed of light. ## Why Effects Can Vary Across Different Targets

The challenge is that microwave weapons do not interact with every drone in the same way.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://dronelife.com/2025/10/15/microwave-counter-drone-system/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dronelife.com">[dronelife.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dronelife.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">microwave counter drone system</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">15 Oct 2025 — Epirus and General Dynamics unveil Leonidas AR, a mobile microwave counter drone system designed to defeat drone swarms for...</span></span></span>

Their effectiveness depends on whether electromagnetic energy can reach sensitive electronics at damaging levels. Defence specialists often describe this as a coupling problem: the energy must find a path into the systems that keep the drone functioning. Not all drones are equally exposed. Some designs may inadvertently provide better protection through metallic structures, component placement or electromagnetic hardening. ### Partial Rather Than Total Effects

Microwave exposure does not always produce a dramatic crash. Effects can range from temporary disruption and sensor malfunction to complete electronic failure. ### Autonomous Behaviour Complicates Defence

Many [counter-drone]({{ 'missile-savings/' | relative_url }}) approaches rely on disrupting communications links. Increasing autonomy reduces dependence on external control signals. Microwaves remain relevant because they target onboard electronics rather than merely radio links, but autonomous drones still create a challenge: if some members of a swarm survive exposure, they may continue their mission without external guidance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08477" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv+2Security and Defence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

### Geometry and Exposure Matter

Microwave effectiveness is influenced by range, beam shape, orientation and the time a target remains within the effective engagement zone. Simulation research and technical analyses indicate that kill probabilities can vary significantly as these factors change, underscoring why performance should be viewed probabilistically rather than as an automatic outcome.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08477" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

The result is an important operational reality: a microwave system may disable many drones in a swarm while leaving some unaffected or only partially affected.

If a microwave pulse reduces a swarm from fifty drones to ten, the burden on subsequent defensive layers changes dramatically. ## The Real Role of Microwaves in Swarm Defence

The most realistic view is that microwaves are a force multiplier rather than a magic shield.

They offer one of the few mechanisms capable of affecting multiple drones nearly simultaneously, making them uniquely valuable against swarm attacks. Demonstrations by systems such as Phaser, [THOR]({{ 'thor/' | relative_url }}) and Leonidas suggest that large-scale disruption is achievable under favourable conditions. [RTX+2The War Zone](https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/integrated-air-and-missile-defense/phaser-high-power-microwave)

Yet the same body of evidence also points to why military planners continue building layered defences. Drone construction varies. Electromagnetic vulnerability varies. Exposure conditions vary. Some drones may be disabled instantly, while others may survive long enough to require another form of engagement. For that reason, microwaves fit best as the first major reduction layer in a broader counter-swarm architecture, improving the odds for every defensive system that follows rather than replacing them.
